## What is Cancer?

Cancer is a broad term for a large group of diseases that can start in almost any organ or tissue of the body, occurring when abnormal cells grow and divide uncontrollably and can spread into surrounding tissue or to other parts of the body. There are more than a hundred recognised types of cancer, and each type can behave very differently in terms of how it develops, how it is diagnosed, and how it is treated. Because of this variation, this page offers only a general overview and is not a substitute for evaluation by a qualified doctor or oncologist, who can assess specific symptoms and recommend appropriate tests. Cancer requires prompt medical diagnosis and treatment, and outcomes are generally better the earlier it is detected and managed by a qualified oncology team.

## Symptoms

Because cancer covers so many different diseases, symptoms vary enormously depending on the type and the organs involved, and many of these symptoms can also be caused by conditions that are not cancer. Widely recognised general warning signs include:

- An unusual lump or swelling anywhere in the body
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent fatigue that does not improve with rest
- Unexplained pain that does not go away
- Unusual bleeding or discharge, including blood in urine or stool
- A sore or wound that does not heal
- A persistent cough, hoarseness, or breathlessness
- Changes in bowel or bladder habits that persist
- Difficulty swallowing or persistent indigestion
- Changes in the size, shape, or colour of a mole or skin lesion
- Unexplained fever or night sweats

Having one or more of these symptoms does not necessarily mean a person has cancer, as many of these can be caused by other, less serious conditions. However, persistent or unexplained symptoms like these should always be evaluated by a doctor so that the cause can be properly investigated.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cancer develops when changes occur in the genetic material of cells that cause them to grow and divide abnormally. These changes can accumulate over time due to a combination of factors, and in most cases, no single clear cause can be identified. Well-established risk factors recognised by major health bodies include:

- Tobacco use, including smoking and chewing tobacco
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Excess body weight and obesity
- Prolonged, unprotected exposure to ultraviolet (UV) radiation from sunlight or tanning devices
- Certain chronic infections, such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C, and human papillomavirus (HPV)
- Exposure to certain chemicals and environmental pollutants, including some workplace exposures
- Ionising radiation exposure
- A family history of certain cancers and some inherited genetic conditions
- Increasing age, as risk rises for many cancer types over a person's lifetime
- Chronic inflammation and certain pre-existing medical conditions

Having one or more risk factors does not mean a person will definitely develop cancer, and some people with no identifiable risk factors still develop the disease. Many risk factors, such as tobacco use and excessive alcohol consumption, can be modified, while others, such as age and family history, cannot.

## How Homeopathy Approaches Cancer

Homeopathy does not treat, cure, shrink, or otherwise affect cancer or tumours, and it must never be considered a substitute for oncology care. Cancer is a serious, potentially life-threatening condition that requires diagnosis and treatment from qualified medical specialists, typically an oncology team using evidence-based treatments such as surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted therapy, or immunotherapy as appropriate.

Some patients going through the experience of a cancer diagnosis and its treatment choose to explore homeopathy purely as a general wellbeing or complementary measure, for example to support their overall sense of calm or to feel they have some additional support while coping with a difficult time, always alongside, and never instead of, their prescribed oncology treatment. It is important to understand that scientific evidence for homeopathy's effectiveness in any health condition is limited, and bodies such as the NHS state there is no robust evidence that homeopathy is effective for treating any disease, including cancer. Anyone considering this should discuss it openly with their treating oncologist first, and should never delay, reduce, or stop prescribed cancer treatment in favour of homeopathy or any other alternative therapy.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional cancer treatment is determined by an oncology team based on the type, location, and stage of cancer, and may include surgery, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, targeted drug therapy, immunotherapy, hormone therapy, or a combination of these, often alongside supportive care to manage symptoms and side effects. Early diagnosis and prompt treatment, guided by an oncologist, are critical, as delays can allow cancer to progress and can significantly worsen outcomes.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy only as a complementary, general wellbeing approach alongside their full oncology treatment plan, never as a replacement for it. Delaying, reducing, or replacing conventional cancer treatment with homeopathy or any other alternative therapy is dangerous and can be life-threatening. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled oncology appointments, scans, and follow-up visits, even when feeling well
- Take all prescribed treatments exactly as directed, without stopping or skipping doses
- Eat a balanced diet and stay adequately hydrated, adjusting as advised by your care team during treatment
- Rest when needed, but stay as physically active as your doctor advises, as tolerated
- Avoid tobacco and limit alcohol, as both can affect treatment and recovery
- Lean on family, friends, or support groups, and consider speaking to a counsellor if coping with the emotional impact of diagnosis and treatment feels overwhelming
- Discuss any complementary approach, including homeopathy, openly with your oncology team before starting it

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Prompt medical evaluation is essential for anyone, whether newly concerned about possible symptoms or already undergoing cancer treatment, who notices any of the following. These warning signs should never be ignored, and a doctor should be consulted without delay:

- An unexplained lump or swelling that persists or grows
- Unexplained, unintentional weight loss
- Unusual bleeding or discharge from any part of the body
- A sore, ulcer, or wound that does not heal within the expected time
- A persistent cough, hoarseness, or coughing up blood
- Persistent changes in bowel or bladder habits
- Difficulty swallowing, persistent indigestion, or unexplained abdominal pain
- A mole or skin lesion that changes in size, shape, colour, or begins to bleed
- Unexplained, persistent fatigue, fever, or night sweats
- For those already diagnosed with cancer: high fever, severe pain, breathlessness, or any sudden worsening of symptoms during treatment, which may indicate a medical emergency

If you or someone you know experiences any of these signs, see a doctor or oncologist without delay. Early diagnosis and prompt, appropriate treatment make a significant difference to outcomes in cancer care.
