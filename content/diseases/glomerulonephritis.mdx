## What is Glomerulonephritis?

Glomerulonephritis is inflammation of the glomeruli, the tiny clusters of blood vessels inside the kidneys that filter waste and excess fluid from the blood to form urine. When these filtering units become inflamed or damaged, the kidneys may not filter blood properly, which can lead to blood or protein leaking into the urine, fluid retention, and changes in blood pressure. Glomerulonephritis can occur suddenly (acute) or develop gradually over months or years (chronic), and it can result from a range of causes, including infections, immune system conditions, or other kidney diseases.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of glomerulonephritis can vary and may include:

- Dark, cola-coloured, or tea-coloured urine, due to blood in the urine
- Foamy or frothy urine, which can indicate excess protein
- Swelling of the face, eyes, hands, legs, or abdomen
- High blood pressure
- Reduced urine output
- Fatigue and feeling generally unwell
- In some cases, no symptoms at all, with the condition found incidentally on a routine urine test

## Causes & Risk Factors

Glomerulonephritis can be caused by a number of different conditions, and in some cases, no clear cause is found. Recognised causes and associated conditions include:

- Infections, such as a throat or skin infection with certain bacteria, or viral infections including hepatitis B, hepatitis C, or HIV
- Autoimmune conditions, such as lupus or IgA nephropathy
- Vasculitis, conditions causing inflammation of blood vessels
- Diabetes, which can affect kidney filtering units over time
- Certain genetic conditions affecting the kidneys
- Use of some medicines, in rare cases

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for glomerulonephritis depends on the underlying cause and severity, and may include medicines to control blood pressure, reduce protein loss, or calm an overactive immune response, along with treating any underlying infection or condition. Dietary adjustments, such as controlling salt and protein intake, may also be recommended. Regular blood and urine tests are used to monitor kidney function over time, and in more advanced cases, specialist kidney care may be needed.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medical care. Homeopathy is not positioned as a replacement for prescribed treatment or monitoring by a kidney specialist. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled blood pressure, blood, and urine tests to monitor kidney function
- Follow any advice on reducing salt intake to help manage swelling and blood pressure
- Take prescribed medicines as directed, even if you feel well
- Monitor and record your blood pressure at home if advised by your doctor
- Stay well hydrated unless your doctor has advised fluid restriction
- Report any new swelling, changes in urine, or unusual tiredness to your doctor promptly

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention urgently if you notice:

- Significant or rapidly increasing swelling of the face, legs, or abdomen
- A marked reduction in how much urine you are passing, or no urine output
- Very high blood pressure readings, or symptoms such as severe headache or visual disturbance linked to high blood pressure
- Shortness of breath, which can indicate fluid build-up affecting the lungs
- Confusion, severe fatigue, or feeling acutely unwell

These signs can indicate declining kidney function or kidney failure and need urgent medical review without delay.
