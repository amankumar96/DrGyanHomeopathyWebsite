## What is Gynecomastia?

Gynecomastia is the enlargement of glandular breast tissue in males, caused by a relative increase in the hormone oestrogen compared with testosterone. It is a common and generally benign (non-cancerous) condition that can affect one or both sides of the chest. It can occur in newborns due to maternal hormones, in boys during puberty, and in older men as hormone levels shift with age. While usually not a sign of a serious underlying problem, any new breast changes in a male warrant medical evaluation to confirm the diagnosis and rule out other causes.

## Symptoms

Typical features of gynecomastia include:

- Swelling or enlargement of breast tissue, which may affect one or both sides
- A firm or rubbery feel to the tissue directly beneath the nipple, distinct from surrounding fat
- Tenderness or mild discomfort in the breast area, in some cases
- Symmetrical or asymmetrical enlargement
- Typically no skin changes, dimpling, or nipple discharge — if these are present, prompt medical review is needed

## Causes & Risk Factors

Gynecomastia develops when the balance between oestrogen and testosterone shifts, allowing breast tissue to grow. Common causes and risk factors include:

- Normal hormonal changes during puberty, which often resolve within a year or two
- Natural age-related hormone changes in older men
- Certain medications, including some used for prostate conditions, heart conditions, ulcers, or anxiety
- Health conditions affecting the liver, kidneys, thyroid, or testes
- Excess body weight, which can increase oestrogen production
- Use of anabolic steroids or certain recreational drugs
- Rarely, tumours affecting hormone-producing glands

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of gynecomastia starts with identifying and addressing any underlying cause, such as adjusting a contributing medication, managing weight, or treating a hormonal condition. In adolescents, watchful waiting is often appropriate since many cases resolve naturally. In adults with persistent, bothersome, or large enlargement, a doctor may discuss medication or, occasionally, surgical options.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain a healthy body weight, as excess weight can contribute to hormone imbalance
- Review any medications with your doctor if you suspect they may be contributing, rather than stopping them yourself
- Avoid anabolic steroids and recreational drugs linked to hormonal disruption
- Limit alcohol intake, as heavy use can affect liver function and hormone levels
- Speak openly with your doctor, as gynecomastia can cause understandable embarrassment but is a common and manageable condition
- Seek emotional support if self-consciousness about appearance is affecting well-being

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While gynecomastia is usually benign, prompt medical evaluation is needed if you notice:

- A new lump in the breast tissue, especially if it feels hard, irregular, or is not centred beneath the nipple
- Nipple discharge, particularly if bloody
- Skin changes over the breast, such as dimpling, puckering, redness, or ulceration
- Enlargement that is only on one side and developing or changing rapidly
- Pain that is severe or worsening

These features do not necessarily mean anything serious, but they should always be assessed by a doctor promptly to confirm the cause and provide appropriate reassurance or further investigation.
