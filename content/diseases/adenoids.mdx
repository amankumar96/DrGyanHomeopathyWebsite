## What is Adenoids?

Adenoids are a small area of soft tissue located high in the throat, behind the nose, that form part of a young child's immune defences, working alongside the tonsils to help trap germs breathed in through the nose and mouth. Adenoids are naturally present in all children and tend to grow during early childhood before gradually shrinking, usually from around age four onward, and have often disappeared almost completely by adulthood. Problems arise when adenoids become enlarged or repeatedly infected, which can block the back of the nose or contribute to ear problems.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms linked to enlarged or inflamed adenoids include:

- Noisy breathing, mouth breathing, or a persistently blocked or runny nose
- Snoring, and occasionally pauses in breathing during sleep (sleep apnoea)
- A dry mouth or chapped lips from habitual mouth breathing
- Restless or disturbed sleep
- A sore throat or swollen neck glands
- A feeling of blocked ears, or reduced hearing, sometimes linked to glue ear
- Frequent ear or throat infections

## Causes & Risk Factors

Enlarged or inflamed adenoids are most often caused by common bacterial or viral infections that children are repeatedly exposed to during early life. Some children are also naturally born with larger adenoids. Because adenoids are most active and relatively large during the toddler and early school years, problems linked to them are most common in young children and become less common as children grow older and their adenoids shrink.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of adenoid problems depends on severity. Mild cases may simply be monitored over time, sometimes with nasal sprays or medicines to reduce swelling. When enlarged adenoids cause significant blockage, sleep apnoea, or recurrent ear and throat infections, doctors may recommend adenoidectomy, a common day-case surgery usually performed along with tonsil removal if needed.

Some parents use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their child's ENT or paediatric care, often alongside monitoring rather than in place of recommended surgery. ## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep your child's nasal passages clear with saline nasal drops or sprays as advised by your doctor
- Encourage good hydration, which can help thin nasal secretions
- Maintain good hand hygiene and general hygiene practices to reduce the frequency of colds
- Avoid exposure to cigarette smoke and other airway irritants at home
- Elevate your child's head slightly while sleeping if nasal blockage disturbs sleep, as advised by your doctor
- Attend follow-up appointments so your doctor can monitor adenoid size and symptoms over time

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most adenoid problems are not emergencies, but you should seek prompt medical attention if your child has:

- Pauses in breathing during sleep, choking, or gasping (possible signs of sleep apnoea)
- Severe difficulty breathing, or breathing that looks laboured even while awake
- High fever with a severely sore throat, drooling, or difficulty swallowing
- Significant hearing loss or ear pain that does not improve
- Failure to gain weight or grow normally, which may be linked to severe sleep-disordered breathing

If your child shows signs of significant breathing difficulty or choking episodes during sleep, contact your doctor promptly or seek emergency care without delay.
