## What is Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)?

Irritable bowel syndrome is a common, long-term disorder affecting the digestive system, causing symptoms such as abdominal pain, bloating, and changes in bowel habit without any visible damage or inflammation to the bowel. It is sometimes described as a disorder of gut-brain interaction, meaning the way the gut and nervous system communicate seems to play a central role. IBS tends to follow a pattern of flares and calmer periods, and while it cannot currently be cured, most people find their symptoms can be managed reasonably well with diet, lifestyle adjustments, and, when needed, medical treatment.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of IBS include:

- Recurring abdominal pain or cramping, often relieved somewhat by passing stool
- Bloating and a visibly swollen abdomen
- Diarrhoea, constipation, or an alternating pattern of both
- Passing mucus with stool
- A sensation of incomplete bowel emptying
- Symptoms that tend to flare with certain foods, stress, or hormonal changes

Symptoms vary from person to person and can range from mild and occasional to persistent and significantly disruptive to daily life.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of IBS is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve a combination of factors affecting gut movement, sensitivity, and the gut-brain connection. Known contributing factors and triggers include:

- A previous episode of severe gastroenteritis (sometimes called post-infectious IBS)
- Food intolerances or sensitivities
- Stress, anxiety, or depression
- Hormonal changes, with symptoms often fluctuating around the menstrual cycle in women
- A family history of IBS
- Disruption to the normal balance of gut bacteria

IBS is more commonly diagnosed in younger adults and is somewhat more common in women than men.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of IBS usually starts with dietary and lifestyle adjustments, such as identifying and avoiding trigger foods, managing fibre intake appropriately, regular exercise, and stress reduction techniques. Depending on the predominant symptoms, doctors may also prescribe medicines for pain, bloating, diarrhoea, or constipation, and in some cases recommend talking therapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, which can help with the gut-brain aspect of the condition.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep a food and symptom diary to help identify personal triggers
- Eat regular, smaller meals rather than large or irregular ones
- Stay well hydrated and limit caffeine, alcohol, and carbonated drinks if they worsen symptoms
- Adjust fibre intake gradually and under guidance, as both too little and too much can affect symptoms
- Engage in regular physical activity, which can help support healthy bowel movement
- Practise stress-reduction techniques such as relaxation exercises, gentle yoga, or mindfulness
- Try to maintain a consistent sleep routine, as poor sleep can worsen symptoms

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor without delay, rather than assuming symptoms are due to IBS, if you notice:

- Rectal bleeding or blood in the stool
- Unexplained weight loss
- New bowel symptoms starting for the first time after the age of 50
- Severe, persistent abdominal pain that is different from your usual pattern
- Anaemia, persistent vomiting, or difficulty swallowing
- A family history of bowel cancer or inflammatory bowel disease alongside new symptoms

These red-flag symptoms need proper medical investigation to rule out other causes rather than being assumed to be IBS.
