## What is Conjunctivitis?

Conjunctivitis, often called pink eye, is inflammation of the conjunctiva, the thin, clear tissue that covers the white part of the eye and lines the inside of the eyelids. It causes the eye to appear red or pink and can be accompanied by irritation, discharge, or watering. Conjunctivitis is usually caused by a viral or bacterial infection, or by an allergic reaction, and most cases are mild and resolve without lasting harm to the eye, though it is worth having persistent or severe cases checked.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of conjunctivitis include:

- Redness in the white of one or both eyes
- Watery or thick, sticky discharge from the eye
- Itching or a burning, gritty sensation in the eye
- Eyelids that may stick together, especially after sleep, due to discharge
- Mild sensitivity to light
- Swelling of the eyelids in some cases
- Symptoms often starting in one eye and sometimes spreading to the other

## Causes & Risk Factors

Conjunctivitis has several possible causes, including:

- Viral infections, often linked to the common cold and highly contagious
- Bacterial infections, which may cause thicker, yellow or green discharge
- Allergic reactions to pollen, dust, pet dander, or other allergens, often with itching as a prominent feature
- Irritants such as smoke, chlorine in swimming pools, or certain chemicals
- Contact lens wear, particularly with poor hygiene or extended wear
- Close contact with an infected person or contaminated surfaces, especially in schools and childcare settings

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for conjunctivitis depends on the cause. Viral conjunctivitis often resolves on its own with supportive care, bacterial conjunctivitis may be treated with antibiotic eye drops or ointment if needed, and allergic conjunctivitis is usually managed with antihistamines and avoiding the triggering allergen where possible. Good hygiene measures are important to reduce spread, particularly for infectious causes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wash your hands frequently and avoid touching or rubbing the eyes
- Use a clean, damp cloth to gently wipe away discharge, using a fresh area of the cloth for each eye
- Avoid sharing towels, pillows, or washcloths while symptoms are present
- Remove and avoid wearing contact lenses until the eye has fully healed
- Apply a cool compress to soothe irritation and swelling
- Avoid known allergens if allergic conjunctivitis is suspected
- Avoid wearing eye make-up until the infection has cleared, and discard any make-up used during the infection

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Significant eye pain, rather than mild irritation
- Sensitivity to light that is severe, or noticeable blurring of vision
- Intense redness with swelling of the eyelid or surrounding skin
- Symptoms in a newborn baby
- No improvement after a week to ten days, or symptoms that are getting worse
- A history of recent eye injury or recent eye surgery

These signs can indicate a more serious eye problem that needs prompt specialist assessment to protect vision.
