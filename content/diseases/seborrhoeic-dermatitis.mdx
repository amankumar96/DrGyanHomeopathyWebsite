## What is Seborrhoeic Dermatitis?

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is a common, long-term skin condition that causes flaky, itchy, and sometimes red or oily patches, most often on areas of skin with many oil-producing glands, such as the scalp, face, and chest. It can affect babies, where it is sometimes called cradle cap, as well as adults. The condition tends to follow a pattern of flares and remissions, and while it is not usually serious, it can be persistent and uncomfortable. It is not currently curable, but it is generally well managed with regular treatment.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of seborrhoeic dermatitis include:

- Flaky, white or yellowish scales on the scalp, eyebrows, sides of the nose, or ears
- Red or inflamed patches of skin, which may look darker, lighter, or purplish depending on skin tone
- Itching or mild soreness in affected areas
- Oily or greasy-looking skin in some areas
- In babies, thick, yellow, crusty patches on the scalp (cradle cap)

Symptoms can range from mild flaking that resembles dandruff to more widespread, inflamed patches.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of seborrhoeic dermatitis is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve an overreaction to a yeast called Malassezia that lives naturally on the skin, combined with overactive oil glands and individual immune responses. Known risk factors and triggers include:

- Cold, dry weather
- Stress and fatigue
- Hormonal changes
- Certain neurological or immune conditions, which can be associated with more persistent cases
- Oily skin type
- Family history of the condition

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is not caused by poor hygiene, though gentle regular skin and scalp care can help manage it.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wash affected areas regularly with a gentle, suitable cleanser or medicated shampoo as advised
- Avoid scratching or picking at flaky skin, which can worsen irritation
- Manage stress where possible, as it can be a trigger for some people
- Keep skin moisturised, but avoid heavy, greasy products on oily areas
- Dress in breathable fabrics and avoid excessive sweating where possible
- Be patient with treatment, as flare-ups and remissions are part of the normal pattern of this condition

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most flare-ups of seborrhoeic dermatitis are not emergencies, but you should seek medical advice promptly if you notice:

- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, swelling, warmth, pus, or pain
- Rapid spreading of the rash or patches that are not responding to usual treatment
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside a worsening rash
- Significant distress, hair loss, or impact on daily life from persistent symptoms

These situations warrant a timely review by a doctor rather than continued self-treatment.
