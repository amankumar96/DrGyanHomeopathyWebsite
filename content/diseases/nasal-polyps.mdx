## What is Nasal Polyps?

Nasal polyps are soft, painless, non-cancerous growths that develop on the lining of the nasal passages or sinuses, usually as a result of ongoing inflammation. They are often linked to conditions such as chronic sinusitis, allergies, or asthma. Nasal polyps vary in size, and small ones may not cause noticeable symptoms, while larger ones, or multiple polyps, can block the nasal passage and affect breathing, smell, and sinus drainage. They tend to be a long-term, sometimes recurring issue rather than a one-off problem.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms associated with nasal polyps include:

- A persistent blocked or stuffy nose
- Reduced or lost sense of smell and taste
- Runny nose or postnasal drip
- Facial pain, pressure, or headache, particularly around the sinuses
- Snoring or disturbed sleep due to nasal blockage
- Recurrent sinus infections
- A feeling of pressure in the upper teeth or face in some cases

Symptoms often develop gradually and may be mistaken for ongoing allergies or sinus congestion.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of nasal polyps is not fully understood, but they are thought to result from chronic inflammation of the nasal and sinus lining. Factors associated with a higher likelihood of developing nasal polyps include:

- Chronic sinusitis or long-standing sinus inflammation
- Allergic conditions, such as hay fever
- Asthma, particularly in people with more difficult-to-control symptoms
- Sensitivity to aspirin or certain anti-inflammatory medications
- Cystic fibrosis, especially in children with nasal polyps
- A family history of nasal polyps

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use any prescribed nasal steroid sprays regularly and as directed, even when symptoms feel better, for ongoing control
- Try saline nasal rinses to help clear mucus and support nasal hygiene
- Manage underlying allergies or asthma well, as this can reduce inflammation contributing to polyps
- Avoid known allergy triggers where possible, such as dust or pollen
- Avoid smoking and exposure to irritants that can worsen nasal inflammation
- Keep track of symptoms such as smell changes or recurrent sinus infections to discuss with your doctor

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Nasal blockage that is only on one side, especially with bleeding or an unusual appearance, which should always be checked
- Severe facial pain, swelling, or vision changes, which could suggest spread of infection
- High fever along with facial swelling or severe sinus pain
- Sudden, significant worsening of breathing difficulty

While nasal polyps themselves are not usually an emergency, these specific signs should be reviewed by a doctor promptly rather than waited out.
