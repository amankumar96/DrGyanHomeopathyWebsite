## What is Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)?

Benign prostatic hyperplasia, commonly known as BPH or an enlarged prostate, is a non-cancerous growth of the prostate gland that becomes increasingly common as men get older. The prostate surrounds part of the urethra, the tube that carries urine out of the body, so as it enlarges it can press on the urethra and affect how the bladder empties. BPH is not prostate cancer and does not mean a man will develop prostate cancer, though a doctor will typically want to rule out other causes of urinary symptoms, including cancer, before confirming the diagnosis.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of BPH include:

- A weak or slow urine stream
- Difficulty starting urination, or straining to pass urine
- Frequent need to urinate, especially at night (nocturia)
- A sudden, strong urge to urinate
- A feeling that the bladder has not fully emptied
- Dribbling at the end of urination
- In some cases, stopping and starting while urinating

Symptoms tend to develop gradually and vary in severity, and not every man with an enlarged prostate experiences bothersome symptoms.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of BPH is not fully understood, but it is thought to relate to age-related hormonal changes affecting prostate tissue growth over time.

Known risk factors include:

- Increasing age, as BPH becomes substantially more common after age 50
- A family history of BPH
- Obesity and lack of physical activity
- Type 2 diabetes and heart disease, which are associated with higher risk
- Erectile dysfunction, which is often associated with urinary symptoms in ageing men

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Reduce fluid intake in the evening to help limit nighttime urination
- Limit caffeine and alcohol, which can irritate the bladder and increase urine production
- Avoid holding in urine for long periods; urinate when you feel the urge
- Try to fully empty the bladder each time by taking a little extra time
- Stay physically active, as regular exercise is associated with better urinary symptom control
- Avoid certain over-the-counter cold and allergy medicines that can worsen urinary retention; check with a pharmacist or doctor first

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden inability to pass urine at all (acute urinary retention), which is a medical emergency
- Blood in the urine
- Severe pain in the lower abdomen or back
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell along with urinary symptoms, which may suggest infection
- Significant, rapid worsening of urinary symptoms

These situations can require urgent medical care, so contact your doctor or go to an emergency department without delay.
