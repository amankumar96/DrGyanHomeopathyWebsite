## What is Thalassemia?

Thalassemia is an inherited blood disorder in which the body does not produce enough healthy haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. It is caused by changes in the genes responsible for haemoglobin production and is present from birth. Thalassemia ranges widely in severity, from thalassemia trait or minor, where a person carries the gene with little or no symptoms, to thalassemia major, which typically causes significant anaemia and requires lifelong medical management, including regular blood transfusions. The condition cannot be cured, but with appropriate specialist care, many people with thalassemia are able to live full lives.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of thalassemia depend on the type and severity, and may include:

- Fatigue and weakness
- Pale or yellowish skin (jaundice)
- Slow growth or delayed puberty in children
- Enlarged spleen, liver, or heart in more significant forms
- Dark urine
- Bone deformities, particularly of the face, in severe untreated cases
- Shortness of breath, especially with exertion

People with thalassemia trait or minor often have no symptoms at all and may only discover they carry the gene through a blood test.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Thalassemia is caused by inherited changes in genes involved in haemoglobin production, passed from parents to children. Risk factors include:

- Having one or both parents who carry a thalassemia gene
- Family or ethnic background with a higher prevalence of thalassemia, including Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, South Asian, and Southeast Asian backgrounds
- Having a sibling or close relative with thalassemia

Because it is a genetic condition, thalassemia cannot be caused or prevented by lifestyle, diet, or any external exposure, though genetic counselling can help families understand and plan around their risk.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for thalassemia depends on severity. Mild forms may need little or no treatment beyond monitoring, while moderate to severe forms typically require regular blood transfusions to maintain healthy haemoglobin levels, along with iron chelation therapy to remove excess iron that builds up from frequent transfusions. In some cases, bone marrow or stem cell transplant may be considered. These treatments are often essential to prevent serious complications and should not be reduced or stopped without specialist guidance.

Some patients explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this specialist care, often in the hope of supporting general well-being. Homeopathy is not a substitute for transfusions, chelation therapy, or other prescribed treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled transfusion and monitoring appointments
- Take iron chelation medication exactly as prescribed, even when feeling well
- Eat a balanced diet, and discuss iron intake with your care team, as some people with thalassemia need to limit dietary iron
- Take folic acid supplements if advised by your doctor
- Stay up to date with vaccinations, as recommended by your specialist team, particularly if the spleen has been affected
- Rest adequately and pace physical activity according to energy levels
- Seek genetic counselling if planning a family, to understand inheritance risk

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you or your child experience:

- Extreme fatigue, breathlessness, or chest pain, which can signal severe anaemia
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Signs of infection such as high fever, especially if the spleen has been removed
- Significant abdominal swelling or pain, which may relate to an enlarged spleen or liver
- Symptoms of iron overload, such as joint pain, unusual skin darkening, or new heart or liver symptoms

These situations need prompt specialist review, so contact your haematology team or seek emergency care without delay.
