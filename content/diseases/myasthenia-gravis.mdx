## What is Myasthenia Gravis?

Myasthenia gravis is a long-term autoimmune condition in which the immune system produces antibodies that interfere with communication between nerves and muscles at the neuromuscular junction. This leads to muscle weakness that characteristically worsens with activity and improves with rest. It can affect muscles controlling the eyes, face, swallowing, limbs, and in more severe cases, breathing. While there is no cure, many people are able to manage symptoms with ongoing medical treatment and monitoring.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of myasthenia gravis include:

- Drooping of one or both eyelids
- Double or blurred vision
- Weakness that worsens with repeated use of a muscle and improves with rest
- Difficulty chewing, swallowing, or slurred speech
- Weakness in the arms, legs, neck, or facial muscles
- Fatigue that builds up through the day
- In more severe episodes, difficulty breathing

Symptoms can fluctuate from day to day and may vary considerably in severity between individuals.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Myasthenia gravis occurs when the immune system mistakenly produces antibodies that block or damage receptors needed for nerve-to-muscle signalling, most often affecting acetylcholine receptors. The exact trigger for this autoimmune response is not fully understood. Recognised associations include:

- An abnormality or tumour of the thymus gland in some individuals
- Other autoimmune conditions occurring alongside myasthenia gravis
- Being female, particularly for onset in younger adulthood, though it can affect anyone, including older men
- Rarely, certain medications or illnesses unmasking or worsening symptoms

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for myasthenia gravis usually includes medications that improve nerve-to-muscle signalling, such as pyridostigmine, along with immune-suppressing treatments to reduce the underlying autoimmune activity. Some people benefit from thymus gland surgery, and more severe or sudden worsening may require treatments such as plasma exchange or intravenous immunoglobulin, often in hospital.

Some patients explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often hoping to support general well-being. Prescribed medication for myasthenia gravis should never be stopped or changed without medical advice, as doing so can risk serious worsening of symptoms. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Pace activities and rest before fatigue becomes severe, planning demanding tasks for times of day when strength is usually better
- Take medications at the times advised by your doctor to help maintain more even symptom control
- Be cautious with new medications, including over-the-counter products, and check with your doctor or pharmacist, as some can worsen myasthenia gravis
- Manage infections promptly, as illness can worsen symptoms
- Wear an eye patch or seek advice for double vision if it affects daily activities, as guided by your care team
- Keep a record of symptom patterns and triggers to share with your neurologist
- Carry information about your condition and medications in case of emergency

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical care immediately if there is:

- Sudden or severe difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Difficulty swallowing that leads to choking, or an inability to swallow saliva
- Rapidly worsening weakness affecting the whole body
- Severe weakness following an infection, new medication, or surgery

These can be signs of myasthenic crisis, a medical emergency in which breathing muscles become critically weak. Never stop or reduce prescribed medication on your own, and seek emergency care without delay if these symptoms occur.
