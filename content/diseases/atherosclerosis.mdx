## What is Atherosclerosis?

Atherosclerosis is a condition in which fatty deposits, known as plaques, build up gradually inside the walls of arteries, the blood vessels that carry oxygen-rich blood around the body. Over time, this build-up narrows and stiffens the arteries, reducing blood flow to organs and tissues. Atherosclerosis can affect arteries throughout the body, including those supplying the heart, brain, and limbs, and is a major underlying process behind common cardiovascular conditions such as heart attack, stroke, and peripheral artery disease. It typically develops slowly over many years, often without symptoms in its early stages.

## Symptoms

Atherosclerosis itself often causes no symptoms until an artery becomes significantly narrowed or blocked. When symptoms do occur, they depend on which arteries are affected and may include:

- Chest pain or tightness (angina) if the heart's arteries are affected
- Shortness of breath on exertion
- Leg pain or cramping when walking, which eases with rest (claudication), if leg arteries are affected
- Weakness, numbness, or difficulty speaking if arteries supplying the brain are affected
- Erectile dysfunction, in some cases, if pelvic arteries are affected
- Fatigue and reduced exercise tolerance

## Causes & Risk Factors

Atherosclerosis develops due to damage to the inner lining of artery walls, allowing fatty deposits, cholesterol, and other substances to accumulate over time. Recognised risk factors include:

- High blood cholesterol, particularly high LDL ("bad") cholesterol
- High blood pressure
- Smoking and tobacco use
- Diabetes or insulin resistance
- Obesity and a sedentary lifestyle
- An unhealthy diet high in saturated fat and low in fruits and vegetables
- Family history of early heart disease
- Increasing age

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of atherosclerosis focuses on reducing cardiovascular risk through lifestyle changes and, where needed, medicines such as statins to lower cholesterol, blood pressure medicines, and antiplatelet drugs to reduce clotting risk. In more advanced cases, procedures such as angioplasty or bypass surgery may be used to restore blood flow.

Patients interested in homeopathy should discuss this openly with their doctor and consider it only as a complementary addition to, never a replacement for, prescribed cardiovascular treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Eat a heart-healthy diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and healthy fats, and lower in saturated fat and salt
- Engage in regular physical activity as advised by your doctor
- Stop smoking and avoid tobacco products
- Maintain a healthy body weight
- Manage blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar levels through regular monitoring and prescribed treatment
- Limit alcohol intake
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, adequate sleep, and social support

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical care immediately if you experience signs of a heart attack or stroke, including:

- Chest pain, pressure, or tightness, especially spreading to the arm, jaw, neck, or back
- Sudden shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or lightheadedness
- Sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the face, arm, or leg
- Sudden difficulty speaking, confusion, or trouble understanding speech
- Sudden severe headache, vision loss, or loss of balance
- Sudden, severe leg pain with pale, cold limb (which may indicate a blocked artery)

These are warning signs of a heart attack or stroke and require immediate emergency medical attention, without delay for any complementary treatment.
