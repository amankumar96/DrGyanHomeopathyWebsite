## What is Prostate Cancer?

Prostate cancer is a cancer that develops in the prostate gland, a small gland below the bladder in men that produces part of the fluid in semen. It is important to understand that prostate cancer is a different condition from benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH), which is a common, non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate covered on a separate page on this site. BPH does not turn into cancer, but because some urinary symptoms of BPH and prostate cancer can overlap, proper medical evaluation is essential to reach the correct diagnosis. Prostate cancer is a malignancy that requires diagnosis, staging, and treatment under the care of a urologist or oncologist. Many prostate cancers grow slowly, and when detected early, outcomes are generally favourable with appropriate specialist treatment.

## Symptoms

Prostate cancer often causes no symptoms at all in its early stages, which is one reason regular check-ups and appropriate screening discussions with a doctor matter. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

- Urinary problems, such as a weak or hard-to-start urine stream
- Frequent urination, especially at night
- Pain or a burning sensation when urinating
- Blood in the urine or semen
- Painful ejaculation
- Persistent pain in the lower back, hips, or pelvis

Many of these symptoms can also be caused by non-cancerous conditions, including BPH, so they should never be assumed to indicate cancer or dismissed as harmless without a doctor's assessment.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of prostate cancer is not fully understood, but it is known to involve changes in the genetic material (DNA) of prostate cells, some of which are inherited and some of which develop over a person's lifetime. Recognised risk factors include:

- Increasing age, as prostate cancer is rare before age 50 and becomes more common with age
- A family history of prostate cancer in a parent, sibling, or child
- Certain inherited genetic factors
- Race and ethnicity, as some population groups have a higher incidence and may be diagnosed at a younger age or with more advanced disease

Having one or more risk factors does not mean a person will develop prostate cancer, and some men with no known risk factors are still diagnosed with it. Only a doctor can assess individual risk and recommend appropriate screening.

## How Homeopathy Approaches Prostate Cancer

Homeopathy does not diagnose, stage, or treat cancer. It has no role in shrinking, slowing, curing, or otherwise affecting prostate cancer, and no qualified practitioner should ever suggest that it does. A homeopathic consultation, if a patient chooses to pursue one, would typically focus only on the person's general wellbeing, how they are coping emotionally with a cancer diagnosis and its treatment, and overall quality of life, never on the cancer itself.

Some patients explore homeopathy purely as a complementary, wellbeing-focused approach alongside their oncology or urology treatment, for example to support general comfort during a demanding treatment journey. It is important to understand that scientific evidence for homeopathy's effectiveness in cancer of any kind, including prostate cancer, does not exist, and major health bodies such as the NHS state there is no robust evidence that homeopathy is effective for any health condition. Homeopathy must never be used as a substitute for diagnosis or treatment by a urologist or oncologist, and relying on it instead of, or to delay, conventional cancer treatment can be dangerous.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Prostate cancer requires diagnosis and treatment planning by a urologist or oncologist. Depending on how early the cancer is detected, its grade, and the patient's overall health, conventional treatment options include active surveillance or watchful waiting for very slow-growing, low-risk cancers, surgery to remove the prostate, radiation therapy, hormone therapy to block the hormones that fuel cancer growth, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy. The treatment team determines the most suitable approach, and this may evolve over time with monitoring.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy only as a complementary, general wellbeing approach alongside this essential oncology or urology treatment, never in place of it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled appointments, scans, and follow-up tests with your urologist or oncologist, even when feeling well
- Take all prescribed medications exactly as directed, without stopping or skipping doses
- Maintain a balanced diet and regular physical activity, as advised by your treatment team, to support overall health during treatment
- Seek support for emotional wellbeing, whether through counselling, support groups, or family and friends, as a cancer diagnosis can be stressful
- Discuss any side effects of treatment, such as urinary or sexual changes, openly with your doctor so they can be managed appropriately
- Avoid relying on unproven remedies or delaying medical care in favour of alternative approaches
- Inform your treatment team before starting any complementary approach, including homeopathy, so it can be considered alongside your care plan

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Prostate cancer and its treatment require ongoing specialist care, but prompt medical evaluation is needed if you notice:

- New or worsening urinary symptoms, such as difficulty urinating, a weak stream, or increased frequency
- Blood in the urine or semen
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent or worsening pain in the bones, back, hips, or pelvis
- New, persistent pelvic or lower abdominal pain
- Any symptoms that develop or worsen during cancer treatment

Unexplained weight loss and persistent bone pain in particular can be signs that require urgent specialist evaluation, so contact your urologist or oncologist, or seek emergency care, without delay.
