## What is Ganglion?

A ganglion, or ganglion cyst, is a non-cancerous, fluid-filled lump that most commonly develops near a joint or tendon, especially around the wrist or hand, though it can also occur in the foot or ankle. The sac contains a thick, jelly-like fluid similar to that found in joints. Ganglions vary in size, can appear gradually or suddenly, and sometimes change size over time. Many cause no problems beyond their appearance, while others can be uncomfortable or limit movement depending on their size and location.

## Symptoms

Common features of a ganglion cyst include:

- A smooth, round or oval lump under the skin, often near the wrist, hand, or foot
- Size that may change over time, sometimes becoming larger or smaller
- Mild ache or discomfort, particularly with certain movements
- A feeling of weakness or altered sensation in the area, if the cyst presses on a nearby nerve
- Usually no pain at all in many cases, especially small ganglions
- Skin over the lump usually looks and feels normal

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of ganglion cysts is not fully understood. They are thought to arise when joint or tendon fluid leaks out and collects within a thin sac, possibly related to wear and tear or minor irritation of the joint or tendon lining.

Factors associated with a higher likelihood of developing a ganglion include:

- Being between 20 and 40 years of age
- Being female, as ganglions are more common in women
- Previous joint or tendon injury in the affected area
- Osteoarthritis in nearby joints, in some cases
- Repetitive use or strain of the joint or tendon, in certain individuals

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid repeatedly pressing on or knocking the lump
- Rest the affected joint if movement causes discomfort
- Wear a wrist splint or supportive brace if recommended by your doctor during flare-ups
- Avoid attempting to burst or squeeze the cyst yourself
- Monitor any changes in size, pain, or sensation and note them for your doctor
- Maintain general joint health through gentle, regular movement

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- Rapid growth, redness, warmth, or increasing pain in the lump, which could suggest infection or another cause
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the hand or foot, suggesting nerve involvement
- A lump that is hard, fixed, or irregular in shape rather than smooth and mobile
- Any new lump that has not been assessed by a doctor before, to confirm the diagnosis

These features need medical evaluation to rule out other causes and decide on the right next steps.
