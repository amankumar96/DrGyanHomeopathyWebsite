## What is Peptic Ulcer?

A peptic ulcer is an open sore that develops on the inner lining of the stomach (a gastric ulcer) or the upper part of the small intestine (a duodenal ulcer). It occurs when the mucous layer that normally protects these linings from stomach acid is damaged, allowing acid to irritate and erode the underlying tissue. Peptic ulcers are common and, with appropriate medical treatment, usually heal over several weeks, though untreated ulcers can lead to serious complications.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of a peptic ulcer include:

- Burning or gnawing pain in the upper abdomen, often between meals or at night
- Pain that may temporarily improve after eating or taking antacids, then return later
- Bloating and a feeling of fullness
- Nausea or occasional vomiting
- Loss of appetite and unintentional weight loss
- Indigestion or heartburn-like discomfort
- In some cases, no noticeable symptoms until a complication develops

## Causes & Risk Factors

The two main causes of peptic ulcers are:

- Infection with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) bacteria, which can damage the protective stomach lining
- Long-term or frequent use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), such as ibuprofen or aspirin

Other factors that can increase risk or worsen symptoms include:

- Smoking
- Heavy alcohol use
- Significant physical stress, such as serious illness or injury, in some cases
- Excess stomach acid production due to rare underlying conditions
- Family history of peptic ulcers, in some individuals

Everyday stress and spicy food do not directly cause peptic ulcers but can aggravate symptoms in someone who already has one.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for peptic ulcers depends on the cause. If H. pylori infection is present, a course of antibiotics combined with acid-reducing medication is typically prescribed. If NSAIDs are the cause, your doctor may recommend stopping or switching these medications and prescribing acid-reducing treatment to allow healing. Follow-up testing may be used to confirm the ulcer has healed or the infection has cleared.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment. It should not be used as a substitute for antibiotics or acid-reducing medication. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid or reduce NSAID painkillers unless advised otherwise by your doctor
- Limit alcohol intake and avoid smoking, both of which can delay healing
- Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid foods that personally worsen your symptoms
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, regular sleep, and exercise where possible
- Avoid lying down immediately after eating
- Complete the full course of any prescribed antibiotics or acid-reducing medication, even if symptoms improve early
- Attend follow-up tests if advised, to confirm healing or clearance of infection

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Peptic ulcers can lead to serious complications. Seek emergency medical care immediately if you experience:

- Vomiting blood, or vomit that looks like dark coffee grounds
- Black, tarry, or very dark stools, which can indicate internal bleeding
- Sudden, severe abdominal pain, especially if it spreads or the abdomen becomes rigid, which can suggest a perforation
- Feeling faint, dizzy, or experiencing a rapid heartbeat alongside abdominal symptoms
- Persistent vomiting that prevents keeping food or fluids down

These symptoms can indicate bleeding or perforation of the ulcer, which are medical emergencies requiring immediate attention.
