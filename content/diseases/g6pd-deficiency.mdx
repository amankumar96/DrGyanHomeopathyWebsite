## What is G6PD Deficiency?

G6PD deficiency is an inherited condition in which the body does not produce enough of an enzyme called glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase, which normally helps protect red blood cells from certain types of stress. Without enough of this enzyme, red blood cells can break down prematurely, a process called hemolysis, when exposed to specific triggers such as certain foods, medicines, or infections. Many people with G6PD deficiency have no symptoms for most of their lives and only experience problems after exposure to a trigger.

## Symptoms

Most people with G6PD deficiency have no symptoms until exposed to a trigger. When a hemolytic episode occurs, symptoms can include:

- Sudden tiredness, weakness, or paleness
- Yellowing of the skin and eyes (jaundice)
- Dark, tea-coloured, or cola-coloured urine
- Rapid heartbeat or shortness of breath
- Enlarged spleen, in some cases
- Fever, in episodes linked to infection

Symptoms usually appear within hours to a few days after exposure to a trigger.

## Causes & Risk Factors

G6PD deficiency is caused by a change in the gene that provides instructions for making the G6PD enzyme. It is inherited in an X-linked pattern, meaning it is more common in males, though females can also be affected or carry the gene.

Known triggers that can cause a hemolytic episode in people with G6PD deficiency include:

- Eating fava beans (broad beans)
- Certain medicines, including some antimalarial drugs, sulfa-based antibiotics, and other oxidant drugs
- Infections, including viral and bacterial illnesses
- Exposure to naphthalene, found in some mothballs
- In newborns, G6PD deficiency can contribute to severe jaundice shortly after birth

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of G6PD deficiency centres on avoiding known triggers, including specific foods and medicines, and prompt treatment of any hemolytic episode, which may require monitoring, fluids, or in some cases a blood transfusion in hospital. Newborns with G6PD deficiency and jaundice may need specific monitoring and treatment to prevent complications.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Learn and keep a written list, confirmed with your doctor, of foods and medicines to avoid
- Always inform any doctor, dentist, or pharmacist about your G6PD deficiency before starting new medicines
- Avoid fava beans and products containing them
- Keep away from mothballs containing naphthalene
- Carry a medical alert card or note, especially when travelling
- Treat infections promptly and inform your doctor of your G6PD status when unwell

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention immediately if you or your child develop, especially after eating fava beans or taking a new medicine:

- Sudden, significant yellowing of the skin or eyes
- Dark, tea-coloured, or cola-coloured urine
- Sudden paleness, extreme tiredness, or weakness
- Rapid breathing, rapid heartbeat, or breathlessness
- Fainting or confusion

These signs can indicate a hemolytic crisis, a medical emergency. It is important to know in advance, together with your doctor, exactly which foods and medicines you need to avoid, and to seek urgent care without delay if these symptoms appear.
