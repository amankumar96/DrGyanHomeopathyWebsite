## What is Fibroadenoma?

A fibroadenoma is a common, benign (non-cancerous) lump that forms in the breast, made up of both glandular and fibrous tissue. It is one of the most frequent causes of a breast lump in younger women, typically those in their teens, twenties, or thirties, though it can occur at other ages too. Fibroadenomas are generally smooth, firm or rubbery, and mobile under the skin, and many remain stable in size over time, though some grow slowly or fluctuate with hormonal changes.

## Symptoms

Typical features of a fibroadenoma include:

- A firm, smooth, rubbery lump in the breast
- A lump that moves easily under the skin when touched
- Usually painless, though some women notice tenderness, particularly before menstrual periods
- Can occur as a single lump or, less commonly, multiple lumps
- Generally unchanged or slow-growing over time, though size can fluctuate with hormonal changes, pregnancy, or breastfeeding

Because other breast conditions, including some that are more serious, can also present as a lump, these features alone cannot confirm a fibroadenoma without medical assessment.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The precise cause of fibroadenomas is not completely understood, but they are believed to be related to the sensitivity of breast tissue to natural reproductive hormones, particularly oestrogen. Recognised associations include:

- Being in the reproductive age group, especially the teenage years to the thirties
- Hormonal fluctuations during the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, or breastfeeding
- Family history of fibroadenomas or other benign breast conditions
- Use of hormonal medications, in some cases

Fibroadenomas are not caused by lifestyle choices, and having one does not mean a woman is more likely to develop breast cancer, though this should still be confirmed through proper medical evaluation.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional evaluation of a breast lump typically involves a clinical breast examination, imaging such as ultrasound or mammography, and sometimes a needle biopsy to confirm the diagnosis. Once confirmed as a fibroadenoma, management may involve simple monitoring with periodic check-ups, or surgical removal if the lump is large, growing, causing discomfort, or if there is any uncertainty about the diagnosis.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Perform regular breast self-examinations to stay familiar with your normal breast tissue
- Attend scheduled follow-up appointments and imaging as advised by your doctor
- Wear a well-fitted, supportive bra if tenderness is an issue
- Maintain a generally healthy lifestyle, including balanced nutrition and regular activity
- Keep a note of any changes in size, shape, or new symptoms to discuss with your doctor
- Avoid unnecessary anxiety once a lump has been medically confirmed as benign, while still keeping up with monitoring

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Any new breast lump should always be evaluated by a doctor promptly, regardless of how it feels, to confirm what it is. Seek medical assessment without delay, and more urgently, if you notice:

- A new lump that has not been previously evaluated
- Rapid growth, hardening, or a change in the shape or feel of an existing lump
- Skin changes over the lump, such as dimpling, puckering, or redness
- Nipple changes, including discharge, inversion, or a nipple that pulls to one side
- Swelling or lumps in the armpit
- Pain that is new, severe, or does not settle

Proper medical evaluation of any breast lump is essential to confirm the diagnosis and rule out other causes, and should never be delayed in favour of a complementary approach alone.
