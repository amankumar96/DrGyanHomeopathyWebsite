## What is Hemiplegia?

Hemiplegia is weakness or paralysis affecting one entire side of the body — typically the arm, leg, and sometimes the face on the same side. It most commonly results from a stroke, where blood supply to part of the brain is interrupted or a bleed occurs, damaging the areas that control movement. It can also follow traumatic brain injury, certain infections, tumours affecting the brain, or, in children, brain injury occurring before or around birth. Hemiplegia is usually a lasting consequence of injury to the brain or spinal cord that has already been treated as a medical emergency, and the ongoing focus afterward is on rehabilitation, support, and monitoring for further events such as another stroke.

## Symptoms

Hemiplegia can present differently depending on the extent and location of the underlying injury, but common features include:

- Weakness or complete loss of movement on one side of the body, affecting the arm, leg, or both
- Stiffness or spasticity in the affected limbs over time
- Difficulty with balance and walking
- Drooping on one side of the face
- Difficulty with fine motor tasks, such as buttoning clothes or writing, on the affected side
- Changes in sensation, such as numbness or tingling, on the affected side
- Fatigue, which is common during rehabilitation

## Causes & Risk Factors

The most common cause of hemiplegia is stroke, either from a blocked blood vessel (ischaemic stroke) or a bleed in the brain (haemorrhagic stroke). Other causes and risk factors include:

- Traumatic brain or spinal cord injury
- Brain tumours affecting motor pathways
- Brain infections, such as encephalitis, in some cases
- Cerebral palsy, where brain injury occurs before, during, or shortly after birth
- Risk factors for stroke itself, including high blood pressure, diabetes, high cholesterol, smoking, atrial fibrillation, and a sedentary lifestyle

## How Homeopathy Approaches Hemiplegia

Hemiplegia usually follows a medical emergency, such as a stroke, that has already been investigated and treated. The ongoing phase of care centres on structured rehabilitation, and homeopathy does not have a recognised role in restoring nerve or brain function, reversing paralysis, or replacing physiotherapy.

Some patients explore homeopathy during the rehabilitation period as a complementary, general well-being approach, alongside their prescribed physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and medical follow-up. It is important to understand that scientific evidence for homeopathy's effectiveness in hemiplegia or stroke recovery is limited, and bodies such as the NHS state there is no robust evidence that homeopathy is effective for any health condition. Any such interest should be discussed with the rehabilitation team.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of hemiplegia focuses on treating the underlying cause where possible, followed by structured rehabilitation, including physiotherapy to maintain and improve movement, occupational therapy for daily living skills, and sometimes speech therapy. Managing underlying risk factors, such as blood pressure and cholesterol, is important to reduce the chance of further events.

Homeopathy, if used at all, should be considered only as a general, complementary well-being support and never as a substitute for rehabilitation or medical follow-up. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend physiotherapy and occupational therapy sessions consistently, as regular practice supports recovery
- Practice prescribed exercises at home between sessions as advised by your therapist
- Use any recommended mobility aids or supports to reduce fall risk
- Manage underlying risk factors such as blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar as directed by your doctor
- Eat a balanced diet and stay as physically active as safely possible
- Seek emotional support, as adjusting to changed physical ability can be challenging for patients and families alike
- Make home modifications, such as grab rails or ramps, if mobility is affected

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Hemiplegia is usually the result of a stroke or brain injury that has already been treated as an emergency, but a new stroke can happen again. Learn and watch for the FAST warning signs, and call emergency services immediately if you notice:

- Face drooping on one or both sides, especially if new or worsening
- Arm weakness, particularly sudden weakness or inability to raise one or both arms
- Speech difficulty, such as slurred speech or trouble finding words
- Time to call emergency services immediately if any of these signs appear, even if they seem to pass

Also seek urgent medical review for sudden worsening of existing weakness, new severe headache, confusion, seizures, or loss of consciousness, as these may indicate a new or ongoing medical emergency.
