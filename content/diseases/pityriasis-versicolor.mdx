## What is Pityriasis Versicolor?

Pityriasis versicolor, also called tinea versicolor, is a common skin condition caused by an overgrowth of a type of yeast that normally lives harmlessly on the skin. This overgrowth leads to small, scaly patches that can appear lighter or darker than the surrounding skin, most often on the chest, back, shoulders, and neck. It is more common in hot, humid climates and tends to be a cosmetic concern rather than a serious health problem, though it can take time and treatment to resolve.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of pityriasis versicolor include:

- Patches of skin that are lighter or darker than the surrounding skin
- Patches commonly appearing on the chest, back, neck, and upper arms
- Mild scaling on the affected patches
- Patches that may be more noticeable after sun exposure, as affected skin does not tan evenly
- Mild itching in some cases, though many people have no itching at all
- Patches that may merge to form larger areas over time

## Causes & Risk Factors

Pityriasis versicolor is caused by an overgrowth of a yeast (a type of fungus) that is normally present on everyone's skin in small amounts. Several factors can encourage this overgrowth, including:

- Hot, humid weather or climates
- Excessive sweating
- Oily skin
- A weakened immune system
- Hormonal changes
- Use of oily or greasy skin products
- Family history or individual skin tendency

It is not caused by poor hygiene, and it is not considered a serious infection, though it often recurs, particularly during warm seasons.

Treatment is generally effective at clearing the fungal overgrowth, though skin color can take weeks to months to fully even out afterward, and recurrence is common, especially in warm weather.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep skin clean and dry, especially after sweating or exercise
- Wear loose, breathable fabrics, particularly in hot or humid weather
- Avoid very oily or greasy skin and hair products on affected areas
- Use sunscreen on affected patches, as sun exposure can make discoloration more noticeable
- Consider periodic use of antifungal washes during warm seasons if your doctor advises this to reduce recurrence
- Be patient with skin color returning to normal, as this takes time even after successful treatment

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Pityriasis versicolor is generally not an emergency, but see your doctor for diagnosis and treatment, and seek prompt review if you notice:

- Patches that spread rapidly or cover a large area of the body
- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or pus
- No improvement despite completing a full course of prescribed antifungal treatment
- Uncertainty about the diagnosis, since other skin conditions can look similar and need different treatment
- Associated fever or feeling generally unwell, which would be unusual for this condition and should be assessed

A doctor can confirm the diagnosis, often with a simple skin examination, and advise on the right antifungal treatment for you.
