## What is Endometriosis?

Endometriosis is a long-term condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the womb (endometrium) grows in other parts of the body, most commonly within the pelvis, such as on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, or the lining of the pelvic cavity. This tissue responds to the menstrual cycle in a similar way to the womb lining, thickening and breaking down each month, but because it has no way to leave the body, it can cause inflammation, pain, and the formation of scar tissue. Endometriosis affects people of reproductive age and can vary widely in severity, with some people experiencing significant pain and others having few or no symptoms.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of endometriosis include:

- Pelvic pain, often linked to the menstrual cycle
- Painful periods that may not improve with usual pain relief
- Pain during or after sexual intercourse
- Pain when passing urine or opening the bowels, particularly during periods
- Heavy menstrual bleeding or bleeding between periods
- Difficulty becoming pregnant
- Fatigue
- Bloating or nausea, particularly around the time of periods

Symptoms vary considerably between individuals, and the severity of symptoms does not always match the extent of the condition found internally.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of endometriosis is not fully understood, though several theories exist, including retrograde menstruation, where menstrual blood flows backward into the pelvis, and genetic or immune system factors. Known associations include:

- A family history of endometriosis
- Starting periods at an early age
- Short menstrual cycles or heavy, long-lasting periods
- Never having given birth
- Certain structural features of the reproductive organs

Research into the underlying causes is ongoing, and it is likely that multiple factors contribute.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use a heat pack on the lower abdomen to help ease period pain
- Keep a symptom diary to track patterns and discuss them with your doctor
- Stay physically active, as gentle regular exercise may help some people manage symptoms
- Prioritise rest during flares of pain or fatigue
- Seek support from others living with endometriosis, such as patient groups, if helpful
- Discuss pain management strategies with your doctor rather than relying solely on over-the-counter medication long term

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

- Severe pelvic pain that is sudden, intense, or different from your usual pattern
- Heavy bleeding that soaks through pads or tampons quickly, or causes dizziness or fainting
- Fever along with pelvic pain, which may suggest an infection
- Difficulty passing urine or having a bowel movement, or significant pain while doing so
- Signs of significant blood loss, such as pale skin, rapid heartbeat, or feeling faint
- Pain that is not controlled by prescribed pain relief

These situations may indicate a complication requiring urgent assessment, so contact your doctor or seek emergency care without delay.
