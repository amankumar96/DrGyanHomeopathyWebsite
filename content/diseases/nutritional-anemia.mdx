## What is Nutritional Anemia?

Nutritional anemia occurs when the body does not have enough healthy red blood cells because it lacks key nutrients needed to make them, most commonly iron, but sometimes vitamin B12 or folate. Red blood cells carry oxygen around the body, so a shortage can leave a person feeling tired, weak, and short of breath. Nutritional anemia is common worldwide and is usually correctable once the underlying deficiency is identified and addressed with the guidance of a doctor.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of nutritional anemia include:

- Persistent tiredness and lack of energy
- Pale skin, lips, or inner eyelids
- Breathlessness, especially during exertion
- Rapid or noticeable heartbeat (palpitations)
- Headaches and dizziness
- Brittle nails and hair loss
- Poor concentration and irritability
- Unusual cravings to eat non-food items such as ice or chalk (pica), sometimes seen in iron deficiency
- Sore or swollen tongue, sometimes seen in vitamin B12 or folate deficiency

## Causes & Risk Factors

Nutritional anemia develops when the body does not get enough of certain nutrients, or cannot absorb them properly. Common causes and risk factors include:

- Diets low in iron, vitamin B12, or folate, including some vegetarian or vegan diets without adequate planning
- Increased nutritional needs during pregnancy or breastfeeding
- Heavy menstrual periods or other chronic blood loss, including from the digestive tract
- Digestive conditions that reduce nutrient absorption, such as coeliac disease or inflammatory bowel disease
- Previous stomach or intestinal surgery
- Certain medications that interfere with nutrient absorption
- Older age, which can be associated with reduced dietary intake or absorption

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Include iron-rich foods such as leafy greens, legumes, nuts, and lean meats where appropriate for your diet
- Pair plant-based iron sources with vitamin C-rich foods to improve absorption
- Limit tea and coffee around mealtimes, as they can reduce iron absorption
- Include sources of vitamin B12, such as dairy, eggs, or fortified foods, particularly on a vegetarian or vegan diet
- Attend follow-up blood tests as advised to monitor improvement
- Discuss any persistent dietary restrictions with a doctor or dietitian

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you experience:

- Severe breathlessness, chest pain, or a rapid heartbeat
- Fainting, severe dizziness, or confusion
- Black, tarry stools or visible blood in stool or vomit, which may indicate internal bleeding
- Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- Marked paleness with extreme fatigue that worsens quickly

These symptoms may indicate severe anemia or an underlying cause needing urgent medical evaluation.
