## What is Post Herpetic Neuralgia?

Post herpetic neuralgia is persistent nerve pain that continues in the area of skin where a shingles rash previously occurred, even after the rash itself has healed. Shingles is caused by reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus, the same virus that causes chickenpox, and it can damage the affected nerves, leading to ongoing pain signals even without an active rash. It is more common in older adults and tends to improve gradually over time, though for some people it can persist for months or longer and significantly affect daily life.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of post herpetic neuralgia include:

- Persistent burning, aching, or stabbing pain in the area of a previous shingles rash
- Sharp, electric-shock-like sensations
- Extreme sensitivity to light touch (allodynia), where even clothing or a light breeze can be painful
- Itching or numbness in the affected area
- Pain that can interfere with sleep, mood, and daily activities
- Symptoms typically confined to the specific area, often on one side of the body, where the original shingles rash appeared

## Causes & Risk Factors

Post herpetic neuralgia results from damage to nerve fibers caused by the varicella-zoster virus during a shingles episode. The virus affects how pain signals are processed, leading to ongoing pain even after the skin has healed. Risk factors include:

- Older age, with risk increasing significantly after age 50 to 60
- Having had more severe shingles pain or rash
- Shingles affecting the face, particularly around the eye
- A delay in starting antiviral treatment after shingles symptoms begin
- A weakened immune system

Conventional treatment for post herpetic neuralgia often includes medications specifically used for nerve pain, such as certain anticonvulsants or antidepressants used at pain-relieving doses, topical treatments like lidocaine patches or capsaicin cream, and sometimes other pain management approaches for more persistent or severe cases. Early antiviral treatment during the initial shingles episode can also reduce the risk and severity of post herpetic neuralgia.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wear loose, soft clothing over the affected area to reduce irritation from touch
- Apply cool compresses if they provide comfort, avoiding anything that irritates the skin
- Prioritise good sleep habits, as pain can disrupt sleep and poor sleep can worsen pain perception
- Gentle relaxation techniques, such as deep breathing or mindfulness, may help with coping
- Stay connected with support, as persistent pain can affect mood and daily life
- Discuss any new over-the-counter products with your doctor before applying them to sensitive skin

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Pain that is severe, worsening, or not responding to prescribed treatment
- New rash, blistering, redness, or signs of skin infection in the affected area
- Pain or rash near the eye, which can threaten vision and needs urgent specialist assessment
- Signs of low mood, anxiety, or difficulty coping with persistent pain
- Any new weakness, confusion, or other neurological symptoms

These situations warrant timely medical review to adjust pain management or investigate further, so contact your doctor without delay.
