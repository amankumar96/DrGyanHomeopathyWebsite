## What is Diabetes Type I?

Type 1 diabetes is a lifelong autoimmune condition in which the immune system attacks and destroys the insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. As a result, the body produces little or no insulin, the hormone needed to move glucose from the blood into cells for energy. Without insulin treatment, glucose builds up in the bloodstream, which can lead to serious short-term and long-term health problems. Type 1 diabetes most often develops in children, teenagers, and young adults, though it can occur at any age, and it requires insulin treatment for life.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of type 1 diabetes include:

- Increased thirst
- Frequent urination, including at night
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent tiredness or lack of energy
- Increased hunger
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of cuts or wounds
- Thrush or other recurrent infections

Symptoms can develop quite quickly, over days to weeks, particularly in children, and should always be checked by a doctor promptly.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Type 1 diabetes occurs when the immune system mistakenly destroys the insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. The exact trigger for this autoimmune process is not fully understood, but known factors include:

- A family history of type 1 diabetes or other autoimmune conditions
- Certain genetic markers
- Possible environmental triggers, such as certain viral infections, though this link is still being studied

Unlike type 2 diabetes, type 1 diabetes is not caused by diet, body weight, or lifestyle, and there is currently no known way to prevent it.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for type 1 diabetes centres on lifelong insulin therapy, delivered through injections or an insulin pump, combined with regular blood glucose monitoring, carbohydrate counting, and routine follow-up with a diabetes care team to manage the condition and reduce the risk of complications.

Insulin treatment is not optional and cannot be replaced by homeopathy or any other complementary approach. ## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Monitor blood glucose levels as advised by your diabetes care team
- Take insulin exactly as prescribed, and never skip or adjust doses without medical guidance
- Learn to recognise the signs of high and low blood glucose
- Carry a fast-acting source of sugar for treating low blood glucose episodes
- Eat regular, balanced meals and coordinate carbohydrate intake with insulin doses as advised
- Stay physically active, adjusting insulin or food intake around exercise as guided by your care team
- Attend regular check-ups, including eye, kidney, and foot screenings

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical care immediately if you or someone with type 1 diabetes experiences:

- Signs of diabetic ketoacidosis: nausea or vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity-smelling breath, rapid breathing, extreme tiredness, or confusion
- Very high blood glucose readings that do not come down with usual correction doses
- Signs of severe low blood glucose (hypoglycaemia): shakiness, sweating, confusion, slurred speech, difficulty concentrating, seizures, or loss of consciousness
- Persistent vomiting or inability to keep fluids down
- Unexplained drowsiness or difficulty waking

Diabetic ketoacidosis and severe hypoglycaemia are medical emergencies. If severe symptoms occur, call emergency services immediately rather than waiting or trying home remedies.
