## What is Lichen Planus?

Lichen planus is an inflammatory condition that is thought to occur when the immune system mistakenly attacks cells in the skin or mucous membranes, causing a distinctive rash of flat-topped, itchy, purplish bumps. It can also affect the inside of the mouth, where it typically appears as lacy white patches, as well as the nails, scalp, and genital skin. The skin form often resolves on its own within one to two years, sometimes leaving darker patches of skin behind, while involvement of the mouth or other mucous membranes can be more persistent and may require longer-term care.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of lichen planus include:

- Flat-topped, shiny, purplish or violet bumps, often on the wrists, forearms, lower legs, or lower back
- Itching, which can range from mild to intense
- Lacy white patches or sores inside the mouth, sometimes with a burning sensation, especially with spicy or acidic foods
- Thin white lines visible on the surface of the bumps (known as Wickham's striae)
- Thinning, ridging, or loss of nails if nails are affected
- Hair loss or scarring on the scalp in some cases
- Painful sores or white patches on genital skin in some individuals

The pattern and severity vary depending on which parts of the body are affected.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of lichen planus is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve the immune system reacting against the skin or mucous membrane cells. Known associations and risk factors include:

- Hepatitis C infection, which has been linked to some cases
- Certain medicines, including some used for blood pressure, heart conditions, and arthritis
- Allergens, such as certain metals or dental materials, particularly for mouth involvement
- Stress, which may trigger or worsen flares in some people
- A family history of lichen planus in rare cases

Lichen planus most commonly affects middle-aged adults and is not considered contagious.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for lichen planus commonly includes topical corticosteroids to reduce inflammation and itching, antihistamines for itch relief, and in more widespread or persistent cases, oral medications or light therapy. Mouth involvement may be managed with special mouth rinses or topical treatments, and regular dental or medical follow-up is often recommended, particularly for mucosal forms.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid scratching affected skin, as this can worsen irritation and lead to marks
- Use mild, fragrance-free soaps and moisturisers
- Avoid spicy, acidic, or very hot foods if you have mouth involvement, as they can trigger discomfort
- Maintain good oral hygiene and attend regular dental check-ups if your mouth is affected
- Try to manage stress, as it may worsen flares for some people
- Review your current medicines with your doctor if lichen planus started after starting a new medicine
- Protect affected skin from unnecessary trauma or injury, which can trigger new patches in some people

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Contact your doctor promptly if you notice:

- Painful mouth sores that interfere with eating, drinking, or speaking
- Sores or unusual changes in genital skin
- Signs of infection, such as increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or discharge from affected skin
- Rapid spreading of the rash or new widespread symptoms
- A persistent mouth or genital patch that changes in appearance, thickens, or does not heal, as any such change should be properly checked by a doctor

Ongoing monitoring by a doctor or dermatologist is generally recommended for lichen planus affecting the mouth or genital area, so do not hesitate to seek medical review if something feels different or concerning.
