## What is Lumbar Spondylitis?

Lumbar spondylitis, more accurately called lumbar spondylosis, refers to age-related degenerative changes affecting the discs, joints, and bones of the lower part of the spine. Over time, the discs between the vertebrae can lose height and flexibility, and small bony growths called osteophytes may develop at the joint edges. These changes are a common and often natural part of ageing, and many people with such changes on imaging have no symptoms at all. When symptoms do occur, they typically involve lower back pain and stiffness, which may be mild and intermittent or, less commonly, more persistent.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms associated with lumbar spondylosis include:

- Lower back pain, often worse after activity or at the end of the day
- Stiffness in the lower back, particularly noticeable in the morning or after sitting for long periods
- Reduced flexibility when bending or twisting the lower back
- Pain that may ease with gentle movement but worsen with prolonged standing or sitting
- Occasional pain radiating into the buttock or leg if a nearby nerve is affected
- Muscle tightness or spasm around the lower back

Symptoms vary considerably, and the degree of wear seen on a scan does not always match how much pain a person feels.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Lumbar spondylosis develops mainly due to the natural ageing process of the spine, but several factors can influence how early or severely it develops, including:

- Increasing age, as disc and joint wear becomes more common over time
- Previous back injuries or repetitive strain
- Occupations or activities involving heavy lifting, bending, or prolonged sitting
- Being overweight, which increases load on the lower spine
- Reduced core and back muscle strength
- Genetic factors affecting disc and joint health
- Smoking, which may affect disc nutrition and healing

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay as active as possible and avoid prolonged bed rest
- Practise good posture when sitting, standing, and lifting
- Use proper lifting technique, bending at the knees rather than the back
- Maintain a healthy body weight to reduce strain on the lower spine
- Incorporate gentle stretching and core-strengthening exercises, ideally under professional guidance
- Use supportive, comfortable seating, especially during long periods at a desk
- Apply heat or cold packs for symptomatic relief, as advised by a healthcare professional

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical attention if lower back pain is accompanied by:

- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in one or both legs
- Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the genitals or buttocks (saddle area), which can indicate a rare but serious nerve compression requiring emergency care
- Severe pain following a significant injury or fall
- Unexplained weight loss, fever, or night sweats alongside back pain
- Pain that is constant, worsening, and not relieved by rest or position changes

These features can indicate a more serious underlying problem and need prompt assessment rather than self-treatment.
