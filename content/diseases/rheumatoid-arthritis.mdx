## What is Rheumatoid Arthritis?

Rheumatoid arthritis is a long-term autoimmune condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the lining of the joints, causing inflammation, pain, and swelling. It most commonly affects the small joints of the hands, feet, and wrists, often on both sides of the body symmetrically, though it can involve other joints and, in some cases, other organs. Rheumatoid arthritis tends to follow a pattern of flares, when symptoms worsen, and periods of lower activity. Without adequate treatment, ongoing inflammation can lead to progressive joint damage, so early diagnosis and consistent treatment are important.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of rheumatoid arthritis include:

- Joint pain, swelling, and tenderness, typically affecting the same joints on both sides of the body
- Stiffness that is often worse in the morning and may last more than 30 minutes
- Warm, swollen joints, most commonly in the hands, wrists, and feet
- Fatigue, low-grade fever, and a general feeling of being unwell
- Reduced grip strength and difficulty with fine hand movements
- Firm lumps under the skin (rheumatoid nodules) in some people
- Over time, joint deformity if inflammation is not well controlled

## Causes & Risk Factors

Rheumatoid arthritis occurs when the immune system attacks the synovium, the lining of the membranes surrounding joints, leading to inflammation that can damage cartilage and bone over time. The exact trigger is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve a combination of genetic susceptibility and environmental factors.

Recognised risk factors include:

- Being female, as rheumatoid arthritis is more common in women
- A family history of rheumatoid arthritis
- Smoking, which is linked to both increased risk and more severe disease
- Age, as it most commonly begins between 40 and 60 years, though it can occur at any age
- Obesity, which may increase risk
- Certain genetic markers identified through blood testing

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for rheumatoid arthritis centres on disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs), sometimes combined with biologic therapies, to control the underlying immune-driven inflammation and protect joints from long-term damage. Anti-inflammatory medication and steroids may also be used to manage symptoms, particularly during flares, alongside regular monitoring by a rheumatologist.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often hoping it may support general well-being or comfort. Homeopathy is not positioned as a replacement for doctor-prescribed care, and DMARDs or other prescribed medication should never be stopped without medical advice. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay physically active with low-impact exercise such as swimming, cycling, or walking to help maintain joint mobility and muscle strength
- Maintain a healthy, balanced diet and a healthy body weight to reduce stress on joints
- Apply heat for stiffness and cold packs for acute swelling, as each may help at different times
- Pace daily activities and balance rest with movement during flares
- Avoid smoking, as it is linked to more severe disease and poorer treatment response
- Seek support for fatigue and mood changes, which are common with chronic inflammatory conditions
- Use assistive devices or occupational therapy advice if hand function is affected

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden, severe swelling in one or more joints, especially with fever or feeling unwell, which could indicate joint infection and needs urgent review
- A significant new flare that is not responding to your usual treatment plan
- Chest pain, breathlessness, or persistent cough, as rheumatoid arthritis can occasionally affect the lungs or heart
- Eye pain, redness, or vision changes
- Signs of infection, such as a wound that is not healing, especially if you are on immune-suppressing medication

Do not stop or change your DMARDs or other prescribed medication on your own; contact your rheumatology team promptly if symptoms worsen significantly.
