## What is Epiphora?

Epiphora is the medical term for excessive watering or tearing of the eye. It occurs either because the eye produces too many tears or because the normal drainage pathway, which carries tears from the eye into the nose, is partially or fully blocked. Epiphora can affect one or both eyes, can be temporary or persistent, and occurs across all age groups, including newborn infants whose tear ducts have not fully opened.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms associated with epiphora include:

- Tears overflowing onto the cheek, beyond normal moistening of the eye
- A constant damp or wet feeling around the eye
- Blurred vision due to excess tear film
- Redness or irritation of the skin below the eye from frequent wiping
- Sticky discharge or crusting, particularly if infection is present
- Sensitivity to light or a feeling of grittiness in some cases

## Causes & Risk Factors

Epiphora can result from either overproduction of tears or a drainage problem. Common causes include:

- Blocked or narrowed tear ducts, which may be present from birth in infants or develop with age in adults
- Irritation from allergies, dust, smoke, or foreign bodies in the eye
- Eyelid problems, such as the eyelid turning inward or outward
- Dry eye disease, which can paradoxically trigger reflex watering
- Conjunctivitis or other eye infections
- Certain medications and, rarely, nerve-related conditions affecting blinking or tear drainage

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep the eyes clean using a clean, damp cotton pad, wiping gently from the inner to outer corner
- Avoid rubbing the eyes, which can worsen irritation
- Identify and reduce exposure to known allergens, smoke, or dust where possible
- Use a humidifier if dry indoor air seems to worsen symptoms
- Protect the eyes with sunglasses in bright, windy, or dusty conditions
- For infants, gentle massage of the tear duct area, as shown by a doctor, is sometimes advised

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Thick, yellow or green discharge, significant redness, or swelling around the eye, which may indicate infection
- Pain in or around the eye, rather than simple irritation
- Sudden change in vision
- A lump, swelling, or tenderness near the inner corner of the eye or alongside the nose, which can suggest a tear sac infection
- Watery eye in a baby that persists beyond a few months, or is accompanied by significant swelling or discharge

These situations may need urgent assessment, so contact your doctor rather than relying on self-care alone.
