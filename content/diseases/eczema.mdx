## What is Eczema?

Eczema, also known as atopic eczema or atopic dermatitis, is a common condition that causes patches of skin to become dry, itchy, cracked, and sore. It affects people of all ages but often begins in childhood, and many children find it improves as they grow older, though some adults continue to experience flares throughout life. Eczema tends to follow a pattern of flare-ups, when symptoms worsen, and periods when the skin is calmer. It is a chronic condition and currently cannot be cured, but it can usually be managed well with ongoing care.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of eczema include:

- Dry, sensitive skin that can feel rough or scaly
- Intense itching, which may be worse at night
- Red, inflamed patches on lighter skin, or darker brown, purple, or grey patches on darker skin
- Cracked or weeping skin in more severe flares
- Thickened, leathery skin from repeated scratching over time
- Patches commonly appearing on the hands, inner elbows, backs of the knees, and on babies' faces and scalps

Symptoms vary considerably between individuals and can range from mild dryness to widespread, intensely itchy inflammation.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of eczema is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve a combination of a weakened skin barrier, an overactive immune response, and genetic factors. Eczema often runs in families alongside other "atopic" conditions such as asthma and hay fever.

Several factors are known to trigger or worsen flares, including:

- Irritants such as soaps, detergents, and some fabrics
- Environmental allergens such as pollen, dust mites, and pet dander
- Changes in temperature or humidity
- Skin infections
- Stress and hormonal changes
- Certain foods, in some individuals, particularly children

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Moisturise regularly with a fragrance-free emollient, even when skin looks clear
- Use mild, soap-free cleansers and lukewarm rather than hot water when bathing
- Pat skin dry gently rather than rubbing after washing
- Identify and avoid personal triggers such as certain fabrics or products where possible
- Keep fingernails short to reduce skin damage from scratching
- Try to manage stress, as it can worsen flares for some people
- Dress in soft, breathable fabrics and avoid overheating

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Skin that becomes blistered, crusted, weeping fluid, or develops pus-filled spots, which may indicate infection
- Sudden, widespread worsening of the rash, especially with fever or feeling generally unwell, which can suggest a serious viral infection called eczema herpeticum
- Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pain around a patch of skin
- Eczema affecting a large area of the body along with feeling unwell

These situations can require urgent medical care, so contact your doctor without delay.
