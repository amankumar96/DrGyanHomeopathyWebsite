## What is Myopia?

Myopia, also known as short-sightedness or nearsightedness, is a common refractive error of the eye in which distant objects appear blurred while close objects can usually be seen clearly. It occurs when the eyeball is slightly longer than normal, or the cornea is too curved, causing light to focus in front of the retina rather than directly on it. Myopia often begins in childhood or the teenage years and can progress gradually, particularly during periods of growth, before generally stabilising in early adulthood. It is one of the most common eye conditions worldwide and is usually corrected with glasses or contact lenses.

## Symptoms

Common signs and symptoms of myopia include:

- Blurred vision when looking at distant objects, such as road signs or a classroom board
- Clear vision for close-up tasks such as reading
- Squinting to try to see distant objects more clearly
- Eye strain or headaches, particularly after activities requiring distance vision
- In children, sitting very close to the television or holding books very close to the face
- Difficulty with night driving due to reduced clarity of distant lights and signs

## Causes & Risk Factors

Myopia develops due to the shape or length of the eyeball affecting how light is focused, influenced by a combination of genetic and environmental factors, including:

- A family history of myopia, which increases the likelihood of a child developing it
- Extended periods of close-up visual work, such as prolonged reading or screen use
- Reduced time spent outdoors, particularly during childhood, which has been associated with higher myopia rates
- Rapid eye growth during childhood and adolescence
- Certain ethnic backgrounds, which are associated with differing rates of myopia in population studies

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend regular eye examinations, especially for children, to monitor vision changes
- Encourage adequate time outdoors, particularly for children, as this is associated with a lower risk of myopia progression
- Take regular breaks during prolonged close-up work, such as reading or screen use, following the general guidance of looking at something distant every so often
- Ensure good lighting when reading or doing close-up tasks to reduce eye strain
- Maintain an appropriate viewing distance from screens and hold reading material at a comfortable distance
- Wear correctly prescribed glasses or contact lenses as advised by your eye care professional

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most changes in myopia develop gradually and can be managed through routine eye care, but you should seek urgent, same-day eye assessment if you notice:

- Sudden flashes of light in your vision
- New floaters, especially a sudden increase in their number
- A curtain-like shadow or dark area appearing in part of your vision
- A sudden, significant drop in vision

These symptoms can indicate a possible retinal detachment, a risk that is higher in people with high degrees of myopia, and need prompt evaluation by an eye specialist without delay.
