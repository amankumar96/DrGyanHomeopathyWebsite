## What is Erectile Dysfunction?

Erectile dysfunction (ED) refers to the persistent or recurrent difficulty in achieving or maintaining an erection firm enough for satisfactory sexual activity. Occasional difficulty is common and not usually a cause for concern, but when the problem is frequent or ongoing, it is considered a medical condition worth evaluating. ED becomes more common with increasing age, but it can affect men of any adult age and is often linked to broader physical or psychological health.

## Symptoms

Erectile dysfunction may present as:

- Difficulty achieving an erection sufficient for sexual activity
- Difficulty maintaining an erection throughout sexual activity
- Reduced firmness of erections compared to previously
- Reduced interest in sexual activity, which may occur alongside or separately from erectile difficulty
- Anxiety or distress specifically related to sexual performance

These symptoms can occur occasionally or consistently, and their pattern often provides useful clues to the underlying cause.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Erectile dysfunction can result from physical causes, psychological causes, or commonly a combination of both. Known contributing factors include:

- Cardiovascular conditions, such as narrowed or hardened blood vessels, high blood pressure, and heart disease
- Diabetes, which can affect both blood vessels and nerves
- Hormonal imbalances, including low testosterone
- Obesity and lack of physical activity
- Smoking and excessive alcohol use
- Certain medications, including some used for blood pressure and depression
- Psychological factors such as stress, anxiety, depression, and relationship difficulties
- Neurological conditions and prior pelvic surgery or injury

Conventional evaluation of erectile dysfunction typically starts with a review of general health, cardiovascular risk factors, blood tests, and medication history, since identifying and addressing an underlying cause is often central to treatment. Options may include lifestyle changes, treating underlying conditions such as diabetes or high blood pressure, prescribed medication, or counselling where psychological factors play a significant role.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain regular physical activity and a healthy body weight
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol intake
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, adequate rest, or counselling if needed
- Discuss any medications you take with your doctor, as some can contribute to erectile difficulty
- Communicate openly with your partner, as relationship factors can play a role
- Attend regular health check-ups, particularly for blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor promptly, rather than only addressing erectile dysfunction as a standalone issue, if you notice:

- New or persistent erectile difficulty, as this can be an early warning sign of cardiovascular disease or diabetes and warrants a general medical check-up
- Chest pain, breathlessness, or unusual fatigue alongside erectile difficulty, which could indicate a cardiac problem requiring urgent assessment
- Symptoms of high or uncontrolled blood sugar, such as excessive thirst, frequent urination, or unexplained weight loss
- Erections lasting more than four hours (priapism), which is a medical emergency requiring immediate care
- Signs of low mood, anxiety, or relationship distress that are significantly affecting quality of life

Do not delay seeking medical review, as addressing an underlying cause early can be important for your broader health, not only for sexual function.
