## What is Bronchitis?

Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchial tubes, the airways that carry air to and from the lungs. It is usually caused by an infection and commonly follows a cold or flu-like illness. Acute bronchitis is short-term and typically improves within about three weeks, often without needing specific treatment. Chronic bronchitis is a longer-term condition, generally associated with ongoing exposure to irritants such as cigarette smoke, and involves a persistent cough that lasts for extended periods, often recurring over several months each year.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of bronchitis include:

- A persistent cough, which may be dry or produce mucus that can be clear, white, yellowish, or green
- Chest discomfort or soreness, especially when coughing
- Wheezing or a feeling of tightness in the chest
- Shortness of breath, particularly with exertion
- Sore throat and a runny or blocked nose
- Mild fever, chills, and fatigue
- Headache and general body aches

## Causes & Risk Factors

Acute bronchitis is most often caused by viruses, the same ones responsible for colds and flu, and spreads through coughing, sneezing, and close contact with an infected person. Chronic bronchitis is usually caused by long-term exposure to irritants that damage the airways.

Risk factors include:

- Smoking or regular exposure to secondhand smoke, the leading cause of chronic bronchitis
- Exposure to air pollution, dust, or workplace chemical fumes
- Having a recent cold, flu, or other respiratory infection
- Weakened immunity, due to illness, certain medicines, or older age
- Underlying lung conditions, such as asthma

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Most cases of acute bronchitis resolve on their own with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter pain and fever relievers such as paracetamol. A humidifier or steam inhalation may ease symptoms, and inhaled medicines may be prescribed if wheezing is present. Antibiotics are not usually helpful for viral bronchitis but may be prescribed if a bacterial infection is suspected. Chronic bronchitis is managed with strategies that may include stopping smoking, inhalers, pulmonary rehabilitation, and other treatments prescribed by a doctor.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often in the hope of supporting recovery or general well-being. Homeopathy is not positioned as a replacement for doctor-prescribed care, especially for chronic bronchitis or any case involving breathing difficulty. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Rest and stay well hydrated to help the body recover
- Avoid smoking and secondhand smoke, which irritate the airways and slow recovery
- Use a humidifier or inhale steam to help loosen mucus and ease breathing
- Avoid exposure to dust, strong fumes, or air pollution where possible
- Cover your mouth when coughing and wash hands regularly to avoid spreading infection
- Get recommended vaccinations, such as flu and pneumonia vaccines, if advised by your doctor, especially if you have chronic lung conditions

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- A cough that lasts longer than three weeks without improving
- Coughing up blood or blood-streaked mucus
- Chest pain that is severe, persistent, or worsens with breathing
- High fever, chills, or feeling significantly unwell
- Underlying conditions such as heart disease, lung disease, or diabetes alongside bronchitis symptoms
- Being pregnant, aged over 65, or having a weakened immune system with bronchitis symptoms

Call emergency services immediately if you or someone else has severe difficulty breathing, bluish or grey discolouration of the lips or face, sudden confusion, or is struggling to stay awake, as these can be signs of a serious complication requiring urgent care.
