## What is Hailey-Hailey Disease?

Hailey-Hailey disease, also called familial benign chronic pemphigus, is a rare, inherited skin condition in which the cells of the outer skin layer do not stick together normally. This leads to recurrent, painful, raw, and sometimes blistered patches, most often in skin folds such as the neck, armpits, groin, and under the breasts. It is a lifelong condition that tends to flare and settle over time, often worsening with heat, sweating, and friction, and typically first appears in adulthood.

## Symptoms

Common features of Hailey-Hailey disease include:

- Red, raw, or moist patches, usually in skin folds (neck, underarms, groin, under the breasts)
- Small blisters that can break easily, leaving painful, weeping areas
- Cracking or fissuring of affected skin
- An unpleasant odour from affected areas, often linked to secondary infection
- Itching, burning, or soreness in affected patches
- Flares that tend to worsen in hot, humid weather or with sweating and friction, and settle somewhat in between

## Causes & Risk Factors

Hailey-Hailey disease is caused by an inherited change in a gene responsible for helping skin cells stick together properly. This gene change is usually inherited from a parent, though the condition can vary in severity even within the same family.

Factors that commonly trigger or worsen flares include:

- Heat and humidity
- Sweating
- Friction from tight clothing or skin rubbing on skin in folds
- Skin infections, including bacterial, fungal, or viral infections
- Stress, which some patients report as a trigger
- Sun exposure, in some individuals

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep skin folds as clean, dry, and cool as possible
- Wear loose, breathable clothing to reduce friction and sweating
- Use gentle, soap-free cleansers on affected areas
- Try to manage heat exposure, especially during hot or humid weather
- Treat early signs of a flare or infection promptly rather than waiting
- Use barrier creams or moisture-absorbing powders in skin folds if advised by your doctor
- Manage stress where possible, as some patients find it a trigger

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pus in affected skin, which may indicate infection
- A strong or worsening odour from affected areas
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside a skin flare
- Rapidly spreading or unusually severe raw, weeping patches
- Pain that is significantly worse than your usual flares
- A flare that is not responding to your usual treatment within the expected time

These signs can indicate secondary infection or a more significant flare needing prompt medical review.
