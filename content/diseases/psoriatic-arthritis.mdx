## What is Psoriatic Arthritis?

Psoriatic arthritis is a long-term inflammatory condition that affects some people who have psoriasis, a skin condition causing red, scaly patches. It causes inflammation in and around the joints, and can also affect tendons, ligaments, nails, and sometimes the spine. Not everyone with psoriasis develops psoriatic arthritis, and the severity varies widely between individuals, ranging from mild joint discomfort to more significant joint damage if left unmanaged. Early diagnosis and ongoing treatment are important to help protect joints from long-term damage.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of psoriatic arthritis include:

- Painful, swollen, and stiff joints, often in the fingers and toes
- "Sausage-like" swelling of entire fingers or toes (dactylitis)
- Stiffness that is typically worse in the morning or after rest
- Nail changes such as pitting, thickening, or separation from the nail bed
- Pain where tendons or ligaments attach to bone, such as the heel (enthesitis)
- Lower back or neck pain if the spine is affected
- Fatigue and, for many, accompanying patches of psoriasis on the skin

## Causes & Risk Factors

Psoriatic arthritis occurs when the immune system mistakenly attacks healthy joint and skin tissue, leading to inflammation. The exact trigger is not fully understood, but genetic and environmental factors both appear to play a role.

Recognised risk factors include:

- A personal history of psoriasis, particularly if severe or affecting the nails
- A family history of psoriasis or psoriatic arthritis
- Age, as it most commonly develops between 30 and 50 years, though it can occur at any age
- Obesity, which is linked to increased risk and severity
- Physical trauma or infection, which may trigger onset in some people

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for psoriatic arthritis typically includes anti-inflammatory medication for symptom relief, along with disease-modifying anti-rheumatic drugs (DMARDs) or biologic therapies aimed at controlling the underlying inflammation and protecting joints from long-term damage. Regular monitoring by a rheumatologist is usually part of ongoing care.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often hoping it may support general well-being or comfort. Homeopathy is not positioned as a replacement for doctor-prescribed care, and disease-modifying medication should never be stopped without medical advice. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay physically active with low-impact exercise such as swimming or walking to help maintain joint mobility
- Maintain a healthy body weight to reduce stress on weight-bearing joints
- Apply heat for stiffness and cold packs for acute swelling, as each may help at different times
- Pace activities and rest when joints are inflamed, without becoming completely inactive
- Protect skin from injury, as trauma can sometimes trigger psoriasis flares
- Seek support for fatigue and mood changes, which are common with chronic inflammatory conditions

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden, severe swelling in one or more joints, especially with fever or feeling unwell, which could indicate joint infection
- New or worsening back pain with stiffness that does not improve with movement
- Sudden loss of joint function or inability to bear weight on a joint
- Eye pain, redness, or vision changes, as psoriatic arthritis can be associated with eye inflammation
- Signs of a significant flare that is not responding to your usual treatment plan

Do not stop or change disease-modifying medication on your own; contact your rheumatology team promptly if symptoms worsen significantly.
