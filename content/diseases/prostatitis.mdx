## What is Prostatitis?

Prostatitis refers to inflammation of the prostate gland, a small gland below the bladder in men that contributes fluid to semen. It can appear suddenly as an acute bacterial infection, or develop as a long-term, often harder-to-explain condition sometimes called chronic prostatitis or chronic pelvic pain syndrome. Prostatitis can affect men of any age, though certain forms are more common in younger and middle-aged men. The acute infectious form is a medical urgency, while the chronic form tends to wax and wane over months or years.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of prostatitis can include:

- Pain or a burning sensation when passing urine
- Pain in the pelvis, lower back, groin, or area between the scrotum and rectum (perineum)
- A frequent or urgent need to urinate, sometimes with a weak flow
- Difficulty starting urination or fully emptying the bladder
- Pain during or after ejaculation, or discomfort during sex
- In acute bacterial prostatitis: fever, chills, body aches, and feeling generally unwell

Symptoms can vary significantly between the acute and chronic forms, and some men with chronic prostatitis have symptoms that fluctuate over time.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Acute bacterial prostatitis is usually caused by a bacterial infection, sometimes spreading from the urinary tract or related to a sexually transmitted infection. Chronic bacterial prostatitis involves recurring infection, while the most common form, chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome, often has no identifiable infection and its exact cause remains unclear.

Factors that may increase risk include:

- A recent urinary tract infection or catheter use
- Pelvic trauma or prior pelvic surgery
- Pelvic floor muscle dysfunction or tension
- Stress and anxiety, which some men report worsens symptoms
- Dehydration or prolonged sitting, in some cases
- A history of previous prostatitis episodes

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay well hydrated and avoid excessive caffeine or alcohol, which can irritate the bladder
- Try to urinate regularly rather than holding urine for long periods
- Use a cushion when sitting for long periods if perineal pressure worsens discomfort
- Gentle stretching or pelvic floor relaxation exercises may help some men with chronic symptoms
- Practise stress-reduction techniques, as tension may worsen symptoms for some men
- Avoid prolonged cycling or activities that put direct pressure on the perineum during flares

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention immediately if you experience:

- Fever, chills, or feeling very unwell alongside pelvic or urinary symptoms
- Inability to pass urine at all (urinary retention)
- Severe pelvic, perineal, or lower abdominal pain
- Blood in the urine or semen
- Confusion or a rapid decline in general health

These can indicate acute bacterial prostatitis or urinary retention, which are medical emergencies requiring prompt treatment, so do not delay in seeking care.
