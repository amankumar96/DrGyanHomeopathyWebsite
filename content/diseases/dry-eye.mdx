## What is Dry Eye?

Dry eye is a common condition that occurs when the eyes do not produce enough tears, or when the tears produced are of poor quality and evaporate too quickly. Tears are important for keeping the surface of the eye lubricated, nourished, and protected, so when this balance is disrupted, it can lead to ongoing irritation and discomfort. Dry eye can affect one or both eyes and ranges from a mild, occasional nuisance to a more persistent condition that affects daily comfort and vision.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of dry eye include:

- A stinging, burning, or gritty sensation in the eyes
- Redness of the eyes
- Sensitivity to light
- Blurred vision that may improve with blinking
- A feeling of something being in the eye
- Episodes of watery eyes, which can occur as the eye overcompensates
- Stringy mucus in or around the eyes
- Discomfort when wearing contact lenses
- Difficulty with activities like reading or screen use for extended periods

## Causes & Risk Factors

Dry eye can result from reduced tear production, increased tear evaporation, or an imbalance in the components of tears. Common causes and risk factors include:

- Increasing age, as tear production naturally declines over time
- Hormonal changes, including during menopause
- Prolonged screen use, which reduces blink frequency
- Environmental factors such as wind, dry air, air conditioning, or smoke
- Certain medications, including some antihistamines, antidepressants, and blood pressure medicines
- Contact lens wear
- Underlying conditions such as autoimmune disorders (for example, Sjögren's syndrome) or eyelid problems like blepharitis
- Previous eye surgery

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take regular breaks from screens and remember to blink fully and often
- Use a humidifier in dry or air-conditioned environments
- Avoid direct airflow from fans, heaters, or air conditioning into the eyes
- Stay well hydrated
- Consider wraparound sunglasses outdoors to reduce wind exposure
- Clean eyelids gently if advised, especially if blepharitis is present
- Remove eye make-up thoroughly before sleep
- Use preservative-free artificial tears as recommended by your eye care professional

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden, severe eye pain
- Sudden changes in vision, such as blurring that does not clear with blinking
- Significant redness accompanied by discharge, suggesting possible infection
- Sensation of something stuck in the eye that does not resolve with blinking or rinsing
- Symptoms that worsen despite regular use of lubricating drops
- Dry eye accompanied by joint pain, dry mouth, or other symptoms that may suggest an underlying autoimmune condition

These situations may indicate a more serious eye problem requiring prompt assessment, so contact your eye doctor without delay.
