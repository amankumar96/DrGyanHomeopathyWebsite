## What is Loss of Libido?

Loss of libido refers to a reduced interest or desire for sexual activity, compared to what feels normal for that person. It is a common and often sensitive experience that can affect people of any age or gender at some point in life. For many, it is temporary and linked to stress, tiredness, or a passing phase in a relationship. For others, it can be more persistent and may relate to underlying hormonal, psychological, physical, or relationship factors. There is no single "normal" level of sexual desire, and what matters most is whether a change feels troubling or out of character for the individual.

## Symptoms

People experiencing loss of libido may notice:

- A reduced interest in sexual activity or intimacy
- Fewer or no sexual thoughts or fantasies
- Reduced enjoyment of previously pleasurable intimate activity
- Avoidance of physical closeness with a partner
- Associated low mood, fatigue, or reduced self-confidence
- Strain or distance within a relationship as a result of the change

These experiences vary widely between individuals, and a change in desire does not automatically indicate an illness.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Loss of libido can arise from a combination of physical, psychological, and relational factors, including:

- Hormonal changes, such as those related to menopause, low testosterone, thyroid disorders, or pregnancy and postpartum recovery
- Psychological factors such as stress, anxiety, depression, or low self-esteem
- Relationship difficulties, including unresolved conflict or lack of emotional connection
- Side-effects of certain medications, including some antidepressants, blood pressure medicines, and hormonal treatments
- Chronic illness, chronic pain, or fatigue
- Excessive alcohol use or recreational drug use
- Poor sleep and high life stress

Because the causes are varied, identifying contributing factors often requires an open conversation with a doctor.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Prioritise regular, good-quality sleep where possible
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, exercise, or activities you enjoy
- Maintain open, non-judgemental communication with your partner about how you are feeling
- Limit excessive alcohol intake and avoid recreational drug use
- Stay physically active, as regular exercise can support mood and energy levels
- Be patient with yourself, as desire can naturally fluctuate with life circumstances

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While loss of libido is rarely an emergency on its own, you should arrange to see a doctor, without undue delay, if it is:

- Persistent and causing you distress or affecting your relationship
- Accompanied by other symptoms such as unexplained fatigue, significant weight change, menstrual irregularities, or mood changes suggestive of depression
- Possibly linked to a medication you are taking, so this can be reviewed rather than stopped suddenly on your own
- Associated with thoughts of self-harm or a significant decline in mental health, which needs prompt support

A change in sexual desire can sometimes be a sign of an underlying hormonal, psychological, or relationship issue, and discussing it openly with a doctor is a reasonable and sensible step rather than something to feel embarrassed about.
