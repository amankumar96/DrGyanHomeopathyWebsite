## What is Lichen Planopilaris?

Lichen planopilaris is an uncommon inflammatory condition that affects the hair follicles of the scalp, causing redness, scaling, and discomfort around affected follicles, which can progress to permanent, scarring hair loss if not managed. It is considered a form of scarring (cicatricial) alopecia and is thought to be related to lichen planus, a broader inflammatory skin condition. Because the inflammation can destroy hair follicles permanently, timely assessment and treatment are important to help limit further hair loss.

## Symptoms

Common features of lichen planopilaris include:

- Patchy hair loss on the scalp, often starting as small areas that may expand over time
- Redness, scaling, or small bumps around hair follicles at the edges of the affected area
- Itching, burning, or tenderness of the scalp
- Smooth, shiny patches of scalp where follicles have already been lost (scarring)
- Visible loss of the normal follicle openings in affected areas, which a dermatologist can identify
- Gradual progression if left untreated, though the pace varies between individuals

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of lichen planopilaris is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve the immune system mistakenly attacking hair follicles, leading to inflammation and eventual scarring. It is considered related to lichen planus, which involves a similar immune-related process affecting skin and mucous membranes.

Factors associated with lichen planopilaris include:

- A personal or family history of lichen planus or related autoimmune conditions
- Being middle-aged, with women affected more often than men
- The condition is not known to be caused by poor hygiene, diet, or an infection
- The precise trigger for the immune response in a given individual is often not identifiable

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for lichen planopilaris aims to reduce inflammation and halt further hair loss, as already-scarred follicles cannot be restored. This often involves topical or injected corticosteroids, and sometimes other anti-inflammatory or immune-modulating medications prescribed and monitored by a dermatologist. Regular follow-up is usually recommended to track disease activity, since it can flare after quiet periods.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this specialist treatment, rather than as a replacement for it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend scheduled dermatology follow-ups, even if symptoms seem to have settled
- Avoid harsh hair treatments, tight hairstyles, or excessive heat styling that may irritate the scalp
- Use gentle, fragrance-free shampoos and scalp care products
- Avoid scratching or picking at itchy or scaling areas
- Protect the scalp from excessive sun exposure
- Keep a record of any new patches or changes in hair loss to discuss with your dermatologist
- Manage stress where possible, as some patients report it affects flares

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a dermatologist promptly, and do not delay, if you notice:

- New or rapidly expanding patches of hair loss
- Increasing redness, scaling, burning, or tenderness of the scalp
- Signs of secondary skin infection, such as pus, warmth, or spreading redness
- Hair loss accompanied by lesions elsewhere, such as in the mouth, nails, or skin, which may suggest wider lichen planus involvement
- Any sudden worsening after a period of stability

Because the hair loss from this condition can become permanent, prompt medical assessment gives the best chance of limiting further damage, so it should never be left unmonitored or treated with self-care alone.
