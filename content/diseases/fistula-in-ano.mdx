## What is Fistula-in-ano?

Fistula-in-ano, commonly called an anal fistula, is an abnormal tunnel that develops between the inner lining of the anal canal and an opening on the skin near the anus. It most often forms after an anal abscess, a collection of pus caused by infection of a small gland inside the anal canal, has drained either on its own or through treatment. Instead of healing completely, a persistent channel can remain, allowing fluid, pus, or occasionally stool to leak out intermittently. It is a long-standing, recurring problem for many people until it is properly treated.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of an anal fistula include:

- Persistent or recurring discharge of pus, blood, or fluid near the anus
- Skin irritation, soreness, or itching around the anus
- Pain, which may worsen when sitting, moving the bowels, or coughing
- Swelling or a tender lump near the anus, which can suggest a related abscess
- Fever and feeling generally unwell during an active flare
- A visible or felt opening in the skin near the anus

Symptoms often come and go, with periods of swelling and discharge followed by temporary improvement.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Most anal fistulas develop after an infection in one of the small glands inside the anal canal leads to an abscess. When this abscess is drained, a tract sometimes remains connecting the gland to the skin surface.

Factors associated with a higher risk of developing a fistula include:

- A previous anal abscess
- Inflammatory bowel disease, particularly Crohn's disease
- Chronic diarrhoea
- Previous anal or rectal surgery
- Tuberculosis or certain other infections, in some cases
- Radiation treatment to the pelvic area

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep the area clean and gently dry after bathing or bowel movements
- Use warm sitz baths to soothe discomfort, if advised by your doctor
- Wear loose, breathable underwear to reduce irritation
- Eat a fibre-rich diet and stay well hydrated to keep stools soft
- Avoid straining during bowel movements
- Attend follow-up appointments to monitor healing after any procedure

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Increasing pain, swelling, redness, or warmth near the anus, which may suggest a new or worsening abscess
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell alongside anal symptoms
- Difficulty passing urine or stool
- Spreading redness or swelling extending beyond the anal area
- Heavy bleeding from the area

These situations can indicate a complication such as a spreading infection and need urgent medical care without delay.
