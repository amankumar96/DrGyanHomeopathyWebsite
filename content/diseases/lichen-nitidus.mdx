## What is Lichen Nitidus?

Lichen nitidus is an uncommon skin condition that causes the appearance of many tiny, shiny, flesh-coloured or slightly pink bumps, usually just a millimetre or two across. The bumps often appear on the arms, chest, abdomen, or genital area, and sometimes cluster together. It is generally a benign, self-limiting condition, meaning it usually resolves on its own over time, often within one to two years, without leaving lasting marks on the skin.

## Symptoms

Common features of lichen nitidus include:

- Numerous tiny, shiny, flat-topped bumps, usually flesh-coloured, pink, or slightly lighter than surrounding skin
- Bumps commonly found on the inner arms, chest, abdomen, or genital area, though they can appear elsewhere
- Little or no itching in most cases, although some people do experience mild itching
- Bumps that may cluster in lines, sometimes along scratch marks (a pattern called the Koebner phenomenon)
- Skin that otherwise looks and feels normal between the bumps

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of lichen nitidus is not well understood. It is not known to be caused by an infection, allergy, or specific food or environmental exposure, and it is not contagious. Some researchers consider it possibly related to lichen planus, another skin condition involving the immune system, although this link is not firmly established.

Points that may be relevant include:

- It can occur in people of any age, though it is more often reported in children and young adults
- There is no clearly identified dietary, environmental, or lifestyle trigger
- It is not thought to be hereditary in most cases, though rare familial clusters have been reported
- Skin trauma or scratching may sometimes trigger new bumps to appear along the scratched line

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid scratching or picking at the bumps, as this may trigger new ones along the scratch line
- Use gentle, fragrance-free skin care and moisturisers
- Keep an eye on the bumps and note any changes to share with your doctor
- Avoid harsh soaps or skin products that might irritate the area
- Protect the skin from excessive friction or trauma where the bumps are present
- Be patient, as the condition often fades gradually over months to a couple of years

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Lichen nitidus itself is rarely a medical emergency, but you should see a doctor for assessment, and promptly if:

- The bumps become painful, blistered, or start bleeding
- You notice signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pus
- The rash spreads very rapidly or looks markedly different from typical lichen nitidus
- You are unsure of the diagnosis, since several skin conditions can look similar and benefit from a dermatologist's assessment
- The bumps are accompanied by fever or feeling generally unwell

A doctor can confirm the diagnosis and rule out other skin conditions that may need different treatment.
