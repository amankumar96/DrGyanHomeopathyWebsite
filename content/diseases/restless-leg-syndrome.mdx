## What is Restless Leg Syndrome?

Restless leg syndrome is a condition of the nervous system that causes an overwhelming, often irresistible urge to move the legs, usually accompanied by uncomfortable sensations such as crawling, tingling, or aching. Symptoms typically occur or worsen during rest, particularly in the evening or at night, and are temporarily relieved by movement. This pattern can make it difficult to fall asleep or stay asleep, and over time it can affect daytime energy, mood, and concentration. Restless leg syndrome can occur on its own or alongside other conditions and tends to vary in severity from mild and occasional to frequent and disruptive.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of restless leg syndrome include:

- An urge to move the legs, often with uncomfortable sensations described as crawling, pulling, itching, or aching deep in the legs
- Symptoms that begin or worsen during periods of rest or inactivity, especially in the evening or at night
- Temporary relief with movement, such as walking, stretching, or shaking the legs
- Difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep due to symptoms
- Involuntary jerking movements of the legs during sleep, noticed by the person or a bed partner
- Daytime tiredness, irritability, or difficulty concentrating from disrupted sleep

## Causes & Risk Factors

In many cases, restless leg syndrome has no clearly identifiable single cause and may run in families (primary restless leg syndrome). It is thought to involve how the brain processes dopamine, a chemical messenger involved in muscle movement. In other cases, it occurs secondary to an underlying factor.

Recognised risk factors and associated conditions include:

- Iron deficiency, even without anaemia
- Pregnancy, particularly in the later stages
- Chronic kidney disease
- Peripheral neuropathy or other nerve conditions
- Certain medications, including some antidepressants and antihistamines
- A family history of restless leg syndrome
- Older age, although it can occur at any age

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain a regular sleep schedule and good sleep hygiene
- Engage in moderate regular exercise, though avoid intense exercise close to bedtime
- Limit caffeine, alcohol, and tobacco, especially in the evening
- Try leg stretches, massage, or warm baths before bed
- Keep a symptom diary to help identify personal triggers
- Discuss any medications you take with your doctor, as some can worsen symptoms

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While restless leg syndrome is not usually an emergency, you should see a doctor promptly if:

- Symptoms are severe, worsening rapidly, or significantly disrupting sleep and daily life
- You notice signs of significant anaemia, such as unusual paleness, severe fatigue, or breathlessness
- Symptoms appear alongside numbness, weakness, or other new neurological symptoms in the legs
- Symptoms begin suddenly after starting a new medication

A doctor can check for underlying causes such as iron deficiency or kidney problems and discuss appropriate management options.
