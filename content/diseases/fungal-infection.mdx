## What is a Fungal Infection?

Fungal skin infections are common conditions caused by microscopic fungi that live on the skin, hair, or nails. The most well-known type affecting the skin is ringworm (tinea), which despite its name is not caused by a worm but by a fungus that can create a ring-shaped rash. Other common fungal infections include athlete's foot (affecting the feet) and fungal nail infections. These infections are generally not serious but can be persistent, itchy, and contagious if not properly treated.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of a fungal skin infection include:

- A rash that may look red, pink, or darker than the surrounding skin, depending on skin tone
- Scaly, dry, or flaky patches of skin
- Itching or a burning sensation around the affected area
- A ring-shaped or circular pattern to the rash, which may spread outward
- Cracked, peeling skin, particularly between the toes in athlete's foot
- Thickened, discoloured, or brittle nails in fungal nail infections

## Causes & Risk Factors

Fungal skin infections are caused by fungi that thrive in warm, moist environments. They can spread through:

- Direct skin-to-skin contact with an infected person
- Contact with an infected animal
- Contaminated items such as towels, bedding, combs, or shared footwear
- Contact with infected soil

Risk factors that make infection more likely include sweating heavily, wearing tight or non-breathable clothing and footwear, having a weakened immune system, and not drying the skin thoroughly after washing or swimming.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep skin clean and thoroughly dry, especially in skin folds and between the toes
- Wear breathable, loose-fitting clothing and change out of sweaty clothes promptly
- Avoid sharing towels, combs, hairbrushes, or footwear with others
- Wash towels, bedding, and clothing regularly, especially during an active infection
- Wear appropriate footwear in communal areas such as swimming pools and gyms
- Have pets checked by a vet if you suspect they may be a source of infection

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- A fungal infection that does not improve after a full course of pharmacy antifungal treatment
- A rash on the scalp, which usually needs prescription antifungal tablets and shampoo rather than creams alone
- Signs of a weakened immune system alongside a spreading or worsening infection
- Increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or pus, which may suggest a secondary bacterial infection
- Fungal infections that keep recurring despite good hygiene and completed treatment courses

These situations usually require a doctor's assessment to confirm the diagnosis and arrange the right treatment.
