## What is Neurodermatitis?

Neurodermatitis, also known as lichen simplex chronicus, is a chronic skin condition characterised by thickened, leathery, intensely itchy patches of skin that develop from a repeated cycle of scratching and itching. It often begins with an initial trigger, such as an insect bite, dry skin, eczema, or localised irritation, but persists because scratching provides temporary relief while further damaging and thickening the skin, which in turn becomes itchier. This self-perpetuating scratch-itch cycle is central to the condition and to its management.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of neurodermatitis commonly include:

- One or more well-defined patches of thickened, leathery skin
- Intense itching, which may be worse at night or during periods of stress
- Skin that appears darker, lighter, or more pigmented than surrounding areas
- Dry, scaly, or rough texture over the affected patch
- Patches commonly found on the neck, wrists, ankles, scalp, or genital area
- Skin changes that worsen with continued scratching

Symptoms can persist for months or longer if the scratch-itch cycle is not addressed.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of neurodermatitis is not fully understood, but it is thought to begin with a localised skin irritation that triggers itching, which is then perpetuated by repeated scratching. Recognised associations and risk factors include:

- A history of eczema, psoriasis, or other skin conditions causing initial irritation
- Insect bites or localised skin injury
- Dry skin, which can increase itch sensitivity
- Stress, anxiety, or other emotional factors, which are commonly reported triggers
- Being in a particular area repeatedly exposed to friction, such as tight clothing or jewellery
- Nerve-related skin sensitivity in some individuals

Antihistamines may help with itching, particularly at night, and addressing underlying stress or anxiety, sometimes with counselling support, can be an important part of management.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Try to consciously avoid scratching or rubbing the affected area, even when it feels difficult
- Keep the area covered or use a dressing at night to reduce unconscious scratching during sleep
- Moisturise regularly with a fragrance-free emollient to support the skin barrier
- Keep fingernails short and smooth to minimise skin damage if scratching does occur
- Explore stress-reduction techniques, such as relaxation exercises, as stress can worsen itching
- Avoid tight clothing or jewellery that may rub against the affected patch

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While neurodermatitis itself is not usually an emergency, seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or pain in the patch
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside the skin changes
- Bleeding, deep cracking, or non-healing sores on the affected skin
- Itching so severe that it significantly disrupts sleep or daily functioning despite self-care measures

These situations may need medical review and treatment, so it is best to consult your doctor rather than continuing with self-care alone.
