## What is Hives (Urticaria)?

Hives, medically known as urticaria, is a common skin reaction that causes raised, itchy bumps or patches to appear suddenly on the skin. These welts can vary widely in size and shape and may appear on any part of the body. Individual welts typically fade within 24 hours, though new ones can continue to appear as others resolve. Hives are classified as acute if they last less than six weeks, and chronic if they persist for six weeks or longer. Most cases of acute hives resolve on their own or with simple treatment, but recurring or persistent hives may need further medical evaluation.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of hives include:

- Raised, red, pink, or skin-coloured bumps or patches (welts)
- Intense itching, sometimes with a stinging or burning sensation
- Welts that vary in size and shape and can appear and disappear within hours
- Patches that may merge to form larger areas of swelling
- Swelling of deeper tissue, known as angioedema, which can affect the eyes, lips, or other areas

## Causes & Risk Factors

Hives occur when histamine and other chemicals are released in the skin, causing fluid to leak from small blood vessels and form swollen welts. Common triggers include:

- Certain foods, such as nuts, shellfish, or eggs
- Medicines, including some antibiotics and pain relievers
- Insect bites or stings
- Infections, including viral illnesses
- Physical triggers such as heat, cold, pressure, or exercise
- Latex, certain plants, or chemical exposure
- In some cases, no clear trigger can be identified (spontaneous hives)

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Try to identify and avoid personal triggers, such as specific foods, medicines, or temperature extremes
- Wear loose, breathable clothing to reduce skin irritation
- Avoid hot showers or baths, which can sometimes worsen itching
- Keep a diary of flares and possible triggers to share with your doctor
- Apply a cool compress to itchy areas for temporary relief
- Manage stress where possible, as it is commonly reported to worsen symptoms

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Call emergency services immediately if hives occur together with:

- Swelling of the lips, mouth, tongue, or throat
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or a feeling of throat tightness
- Difficulty swallowing
- Blue, grey, or pale discolouration of the skin or lips
- Severe dizziness, confusion, drowsiness, or fainting

These can be signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) and require emergency treatment without delay. Also contact your doctor or NHS-equivalent helpline if hives last longer than a couple of days, keep returning, or spread significantly, even without these severe symptoms.
