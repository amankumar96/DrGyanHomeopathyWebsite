## What is Neurofibromatosis?

Neurofibromatosis is a group of genetic conditions that cause tumours, called neurofibromas, to grow along nerves in the body. These growths are mostly benign (non-cancerous) and can develop on or under the skin, or in some cases deeper within the body. There are different types, with neurofibromatosis type 1 being the most common. The condition is lifelong and can vary greatly in severity, even within the same family, ranging from mild skin changes to more involved nerve or bone changes requiring coordinated specialist care.

## Symptoms

Features of neurofibromatosis can vary widely but may include:

- Flat, light brown patches on the skin known as "café-au-lait" spots
- Soft, round growths under or on the skin (neurofibromas), which may increase in number over time
- Clusters of small pigmented spots, particularly in skin folds such as the armpit or groin
- Tiny, harmless growths in the coloured part of the eye (Lisch nodules)
- Larger, soft growths involving a wider area of tissue in some cases (plexiform neurofibromas)
- In some individuals, bone changes, learning differences, or vision changes, depending on the type and areas affected

## Causes & Risk Factors

Neurofibromatosis is caused by a change (mutation) in a specific gene that normally helps regulate cell growth. In many cases, it is inherited from a parent who also has the condition, following a pattern where each child of an affected parent has roughly a fifty percent chance of inheriting it. In a significant number of cases, it arises from a new genetic change with no previous family history. It is not related to anything during pregnancy, diet, or environmental exposure, and cannot be prevented.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep up with scheduled specialist review appointments, even when feeling well
- Monitor the skin and note any new or changing growths to discuss at follow-up
- Protect the skin from excessive sun exposure and friction over areas with growths
- Seek genetic counselling if planning a family, to understand inheritance patterns
- Support emotional well-being, as visible skin changes can affect confidence, especially in children and teenagers
- Maintain a general healthy lifestyle with balanced nutrition and regular activity as tolerated

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Regular specialist monitoring is important throughout life with neurofibromatosis because, though uncommon, some growths can change over time. See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- A neurofibroma that grows rapidly, becomes painful, or changes in texture or appearance
- New weakness, numbness, or loss of function near a growth
- Sudden vision changes or new neurological symptoms
- Any new lump that causes concern, even if the person has had stable neurofibromas for years

Prompt assessment helps ensure any change is reviewed early as part of ongoing specialist care.
