## What is Nephrotic Syndrome?

Nephrotic syndrome is not a single disease but a pattern of symptoms that occurs when the tiny filtering units of the kidneys, called glomeruli, become damaged and allow excessive amounts of protein to leak from the blood into the urine. This protein loss leads to low blood protein levels, swelling (oedema), and often raised cholesterol. It can occur in both children and adults and may result from several underlying kidney conditions. Depending on the cause, nephrotic syndrome can often be managed effectively with treatment, although some forms require long-term monitoring.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of nephrotic syndrome include:

- Swelling, particularly around the eyes, ankles, legs, and abdomen
- Frothy or foamy urine, caused by high protein content
- Significant weight gain due to fluid retention
- Fatigue and a general feeling of being unwell
- Loss of appetite
- Increased susceptibility to infections

Symptoms can develop gradually or appear relatively suddenly, and their severity varies depending on the underlying cause.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Nephrotic syndrome results from damage to the kidney's filtering units, which can occur due to several underlying conditions, including:

- Minimal change disease, a common cause particularly in children
- Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a condition causing scarring in parts of the kidney filters
- Membranous nephropathy, more common in adults
- Diabetes, which can damage the kidneys over time (diabetic nephropathy)
- Autoimmune conditions, such as lupus
- Certain infections or medications, in some cases

Risk factors include having diabetes, certain autoimmune conditions, or a family history of kidney disease.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for nephrotic syndrome depends on the underlying cause but often includes medications such as corticosteroids, particularly for minimal change disease, along with other immune-modulating medications for some causes. Additional treatment often addresses swelling with diuretics, blood pressure control, salt restriction, and sometimes blood-thinning medication to reduce clot risk, which is increased in nephrotic syndrome.

Some patients explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often hoping to support general well-being. Homeopathy should never replace prescribed medication or regular monitoring of kidney function. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Follow any advice on salt and fluid restriction given by your doctor or dietitian to help manage swelling
- Take prescribed medications consistently and attend regular follow-up appointments and blood or urine tests
- Monitor for signs of swelling or weight changes and report significant changes to your doctor
- Take sensible precautions against infection, as nephrotic syndrome can increase susceptibility
- Maintain a generally balanced diet as advised, and discuss any protein intake changes with your care team
- Stay as physically active as your doctor advises, balancing activity with periods of rest if unwell

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Rapidly worsening swelling, particularly affecting the face, abdomen, or difficulty breathing
- Signs of infection, such as fever, chills, or redness and warmth of the skin
- Severe abdominal pain, which can occasionally indicate a complication
- Reduced urine output or signs of significant dehydration
- Sudden leg swelling, pain, or breathlessness, which could suggest a blood clot, a recognised complication of nephrotic syndrome
- Severe fatigue, confusion, or feeling very unwell

These can indicate serious complications, and prompt medical review is important rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
