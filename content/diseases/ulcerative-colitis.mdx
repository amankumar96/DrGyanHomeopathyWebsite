## What is Ulcerative Colitis?

Ulcerative colitis is a long-term condition in which the lining of the colon (large intestine) and rectum becomes inflamed, leading to ulcers and ongoing digestive symptoms. It is one of the two main types of inflammatory bowel disease, alongside Crohn's disease. The condition typically follows a pattern of flare-ups, when symptoms are active, and periods of remission, when symptoms settle. While there is currently no cure, ongoing medical treatment can help many people achieve and maintain remission.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of ulcerative colitis include:

- Recurring diarrhoea, which may contain blood or mucus
- Abdominal pain and cramping, often relieved somewhat by passing stool
- An urgent need to pass stool, sometimes with little warning
- Fatigue and a general feeling of being unwell
- Unintended weight loss during flares
- Loss of appetite
- In some people, symptoms outside the gut, such as joint pain, skin changes, or eye inflammation

Symptoms can range from mild to severe and tend to come and go over time.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of ulcerative colitis is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve an abnormal immune response affecting the bowel lining, influenced by genetic and environmental factors. Known associations include:

- A family history of inflammatory bowel disease
- Genetic factors that may increase susceptibility
- An overactive immune response, possibly triggered by gut bacteria or other environmental factors
- Age, as it most commonly starts between the teenage years and the thirties, though it can begin at any age
- Ethnic background, with some populations showing higher reported rates

Unlike some digestive conditions, diet and stress are not considered root causes, though they may influence symptom flares in some individuals.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for ulcerative colitis aims to reduce inflammation, control symptoms, and maintain remission. This usually involves anti-inflammatory medicines such as aminosalicylates, corticosteroids for flares, and immunosuppressant or biologic therapies for more persistent or severe disease. Regular monitoring, including periodic colonoscopy, is often part of long-term care, and surgery may be considered in some cases.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep a symptom and food diary to help identify personal triggers, in discussion with your doctor or dietitian
- Stay well hydrated, particularly during flares with diarrhoea
- Eat small, regular meals and consider softer, easily digestible foods during active symptoms
- Try to manage stress through relaxation techniques, gentle exercise, or support groups, as stress can affect symptom perception
- Avoid non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs unless advised by your doctor, as they may worsen symptoms
- Attend all scheduled monitoring appointments, including colonoscopy surveillance as recommended
- Do not stop or adjust prescribed medicines without first speaking to your doctor

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical attention if you experience:

- Rectal bleeding that is heavy, persistent, or increasing
- Severe abdominal pain or a swollen, tender abdomen
- High fever, especially with worsening bowel symptoms
- Signs of severe dehydration, such as reduced urination, dizziness, or marked weakness
- Rapid heartbeat or feeling faint
- A significant, sudden worsening of usual symptoms during a flare

These signs can indicate a serious flare or complication of ulcerative colitis and require prompt medical review rather than self-management alone.
