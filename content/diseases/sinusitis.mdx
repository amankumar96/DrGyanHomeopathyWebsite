## What is Sinusitis?

Sinusitis is swelling and inflammation of the lining of the sinuses, the air-filled spaces behind the forehead, cheekbones, and around the eyes and nose. It is usually caused by an infection, most often following a common cold, though allergies and other factors can also trigger it. When the sinus lining swells, it can block the normal drainage of mucus, leading to congestion, pressure, and discomfort in the face and head.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of sinusitis include:

- A blocked or stuffy nose that makes breathing through the nose difficult
- Pain, tenderness, or swelling around the eyes, cheeks, nose, or forehead
- Thick, discoloured nasal discharge
- Mucus running down the back of the throat (post-nasal drip)
- Reduced sense of smell and taste
- Headache, earache, or aching in the upper teeth
- Cough, sore throat, and bad breath
- Fever and tiredness, more common in acute sinusitis

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes and contributing factors include:

- A preceding viral infection, such as the common cold or flu, is the most frequent trigger of acute sinusitis
- Uncontrolled allergic rhinitis (allergies), which causes swelling of the nasal lining
- Nasal polyps or structural issues inside the nose that block normal drainage
- Ongoing infection or persistent inflammation of the sinus lining, more relevant to chronic sinusitis
- Exposure to irritants such as smoke or pollution

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for sinusitis typically starts with self-care such as rest, hydration, and pain relief, since most cases are viral and resolve on their own within about four weeks. If symptoms persist or are linked to allergies, a doctor may recommend steroid nasal sprays or antihistamines. Antibiotics are rarely needed, as sinusitis is usually viral, but may be considered in specific bacterial or severe cases. Chronic sinusitis that does not respond to medical treatment may need specialist referral and, occasionally, surgery.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Rest and stay well hydrated to help thin mucus and support recovery
- Use steam inhalation or warm compresses over the face to ease congestion and pressure
- Try saline (salt water) nasal rinses to help clear nasal passages
- Avoid known allergy triggers where possible, such as dust or pollen
- Use over-the-counter pain relievers as needed for discomfort, following label guidance
- Avoid smoking and exposure to smoke or strong irritants
- Sleep with your head slightly elevated to help ease nighttime congestion

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Contact your doctor if sinusitis symptoms last beyond three weeks, keep recurring, or do not improve despite self-care and over-the-counter painkillers. Seek urgent or emergency medical attention if you experience:

- Severe or worsening facial pain or swelling, especially around one eye
- High fever that does not settle, or feeling generally very unwell
- Vision changes, double vision, or swelling around the eyes affecting sight
- Confusion, severe headache, neck stiffness, or difficulty staying alert
- Weakened immunity (for example, due to other illness or treatment) alongside sinus symptoms

These can indicate a more serious complication requiring prompt medical assessment.
