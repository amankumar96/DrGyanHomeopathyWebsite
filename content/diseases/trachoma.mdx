## What is Trachoma?

Trachoma is an eye infection caused by the bacterium Chlamydia trachomatis. It is recognised by the World Health Organization as a neglected tropical disease and remains one of the leading infectious causes of blindness globally. The infection typically begins in childhood with repeated episodes of conjunctivitis-like inflammation. Over years, repeated infections can scar the inside of the eyelid, eventually causing the eyelashes to turn inward and rub against the cornea, a painful condition that can permanently damage vision if untreated.

## Symptoms

Symptoms often develop gradually and may include:

- Redness, irritation, and swelling of the eyelids
- Discharge from the eyes, often with mucus or pus
- Sensitivity to light
- A feeling of grittiness or foreign body sensation in the eye
- In advanced stages, inward-turning eyelashes (trichiasis) that scratch the eye surface
- Clouding of the cornea and progressive vision loss in long-standing, untreated cases

Early infections, especially in children, may cause only mild symptoms, which is one reason repeated or chronic exposure can go unnoticed until scarring has begun.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Trachoma is caused by particular strains of the bacterium Chlamydia trachomatis. It spreads through:

- Direct contact with eye or nasal discharge from an infected person
- Contaminated hands, towels, or cloths
- Flies that have been in contact with infected secretions

Risk factors include living in crowded households, limited access to clean water and sanitation, and residing in regions where the infection is still endemic. Children are frequently the main reservoir of infection within affected communities, and women are at higher risk of the blinding complications, often due to closer and more frequent contact with infected children.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment of trachoma centres on antibiotics (commonly a single dose of azithromycin) to clear the infection, along with the WHO-recommended "SAFE" strategy: Surgery for advanced inward-turning eyelashes, Antibiotics, Facial cleanliness, and Environmental improvement to reduce transmission. Surgery may be needed to correct eyelid deformity and prevent further corneal damage in advanced cases.

Homeopathy has no established role in treating the underlying infection and should not be relied upon in place of these measures. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and raise any interest in complementary approaches with your treating doctor or ophthalmologist first.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wash your face and hands regularly with clean water, especially children's faces
- Avoid sharing towels, cloths, or pillows with others, particularly during an active infection
- Improve access to clean water and proper waste disposal at home where possible
- Reduce fly exposure around the home and face
- Attend follow-up appointments to monitor for scarring or eyelid changes
- Encourage good hygiene habits in children in affected communities

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor or eye specialist promptly, without delay, if you notice:

- Persistent redness, discharge, or irritation of the eyes, especially in children or in areas where trachoma is known to occur
- Eyelashes turning inward or a sensation of something scratching the eye
- Increasing sensitivity to light or pain in the eye
- Any clouding of the cornea or noticeable decline in vision

Because trachoma is a recognised cause of preventable blindness, any suggestive eye symptoms need prompt medical or ophthalmological assessment rather than delay with complementary approaches alone.
