## What is Hidradenitis Suppurativa?

Hidradenitis suppurativa is a long-term skin condition that causes painful, boil-like lumps to develop, most often in areas where skin rubs together, such as the armpits, groin, under the breasts, and buttocks. These lumps can break open, leak fluid, and heal slowly, sometimes leading to scarring and tunnels forming under the skin (sinus tracts) over time with repeated flares. It usually begins after puberty and tends to follow a pattern of flares and remission. While not currently curable, hidradenitis suppurativa can often be managed with a combination of lifestyle measures and medical treatment, and severity varies greatly between individuals.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of hidradenitis suppurativa include:

- Painful, pea-sized to larger lumps under the skin, usually in the armpits, groin, buttocks, or under the breasts
- Lumps that may fill with fluid, break open, and leak pus or blood-stained discharge
- A persistent or recurring pattern of lumps in the same general areas
- Blackheads that often appear in affected areas
- Thickened, scarred skin after repeated episodes in the same spot
- Tunnels forming under the skin (sinus tracts) connecting multiple lumps in more advanced or longstanding cases
- Odour from leaking lumps, which can be distressing for some patients

Symptoms range from a few occasional lumps to extensive, interconnected areas of inflammation and scarring in more severe cases.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of hidradenitis suppurativa is not fully understood, but it is believed to involve blockage and inflammation of hair follicles, with an overactive immune response contributing to the ongoing cycle of flares. It is not caused by poor hygiene or infection, although secondary bacterial infection can occur in existing lumps.

Known risk factors include:

- A family history of hidradenitis suppurativa
- Smoking, which is strongly linked to both onset and severity
- Being overweight, which can increase friction and worsen flares
- Hormonal factors, as it often starts after puberty and can fluctuate with the menstrual cycle
- Friction from tight clothing or skin rubbing against skin

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- If you smoke, speak with your doctor about support to quit, as smoking is strongly linked to severity
- Maintain a healthy weight where possible, as this can reduce friction and flare frequency
- Wear loose, breathable clothing to reduce friction in affected areas
- Keep the skin clean with a gentle, non-irritating wash, without over-scrubbing
- Avoid shaving or waxing directly over active lumps
- Apply warm compresses to painful lumps, as this may offer some comfort
- Consider joining a support group, as living with a visible, recurring condition can affect emotional well-being

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Signs of spreading infection, such as fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell alongside the skin lumps
- Rapidly increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or pain around a lump
- Large areas of skin breakdown or extensive discharge
- Severe pain that is not manageable with simple measures
- New tunnels or connections forming under the skin between lumps
- Symptoms significantly affecting your ability to move, work, or carry out daily activities

A dermatologist should also be consulted for an initial diagnosis and for guidance on long-term management, as early specialist input can help reduce scarring and improve quality of life.
