## What is Varicose Veins?

Varicose veins are swollen, twisted veins, usually appearing dark purple or blue, most commonly found on the legs and feet. They develop when the small valves inside veins that normally keep blood flowing towards the heart become weakened or damaged, allowing blood to pool and the vein to stretch and bulge. Varicose veins are common, particularly with increasing age, and while they are often a cosmetic concern, some people experience discomfort or other symptoms alongside them.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms associated with varicose veins include:

- Visible, bulging, twisted veins, usually blue or dark purple, often on the legs
- Aching, heavy, or uncomfortable legs, especially after standing for long periods
- Swelling in the feet and ankles
- Burning, throbbing, or muscle cramping in the lower legs
- Itching around the affected vein
- Skin discolouration or changes near long-standing varicose veins

Symptoms can range from a purely cosmetic appearance to ongoing discomfort that affects daily activities.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Varicose veins occur when vein walls weaken and valves fail to work properly, allowing blood to flow backward and pool. The exact reason this happens is not always clear, but several factors increase the likelihood, including:

- Increasing age
- Family history of varicose veins
- Being female, partly linked to hormonal factors
- Pregnancy, due to increased blood volume and hormonal changes
- Prolonged standing or sitting, which can reduce circulation efficiency
- Obesity, which adds pressure on the veins
- A previous history of blood clots in the legs

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep moving and avoid standing or sitting still for long periods
- Elevate your legs above heart level when resting, where possible
- Wear properly fitted compression stockings if recommended by a doctor
- Maintain a healthy body weight to reduce pressure on leg veins
- Stay physically active with regular walking or low-impact exercise
- Avoid tight clothing around the waist, groin, and legs
- Moisturise skin over varicose veins to help prevent dryness and irritation

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- A vein that suddenly becomes very painful, hot, swollen, or hard, which may suggest a blood clot (thrombophlebitis)
- Bleeding from a varicose vein
- Skin ulcers, open sores, or non-healing wounds near a varicose vein
- Sudden swelling of one leg, especially with pain, warmth, or redness
- Skin that becomes increasingly discoloured, hardened, or breaks down over a varicose vein

These situations need timely medical review rather than waiting for symptoms to settle on their own.
