## What is Gall Bladder Stones?

Gall bladder stones, commonly called gallstones, are hard deposits that form inside the gall bladder, a small organ beneath the liver that stores bile used to help digest fats. Stones form when substances in bile, such as cholesterol or bilirubin, become concentrated and crystallise. Gallstones can be as small as a grain of sand or as large as a golf ball, and a person may have one or many. Many people with gallstones never experience symptoms, but for others, stones can cause pain or block the flow of bile, leading to complications.

## Symptoms

Many gallstones cause no symptoms at all. When symptoms do occur, they can include:

- Sudden, intense pain in the upper right or central abdomen, often after a fatty meal
- Pain that may spread to the back or right shoulder blade
- Nausea or vomiting
- Bloating or indigestion
- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice), if a stone blocks a bile duct
- Fever, if infection develops

Pain episodes, sometimes called biliary colic, can last from a few minutes to several hours.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Gallstones form when bile contains too much cholesterol, too much bilirubin, or not enough bile salts, or when the gall bladder does not empty properly. The exact combination of factors varies between individuals.

Recognised risk factors include:

- Being female, particularly with a history of pregnancy
- Being overweight or obese
- Age over 40
- A family history of gallstones
- Rapid weight loss or prolonged fasting
- Diabetes and certain other metabolic conditions
- Certain medicines, including some hormone treatments

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment depends on whether gallstones are causing symptoms. Silent, symptom-free gallstones are often simply monitored. When stones cause recurring pain or complications, treatment usually involves surgical removal of the gall bladder (cholecystectomy), and in some situations, a procedure to clear a blocked bile duct. Pain relief and, if needed, antibiotics may be used to manage acute episodes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Eat regular, balanced meals and avoid very fatty or fried foods, particularly if they trigger discomfort
- Maintain a steady, healthy weight and avoid rapid weight loss or crash dieting
- Stay well hydrated
- Include fibre-rich foods such as vegetables, fruits, and whole grains in your diet
- Keep physically active as part of general health
- Discuss any recurring digestive discomfort with your doctor rather than ignoring it

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention immediately if you experience:

- Severe or persistent abdominal pain that does not settle within a few hours
- Fever or chills along with abdominal pain
- Yellowing of the skin or eyes
- Dark urine or pale, clay-coloured stools
- Persistent vomiting, or pain spreading to the back with nausea

These can be signs of complications such as an infected gall bladder (cholecystitis), a blocked bile duct, or inflammation of the pancreas (pancreatitis), all of which need urgent medical evaluation.
