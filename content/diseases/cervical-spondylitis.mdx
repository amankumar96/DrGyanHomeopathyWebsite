## What is Cervical Spondylitis?

Cervical spondylitis, more accurately called cervical spondylosis, refers to age-related wear-and-tear changes affecting the bones, discs, and joints of the neck (cervical spine). As people age, the discs between the vertebrae can lose height and elasticity, and small bony growths called osteophytes may develop. These changes are extremely common and often do not cause symptoms, but in some people they lead to neck pain, stiffness, or pressure on nearby nerves.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of cervical spondylosis include:

- Neck pain and stiffness, often worse after periods of inactivity such as first thing in the morning
- Pain that may radiate to the shoulders, upper back, or arms
- A grinding or popping sensation when moving the neck
- Headaches that begin at the back of the head and neck
- Tingling, numbness, or weakness in the arms or hands if a nerve is affected
- In more advanced cases, difficulty with balance or coordination if the spinal cord is involved

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cervical spondylosis results from the natural ageing process of the spine, but several factors can contribute to or accelerate these changes, including:

- Increasing age, as wear-related changes become more common over time
- Previous neck injuries
- Occupations or activities involving repetitive neck movements or heavy lifting
- Prolonged poor posture, including extended periods looking down at screens
- Smoking, which has been linked to increased disc degeneration
- A family history of similar spine changes

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain good posture, especially when using computers or phones for long periods
- Take regular breaks to move and stretch the neck during desk work
- Use a supportive pillow that keeps the neck in a neutral position while sleeping
- Stay physically active, including gentle neck and shoulder exercises as advised by a physiotherapist
- Avoid carrying heavy bags on one shoulder for long periods
- Apply warmth to the neck to help ease muscle stiffness
- Avoid smoking, as it may contribute to disc degeneration

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- New or worsening weakness, numbness, or tingling in the arms, hands, or legs
- Loss of coordination, unsteady walking, or difficulty with balance
- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Severe neck pain following an injury or fall
- Symptoms that progress rapidly over days

These can be signs of significant nerve or spinal cord involvement and require urgent medical evaluation.
