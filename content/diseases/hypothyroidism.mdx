## What is Hypothyroidism?

Hypothyroidism is a common condition in which the thyroid gland, located at the front of the neck, does not produce enough thyroid hormone to meet the body's needs. Thyroid hormone helps regulate metabolism, so when levels are too low, many of the body's processes slow down. This can affect energy levels, weight, mood, and many other bodily functions. The most common cause is an autoimmune condition called Hashimoto's thyroiditis, though it can also result from previous thyroid surgery, radioactive iodine treatment, certain medicines, or iodine deficiency. Hypothyroidism is generally well managed with daily thyroid hormone replacement medicine, usually needed for life.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of hypothyroidism include:

- Persistent tiredness and low energy
- Unexplained weight gain
- Feeling unusually cold
- Dry skin and hair, and hair thinning
- Constipation
- Low mood or depression
- Muscle aches and weakness
- Slowed thinking or poor concentration ("brain fog")
- A slower heart rate
- Irregular or heavy menstrual periods in women
- Swelling at the front of the neck (goitre), in some cases
- Puffiness around the face

Symptoms often develop slowly over months or years, which can make hypothyroidism easy to overlook or mistake for normal ageing or tiredness.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The most common cause of hypothyroidism worldwide is Hashimoto's thyroiditis, an autoimmune condition in which the immune system gradually damages the thyroid gland, reducing its ability to produce hormone. Other causes include:

- Previous thyroid surgery or radioactive iodine treatment
- Certain medicines, such as some used for mood disorders or heart conditions
- Iodine deficiency, in regions where dietary iodine intake is low
- Congenital thyroid problems present from birth
- Radiation treatment to the neck area

Risk factors include being female, increasing age, having a family history of thyroid or autoimmune conditions, and having other autoimmune disorders.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for hypothyroidism usually involves daily thyroid hormone replacement medicine (commonly levothyroxine), taken consistently and adjusted based on regular blood tests measuring thyroid hormone levels. Most people need this treatment for the rest of their lives, with periodic monitoring to ensure the dose remains correct.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this prescribed treatment, often in the hope of supporting general well-being. Homeopathy should never be used as a replacement for thyroid hormone replacement medicine, and prescribed treatment should never be stopped or changed without your doctor's guidance. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes to your care plan.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take your prescribed thyroid medicine consistently, generally on an empty stomach as advised, and at the same time each day
- Attend all scheduled blood tests to monitor thyroid hormone levels
- Eat a balanced, nutritious diet to support overall energy and well-being
- Engage in regular, gentle physical activity as tolerated
- Allow adequate rest, as fatigue is a common symptom
- Discuss any new supplements, especially iodine, biotin, or calcium, with your doctor, as these can interfere with thyroid tests or medicine absorption
- Be patient, as it can take some weeks for dose adjustments to improve symptoms

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Extreme tiredness, drowsiness, or difficulty staying awake
- Confusion or significant changes in mental state
- A very slow heart rate, or feeling faint
- Severe swelling of the face, hands, or legs
- Difficulty breathing
- Significant worsening of symptoms, especially during cold weather, infection, or after missing doses of thyroid medicine

In rare, severe cases, long-standing untreated hypothyroidism can lead to a life-threatening condition called myxedema coma, which needs emergency hospital treatment. Never stop or change your prescribed thyroid medicine without speaking to your doctor first.
