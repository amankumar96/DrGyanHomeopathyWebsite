## What is Glaucoma?

Glaucoma is a group of eye conditions that damage the optic nerve, the nerve that carries visual information from the eye to the brain. It is most often, though not always, associated with a build-up of pressure inside the eye. Glaucoma usually develops slowly over years, and the most common form can progress without obvious symptoms until peripheral vision is already affected. Less commonly, a sudden form called acute angle-closure glaucoma can develop quickly and is a medical emergency. Vision lost to glaucoma generally cannot be restored, which makes early detection and ongoing monitoring important.

## Symptoms

Glaucoma often develops without symptoms in its early stages. When symptoms do appear, they can include:

- Gradual loss of peripheral (side) vision, often unnoticed until advanced
- Blurred vision
- Difficulty adjusting to low light
- Seeing haloes or rings around lights
- In acute angle-closure glaucoma: sudden severe eye pain, redness, headache, blurred vision, nausea, and vomiting

Because many forms progress silently, regular eye examinations are the main way glaucoma is detected early.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Glaucoma is usually linked to increased pressure inside the eye, caused by a build-up of fluid when it does not drain properly, though optic nerve damage can occasionally occur even with normal eye pressure.

Recognised risk factors include:

- Age over 40, with risk increasing further with age
- A family history of glaucoma
- Certain ethnic backgrounds, which carry a higher risk for specific types of glaucoma
- Short-sightedness or long-sightedness
- Diabetes
- Long-term use of steroid medicines, including steroid eye drops
- Previous eye injury or certain other eye conditions

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for glaucoma aims to lower eye pressure to prevent further optic nerve damage, usually with prescribed eye drops, and sometimes laser treatment or surgery if drops are not sufficient. Regular monitoring by an eye specialist, including eye pressure checks and visual field tests, is essential to track progress over time.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this specialist care. Homeopathy is not positioned as a replacement for prescribed eye drops, laser treatment, or surgery. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and never stop or skip your prescribed eye drops without your eye doctor's advice.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use prescribed eye drops exactly as directed and attend all follow-up eye appointments
- Have regular eye examinations, especially if you have risk factors such as a family history or diabetes
- Protect your eyes from injury during sports or manual work
- Maintain a generally healthy lifestyle, including regular exercise and a balanced diet
- Inform your eye doctor about all medicines you take, including steroids
- Tell close family members about your diagnosis, since glaucoma can run in families

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency care immediately if you experience:

- Sudden, severe eye pain
- Sudden redness of the eye
- Sudden blurred vision or vision loss
- Haloes or rings seen around lights
- Nausea or vomiting accompanying eye pain or headache

These can be signs of acute angle-closure glaucoma, a sight-threatening emergency that requires immediate treatment. Never stop using your prescribed glaucoma eye drops on your own; if you have concerns about them, contact your eye doctor rather than discontinuing treatment yourself.
