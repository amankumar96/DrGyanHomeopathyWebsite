## What is Alzheimer's Disease?

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive brain condition and the most common underlying cause of dementia. It involves a gradual decline in memory, thinking, reasoning, and the ability to carry out everyday activities, caused by changes in the brain that develop over many years. Alzheimer's disease currently has no cure, in either conventional or alternative medicine, and its course generally progresses over time, though the rate of progression varies between individuals. Diagnosis and ongoing management require assessment by a neurologist or specialist memory service.

## Symptoms

Alzheimer's disease typically develops gradually and may include:

- Increasing forgetfulness, such as repeating questions or forgetting recent conversations
- Difficulty finding the right words or following conversations
- Getting confused about time, dates, or familiar places
- Difficulty with routine tasks, such as managing money or following instructions
- Changes in mood, personality, or behaviour, such as increased anxiety or withdrawal
- In later stages, difficulty recognising family members, significant disorientation, and problems with mobility, speech, and swallowing

Symptoms generally worsen over time, though the pace of decline differs from person to person.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of Alzheimer's disease is not fully understood, but it is associated with the gradual build-up of abnormal proteins in the brain, which damages brain cells over time. Known risk factors include:

- Increasing age, with risk rising significantly after 65
- A family history of Alzheimer's disease or certain genetic factors
- Cardiovascular risk factors such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, obesity, and diabetes
- Untreated depression
- Smoking and excessive alcohol use
- Lack of physical activity and limited social or mental engagement

## How Homeopathy Approaches Alzheimer's Disease

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurological condition that must be diagnosed and managed by a neurologist or specialist memory service. There is no cure for Alzheimer's disease, and homeopathy does not slow, reverse, or otherwise treat the underlying brain changes that cause it. Any suggestion that homeopathy can treat Alzheimer's disease itself would be inaccurate and irresponsible.

Some families, while continuing the person's prescribed neurological care, choose to explore individualised homeopathy purely as general, complementary support — for example, aimed at the person's and family's overall sense of wellbeing, calm, and comfort, not at the disease process itself. It is important to understand that scientific evidence for homeopathy in Alzheimer's disease or dementia is essentially absent, and major health bodies such as the NHS state there is no robust evidence that homeopathy is effective for any health condition. This page is intended for general education; specialist diagnosis and conventional care should never be delayed or replaced because of homeopathic treatment.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of Alzheimer's disease, directed by a neurologist or memory specialist, may include certain medicines that can help relieve some symptoms for a period of time, along with non-drug approaches such as cognitive stimulation therapy, structured routines, environmental adaptations, and psychological support for both the patient and family carers. These approaches do not cure the condition but can help support quality of life.

Homeopathy has no established role in treating the underlying disease and should only ever be considered, if at all, as an additional, general wellbeing measure alongside this required specialist and caregiving support — never as a substitute. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain consistent daily routines to reduce confusion and anxiety
- Encourage regular, gentle physical activity as tolerated
- Support a balanced diet and adequate hydration
- Keep the home environment safe, well-lit, and free of trip hazards
- Encourage social interaction and mentally engaging activities, within the person's comfort level
- Manage cardiovascular risk factors such as blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar under medical guidance
- Seek support for caregivers, including respite care and caregiver support groups, as caregiving can be physically and emotionally demanding

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While Alzheimer's disease itself is managed on an ongoing, specialist basis, seek prompt or urgent medical attention if the person experiences:

- A sudden, rapid change in confusion or alertness, which may indicate an infection, medication side effect, or another acute medical problem rather than normal disease progression
- Sudden weakness, difficulty speaking, facial drooping, or vision changes, which could indicate a stroke
- Falls resulting in injury, or a sudden decline in mobility
- Signs of severe dehydration, infection (such as fever), or difficulty swallowing leading to choking
- Significant behavioural changes such as aggression, severe agitation, or risk of harm to self or others

A sudden change in a person with Alzheimer's disease should never be assumed to simply be "the disease getting worse" without medical assessment, as it may indicate a separate, treatable problem. Seek urgent medical attention if any of the above occur.
