## What is Verruca Plana?

Verruca plana, commonly known as a flat wart, is a type of skin growth caused by infection with human papillomavirus (HPV). As the name suggests, these warts are smaller, smoother, and flatter than common warts, and they often appear in clusters of several to many lesions rather than as a single growth. They are frequently seen on the face, back of the hands, or shins, and are particularly common in children and young adults. Flat warts are generally harmless and many clear up on their own, although this can take months to a couple of years.

## Symptoms

Typical features of flat warts include:

- Small, flat-topped, smooth bumps, usually a few millimetres across
- Skin-coloured, light brown, or slightly pink appearance
- Lesions that often appear in clusters or lines, sometimes spreading along a scratch mark
- Most commonly found on the face, hands, or lower legs
- Usually painless, though occasionally mildly itchy
- Can multiply in number over weeks to months, especially if scratched or shaved over

## Causes & Risk Factors

Flat warts are caused by certain strains of human papillomavirus (HPV), which infects the outer layer of skin and causes it to grow abnormally. The virus spreads through direct skin-to-skin contact or indirectly through contaminated surfaces, towels, or shared objects, and can enter through small breaks or cuts in the skin.

Factors that increase the likelihood of developing or spreading flat warts include:

- Direct contact with someone who has warts
- Shaving over an area with early or hidden warts, which can spread the virus along the shaved region
- Scratching or picking at existing warts
- A weakened immune system, which can make warts more persistent or widespread
- Being a child or young adult, as this group is more commonly affected

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid picking, scratching, or shaving directly over visible warts to reduce spread
- Keep the area clean and dry, and avoid sharing towels, razors, or footwear
- Cover warts with a plaster or clothing in communal areas such as swimming pools where appropriate
- Avoid biting or picking at warts on the fingers, which can spread the virus
- Be patient, as many flat warts resolve naturally over months without any treatment
- Maintain general skin and immune health through balanced nutrition and adequate rest

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Flat warts are generally harmless, but it is important to have any skin lesion properly assessed if you notice:

- A lesion that changes in colour, shape, or size, especially if it grows rapidly
- Bleeding, ulceration, or an unusual or irregular appearance
- Pain, significant inflammation, or signs of infection such as spreading redness, warmth, or pus
- Warts that spread extensively or persist for a long time despite care
- Any skin growth you are not confident is simply a wart, particularly in adults or if it looks different from a typical flat wart

A doctor can confirm the diagnosis and make sure an unusual-looking lesion is not something that needs a different kind of attention.
