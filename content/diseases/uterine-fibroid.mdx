## What is Uterine Fibroid?

Uterine fibroids are non-cancerous growths that develop in or around the womb (uterus), made up of muscle and fibrous tissue. They are common, particularly during the reproductive years, and vary widely in size, number, and location. Many fibroids cause no symptoms at all and are discovered incidentally during a scan for another reason, while others can cause troublesome symptoms requiring medical attention. Fibroids are generally benign and often shrink after menopause.

## Symptoms

Many fibroids are symptom-free, but when symptoms do occur they can include:

- Heavy or prolonged menstrual bleeding
- Pelvic pain or a feeling of pressure or heaviness in the lower abdomen
- Frequent need to urinate, due to pressure on the bladder
- Constipation or bowel pressure symptoms, depending on fibroid location
- Pain during sex
- Lower back pain
- In some cases, difficulty conceiving or complications during pregnancy

Symptom severity does not always correlate with fibroid size; small fibroids in certain locations can cause significant symptoms, while larger ones elsewhere may cause none.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of fibroids is not fully understood, but their growth is linked to the hormones oestrogen and progesterone. Known risk factors include:

- Being of reproductive age, as fibroids are rare before puberty and often shrink after menopause
- Family history of fibroids
- Obesity, which is associated with higher oestrogen levels
- Never having been pregnant
- Ethnic background, with some studies showing higher rates and earlier onset in certain populations
- Starting periods at an early age

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of uterine fibroids depends on symptom severity and ranges from watchful waiting with periodic scans for symptom-free fibroids, to medicines that manage heavy bleeding or temporarily shrink fibroids, to procedures such as uterine artery embolisation or surgery (myomectomy or hysterectomy) in more significant cases. The choice of treatment considers factors such as fibroid size, location, symptoms, and whether future fertility is desired.

Some women choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed gynaecological care. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, particularly regarding monitoring or any decisions about fertility and future pregnancy.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain a healthy body weight, as this may help manage hormone-related symptom patterns
- Track your menstrual cycle and symptoms to share useful information with your doctor
- Ensure adequate iron intake if periods are heavy, and discuss supplementation with your doctor if needed
- Stay physically active within your comfort levels
- Use heat packs or over-the-counter pain relief as advised by your doctor for cramping discomfort
- Attend scheduled scans or follow-up appointments to monitor fibroid size and symptoms
- Discuss any fertility plans with your gynaecologist so monitoring and treatment choices can be tailored accordingly

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

- Very heavy menstrual bleeding, passing large clots, or bleeding that soaks through protection quickly
- Signs of significant blood loss, such as severe fatigue, dizziness, or shortness of breath
- Sudden, severe pelvic pain
- Fever along with pelvic pain, which could indicate a complication
- A rapidly enlarging abdomen or a fibroid that seems to be growing quickly
- Any bleeding or pain concerns during pregnancy if you have known fibroids

These symptoms warrant timely assessment by a doctor or gynaecologist rather than waiting for a routine appointment.
