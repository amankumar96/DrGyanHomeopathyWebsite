## What is Gastritis & Acidity?

Gastritis is inflammation or irritation of the lining of the stomach, which can be sudden (acute) or develop gradually over time (chronic). Acidity, commonly experienced as heartburn or acid reflux, refers to excess stomach acid causing a burning sensation, often in the chest or upper abdomen. The two conditions are closely related and frequently occur together, as an irritated stomach lining can make the effects of stomach acid more noticeable and uncomfortable.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of gastritis and acidity include:

- A gnawing or burning ache or pain in the upper abdomen
- Nausea, and occasionally vomiting
- A feeling of fullness or bloating, especially after eating
- Indigestion
- Reduced appetite
- Burping or excess gas
- A burning sensation that may rise toward the chest or throat (acid reflux)

It is worth noting that gastritis does not always cause noticeable symptoms, and some people only discover it incidentally during medical investigation for other reasons.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes of gastritis and acidity include:

- Infection with Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) bacteria, a leading cause of chronic gastritis
- Regular or long-term use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) and aspirin
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Smoking
- Severe physical stress, such as major illness, injury, or surgery
- Autoimmune conditions, where the body's immune system attacks the stomach lining
- Other digestive conditions, such as bile reflux

Risk factors can also include irregular eating habits, high intake of spicy or fatty foods, and certain chronic illnesses.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large heavy meals
- Limit or avoid spicy, fatty, or highly acidic foods if they trigger symptoms
- Reduce alcohol intake and avoid smoking
- Avoid taking NSAIDs or aspirin unnecessarily; discuss alternatives with your doctor if you need regular pain relief
- Manage stress through relaxation, adequate rest, and regular sleep
- Avoid lying down immediately after eating, especially if acid reflux is a concern
- Stay well hydrated and maintain a generally balanced diet

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor if gastritis or acidity symptoms last a week or longer, or sooner if they are severe. Seek emergency medical attention right away if you experience:

- Vomiting blood or material that looks like coffee grounds
- Black, tarry, or foul-smelling stools, or blood in the stool
- Sudden, severe abdominal or chest pain
- Persistent vomiting where you are unable to keep food or fluids down
- Feeling lightheaded, dizzy, or faint alongside stomach symptoms

Also contact your doctor promptly if you notice unexplained weight loss, difficulty swallowing, or a severe, ongoing loss of appetite alongside digestive symptoms, as these require timely medical evaluation.
