## What is Sleep Apnea Syndrome?

Sleep apnea syndrome is a condition in which breathing repeatedly stops and starts during sleep, often due to the throat muscles relaxing and narrowing or blocking the airway. This is known as obstructive sleep apnea, the most common form, though a less common type called central sleep apnea occurs when the brain does not send the correct signals to the muscles that control breathing. These pauses in breathing can happen many times a night, often without the person being fully aware of them, and can disrupt sleep quality and oxygen levels in the body.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of sleep apnea include:

- Loud, persistent snoring
- Noticeable pauses in breathing during sleep, often observed by a partner
- Gasping, choking, or snorting sounds while asleep
- Waking up frequently during the night
- Excessive daytime sleepiness or tiredness
- Morning headaches
- Difficulty concentrating, irritability, or low mood
- Dry mouth or sore throat on waking

Many people with sleep apnea are not aware of the breathing pauses themselves and only recognise the condition through a partner's observations or through daytime symptoms.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Sleep apnea occurs when the airway becomes repeatedly blocked or when brain signals to breathing muscles are disrupted during sleep. Known risk factors include:

- Being overweight or obese, which can increase soft tissue around the airway
- Having a naturally narrow airway, enlarged tonsils, or a recessed jaw
- Age, as sleep apnea is more common in older adults
- Being male, though it also affects women, particularly after menopause
- Family history of sleep apnea
- Smoking and alcohol use, which can relax throat muscles further
- Nasal congestion or structural nasal problems

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for sleep apnea depends on severity and may include lifestyle changes, oral appliances to keep the airway open, or continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy, which is commonly used for moderate to severe cases. In some cases, surgery may be considered. Untreated, moderate to severe sleep apnea is linked to increased risks of high blood pressure, heart problems, and stroke, so prescribed treatment such as CPAP should not be stopped or reduced without medical advice.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain a healthy weight, as excess weight can worsen airway obstruction
- Sleep on your side rather than your back, which can reduce airway blockage for some people
- Avoid alcohol and sedatives close to bedtime, as they relax throat muscles further
- Establish a regular sleep schedule and good sleep hygiene habits
- Avoid smoking, which can increase airway inflammation
- Keep nasal passages clear, and treat nasal congestion where appropriate
- Use your prescribed CPAP or other device consistently, as advised by your sleep specialist

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While sleep apnea is often a gradually progressing condition, seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

- Chest pain, irregular heartbeat, or severe shortness of breath
- Extreme daytime sleepiness that affects safety, such as while driving
- Observed pauses in breathing during sleep that are frequent, prolonged, or associated with choking
- Sudden worsening of symptoms despite using prescribed treatment such as CPAP

Untreated severe sleep apnea carries cardiovascular and other health risks, so these symptoms should be assessed by a doctor rather than managed alone, and prescribed treatment should never be stopped without medical guidance.
