## What is Cystitis?

Cystitis is inflammation of the bladder, most often caused by a bacterial infection that has travelled up the urethra. It is one of the most common forms of urinary tract infection and affects women more often than men, partly due to differences in anatomy. Cystitis can range from a mild, short-lived irritation to a more uncomfortable infection that needs medical treatment. While many episodes resolve with appropriate care, some people experience repeated or recurrent cystitis.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of cystitis include:

- Pain, burning, or stinging when passing urine
- Needing to urinate more often than usual, sometimes passing only small amounts
- A sudden, strong urge to urinate
- Cloudy, dark, or strong-smelling urine
- Mild discomfort or pressure in the lower abdomen or back
- A general feeling of being unwell, in some cases
- Blood in the urine, in some cases

Symptoms can vary in intensity and may be mistaken for other urinary conditions, so a proper diagnosis is helpful.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cystitis is most commonly caused by bacteria, often from the bowel, entering the urethra and travelling to the bladder. Several factors can increase the likelihood of developing it, including:

- Being female, due to a shorter urethra that is closer to the back passage
- Sexual activity
- Pregnancy
- Menopause, due to hormonal changes affecting the urinary tract
- Use of certain types of contraception, such as diaphragms
- Conditions that affect bladder emptying, such as an enlarged prostate or urinary catheters
- Diabetes, which can increase infection risk
- Not drinking enough fluids

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Drink plenty of water to help flush the urinary system
- Urinate when you feel the need, rather than holding it in
- Empty your bladder fully, including after sexual activity
- Wipe from front to back after using the toilet
- Avoid using scented soaps, bubble baths, or douches in the genital area
- Wear breathable, cotton underwear
- Avoid excessive caffeine and alcohol, which can irritate the bladder

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Pain or discomfort in your back or sides, which may indicate the infection has reached the kidneys
- Fever, chills, or shivering
- Nausea or vomiting
- Blood in your urine
- Symptoms that do not improve within a couple of days, or that worsen
- Recurrent episodes of cystitis
- Cystitis during pregnancy
- Symptoms in a man, a child, or a person with diabetes or a weakened immune system

These situations can indicate a more serious infection requiring prompt medical care, so contact your doctor without delay.
