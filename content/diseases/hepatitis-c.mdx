## What is Hepatitis C?

Hepatitis C is an infection caused by the hepatitis C virus that primarily affects the liver. It spreads through contact with infected blood and can cause either a short-term (acute) illness or, more often, a long-term (chronic) infection that persists for years if not treated. Many people with hepatitis C have no noticeable symptoms for a long time, even as the virus quietly affects the liver, which is why it is sometimes discovered only through blood tests done for other reasons. With modern antiviral medicines, most people with hepatitis C can now have the virus cleared from their blood after a course of treatment.

## Symptoms

Many people with hepatitis C have no symptoms at all, particularly in the early stages. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

- Tiredness and general fatigue
- Loss of appetite and mild nausea
- Muscle and joint aches
- Tenderness or discomfort in the upper right abdomen, near the liver
- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice) in some cases
- Dark urine or pale stools
- Itchy skin

Because symptoms can be vague or absent, many infections are only identified years after exposure, often once some liver damage has already developed.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Hepatitis C is caused by infection with the hepatitis C virus, which spreads through contact with infected blood. Common routes of transmission include:

- Sharing needles, syringes, or other equipment used for injecting drugs
- Using unsterilised medical, dental, or tattooing/piercing equipment
- Receiving blood transfusions or organ transplants in settings without adequate screening (now rare in countries with robust blood safety standards)
- Mother-to-child transmission during childbirth, in some cases
- Less commonly, sexual contact involving exposure to blood

People who have had long-term kidney dialysis, healthcare workers exposed to needle-stick injuries, and those who received transfusions before widespread blood screening programmes are at somewhat higher risk.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for hepatitis C typically involves a course of direct-acting antiviral medicines, usually taken for a number of weeks, which can clear the virus from the blood in most people. Regular blood tests and liver health monitoring are an important part of care, both during and after treatment, and specialist referral may be needed if liver damage has already occurred.

Homeopathy, where chosen by a patient, should only ever be considered as a complementary approach alongside this prescribed care, never as a replacement for antiviral medicines or liver monitoring. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes to your care plan.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid alcohol or limit it strictly, as advised by your doctor, to protect your liver
- Eat a balanced diet to support overall health and liver function
- Avoid sharing needles, razors, toothbrushes, or other items that could carry traces of blood
- Attend all scheduled blood tests and liver health check-ups
- Discuss any new medicines or supplements with your doctor, as some can affect the liver
- Practise safer sex and discuss any specific precautions with your doctor if you have hepatitis C
- Get vaccinated against hepatitis A and B if advised, to protect your liver from further infections

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice) that is new or worsening
- Severe abdominal pain or swelling
- Vomiting blood or passing black, tarry stools
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or difficulty staying awake
- Significant swelling of the legs or abdomen
- Easy bruising or unusual bleeding

These can be signs of serious liver complications and need urgent medical assessment without delay.
