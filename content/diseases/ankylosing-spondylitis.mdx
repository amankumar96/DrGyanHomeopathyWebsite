## What is Ankylosing Spondylitis?

Ankylosing spondylitis is a long-term inflammatory condition that mainly affects the joints of the spine and the sacroiliac joints, which connect the lower spine to the pelvis. Over time, the inflammation can lead to stiffness and, in some people, changes in the shape and flexibility of the spine. It typically begins in late adolescence or early adulthood and tends to be a lifelong condition, though its course and severity vary widely between individuals. It belongs to a group of conditions known as spondyloarthropathies.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of ankylosing spondylitis include:

- Persistent lower back and buttock pain, often developing gradually over weeks or months
- Stiffness that is typically worse in the morning or after periods of rest, and improves with movement
- Pain that may disturb sleep, especially in the second half of the night
- Reduced flexibility of the spine over time
- Pain and swelling in other joints, such as the hips, shoulders, or knees
- Fatigue
- Inflammation of the eye (uveitis), causing eye pain, redness, and sensitivity to light, in some people
- In some cases, pain where tendons and ligaments attach to bone, such as the heel

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of ankylosing spondylitis is not fully understood, but it is strongly linked to a genetic marker called HLA-B27, which is present in most people with the condition, though not everyone with this gene develops it. It is thought to involve the immune system mistakenly causing inflammation in the joints.

Known risk factors include:

- A family history of ankylosing spondylitis or related conditions
- Being male, as the condition is diagnosed more often in men, though it also affects women
- Age, as symptoms most often begin between the late teens and early thirties
- Having certain other inflammatory conditions, such as inflammatory bowel disease or psoriasis

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay physically active with regular, gentle stretching and exercise to help maintain mobility
- Practise good posture, especially when sitting for long periods
- Consider swimming or other low-impact activities that support spinal movement
- Use a supportive mattress and pillow to ease morning stiffness
- Avoid smoking, as it is linked to worse outcomes in ankylosing spondylitis
- Take breaks to move around if you sit for long stretches, such as at a desk
- Work with a physiotherapist to develop a suitable, personalised exercise routine

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Sudden, severe back pain, especially after a fall or injury, which could indicate a spinal fracture
- New numbness, weakness, or tingling in the legs, or loss of bladder or bowel control
- Sudden eye pain, redness, and sensitivity to light, which may indicate uveitis and needs prompt eye assessment
- Unexplained weight loss, persistent fever, or severe fatigue
- Chest pain or breathing difficulty, as the condition can occasionally affect the chest wall or heart

These situations can require urgent medical care, so contact your doctor without delay.
