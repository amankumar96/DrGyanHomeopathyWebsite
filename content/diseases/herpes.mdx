## What is Herpes?

Herpes refers to infection with the herpes simplex virus (HSV), of which there are two main types: HSV-1, which most often causes cold sores around the mouth, and HSV-2, which most often causes genital sores, although either type can affect either area. The virus is extremely common worldwide, and many people carry it without ever developing noticeable symptoms. Once a person is infected, the virus remains present in the body in a dormant state within nerve cells, and can become active from time to time, causing outbreaks of sores that heal on their own, usually within one to two weeks. There is currently no cure that removes the virus from the body, but outbreaks can usually be managed well.

## Symptoms

Symptoms can vary widely, and many people have very mild or no noticeable symptoms. When present, they may include:

- Small, painful blisters or sores, which may cluster together before crusting over
- Tingling, itching, or burning sensation in the area shortly before sores appear
- For cold sores: sores around the lips, mouth, or nose
- For genital herpes: sores or blisters on or around the genitals, anus, or inner thighs
- Flu-like symptoms during a first outbreak, such as fever, body aches, and swollen glands
- Pain when urinating, if sores are near the urethra

The first outbreak after infection is often the most noticeable, with later outbreaks typically being milder and shorter.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Herpes is caused by infection with HSV-1 or HSV-2, which spreads through direct skin-to-skin contact with an infected area, including kissing, sharing items that touch the mouth, or sexual contact. The virus can be passed on even when no sores are visible, though the risk is higher when sores are present.

Factors that can trigger a dormant infection to reactivate and cause an outbreak include:

- Physical or emotional stress
- Tiredness or lack of sleep
- Illness, fever, or a weakened immune system
- Sun exposure or wind, particularly for cold sores
- Hormonal changes, such as around menstruation
- Friction or irritation to the skin in the affected area

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for herpes usually involves antiviral medicines, which can help shorten outbreaks, reduce symptoms, and in some cases be taken regularly to reduce how often outbreaks occur or to lower the risk of passing the virus to a partner. A doctor or sexual health service can also advise on pain relief, keeping the area clean, and strategies to reduce transmission.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this conventional care, but it should never replace antiviral treatment or medical advice, particularly for a first suspected episode of genital herpes, which needs proper diagnosis. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes to your care plan.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep the affected area clean and dry, and avoid touching sores unnecessarily
- Wash your hands after touching a sore to avoid spreading the virus to other areas, such as the eyes
- Avoid kissing or intimate contact while sores are present or tingling is felt
- Use sunscreen or lip balm with SPF if sun exposure triggers your cold sores
- Manage stress and prioritise rest, as tiredness and stress are common triggers
- Wear loose, breathable clothing during a genital outbreak to reduce irritation
- Talk openly with sexual partners and a doctor about reducing the risk of transmission

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most herpes outbreaks can be managed with routine care, but seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Sores or symptoms near or affecting the eye, which needs urgent assessment to prevent vision complications
- Severe outbreaks, widespread sores, or symptoms that will not settle in someone who is immunocompromised (for example, due to HIV, chemotherapy, or immune-suppressing medicines)
- A first-ever episode of genital sores, which should be assessed by a doctor or sexual health service for accurate diagnosis and personalised advice
- Difficulty urinating, severe pain, or inability to pass urine
- Sores accompanied by high fever, severe headache, or confusion
- Herpes symptoms occurring in a newborn baby, which always needs urgent medical review

These situations should not be managed with self-care or complementary approaches alone and need prompt medical attention.
