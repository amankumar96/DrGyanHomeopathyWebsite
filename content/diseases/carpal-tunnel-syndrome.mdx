## What is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

Carpal tunnel syndrome is a condition that occurs when the median nerve, which runs from the forearm into the hand, becomes compressed as it passes through a narrow passage in the wrist called the carpal tunnel. This pressure on the nerve can cause numbness, tingling, pain, and weakness in the hand and fingers. It is a common condition, particularly among adults who perform repetitive hand movements, and it can range from mild and occasional to persistent and disabling if left unaddressed.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of carpal tunnel syndrome include:

- Numbness or tingling in the thumb, index, middle, and part of the ring finger
- A burning or aching pain in the hand or wrist, sometimes spreading up the forearm
- Symptoms that are often worse at night and may disturb sleep
- A sensation of swelling in the fingers, even when no visible swelling is present
- Weakness in the hand, leading to dropping objects or difficulty with fine tasks like buttoning clothes
- In longer-standing cases, visible wasting of the muscle at the base of the thumb

## Causes & Risk Factors

Carpal tunnel syndrome develops when tissue around the tendons in the wrist swells or thickens, putting pressure on the median nerve within the confined space of the carpal tunnel. Several factors can increase the risk, including:

- Repetitive hand or wrist movements, including prolonged typing or use of vibrating tools
- Pregnancy, due to fluid retention and hormonal changes
- Obesity
- Diabetes and other conditions that affect nerve health
- An underactive thyroid gland
- Rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory conditions
- A wrist fracture or other structural changes in the wrist
- A family history of carpal tunnel syndrome, as some people have a naturally smaller carpal tunnel

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for carpal tunnel syndrome usually begins with conservative measures such as wrist splinting, especially at night, activity modification, and anti-inflammatory medication. Corticosteroid injections may be used for more persistent symptoms, and surgery to release pressure on the nerve is considered when symptoms are severe, long-standing, or show signs of nerve damage.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take regular breaks from repetitive hand activities, such as typing or using tools
- Keep wrists in a neutral, straight position where possible during activities
- Consider an ergonomic assessment of your workstation, keyboard, and mouse
- Use a wrist splint at night if recommended, to keep the wrist from bending during sleep
- Avoid resting weight on your wrists for prolonged periods
- Gently stretch the wrists and hands during breaks from repetitive tasks
- Manage underlying conditions such as diabetes or thyroid problems with your doctor's guidance

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Constant numbness in the hand that does not go away between activities
- Visible wasting or flattening of the muscle at the base of the thumb
- Sudden or progressive weakness affecting your grip or ability to use the hand
- Symptoms that are rapidly worsening or affecting daily tasks significantly

These signs can suggest ongoing nerve damage, and timely assessment by a doctor is important to prevent lasting impairment.
