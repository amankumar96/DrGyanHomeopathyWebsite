## What is Uveitis?

Uveitis is inflammation of the uvea, the middle layer of the eye that includes the iris, ciliary body, and choroid. It can affect one or both eyes and may be acute (sudden and short-lived) or chronic (persistent or recurring). Because the inflamed structures are closely connected to vision, uveitis is considered a potentially vision-threatening condition that needs prompt assessment and treatment by an eye care professional, rather than a condition to self-manage or delay evaluating.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of uveitis can develop suddenly or gradually and may include:

- Eye redness
- Eye pain, which can range from mild discomfort to significant pain
- Sensitivity to light (photophobia)
- Blurred or reduced vision
- Floaters, or small shapes drifting across the field of vision
- In some cases, symptoms affecting both eyes, or recurring in episodes over time

Symptoms can vary depending on which part of the uvea is affected and how quickly the inflammation develops.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Uveitis can arise from several different underlying processes, including:

- Autoimmune or inflammatory conditions, such as ankylosing spondylitis, inflammatory bowel disease, or sarcoidosis
- Infections, including certain viral, bacterial, fungal, or parasitic infections
- Eye injury or trauma
- Certain medicines, in rare cases
- No identifiable cause at all, known as idiopathic uveitis, which is common

Anyone with an existing autoimmune or inflammatory condition may be at higher risk, and uveitis can sometimes be the first sign that prompts further investigation for such conditions.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for uveitis depends on the cause and severity but commonly involves steroid eye drops, injections, or sometimes oral steroids to reduce inflammation, along with treatment of any underlying infection or systemic condition identified. Pupil-dilating drops may also be used to reduce pain and prevent complications. Regular follow-up with an ophthalmologist is important to monitor the response to treatment and watch for complications such as raised eye pressure or cataracts.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed eye treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and never stop or taper prescribed steroid eye drops without your doctor's guidance, as doing so can trigger a flare.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all follow-up ophthalmology appointments, even if symptoms seem to improve
- Wear sunglasses if light sensitivity is troublesome
- Avoid rubbing or touching the affected eye
- Keep track of any new or changing symptoms to report to your eye specialist
- If an underlying systemic condition is identified, follow through with any referrals or further investigations recommended
- Protect the eyes from injury and avoid activities that risk eye trauma during active inflammation

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Uveitis requires prompt, same-day ophthalmology assessment. Seek urgent medical or eye care immediately if you notice:

- Sudden eye pain
- New or worsening eye redness
- Increased sensitivity to light
- Blurred vision or any sudden change in vision
- New floaters or flashes of light in your vision
- Symptoms affecting a previously unaffected eye, if you have a history of uveitis

Because untreated uveitis can threaten vision, these symptoms should never be managed with complementary approaches alone or delayed in favour of watching and waiting; prompt assessment by an eye specialist is needed.
