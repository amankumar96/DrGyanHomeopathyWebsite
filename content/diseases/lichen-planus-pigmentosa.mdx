## What is Lichen Planus Pigmentosa?

Lichen Planus Pigmentosa is a long-term skin condition that causes slate-grey or brownish patches of pigmentation, most commonly on the face, neck, and sometimes other sun-exposed or skin-fold areas. It is related to lichen planus, a group of inflammatory skin conditions, but in this form the pigmentation itself is usually the main and sometimes only feature, without the itchy raised bumps seen in classic lichen planus. It tends to develop gradually and follows a slow, chronic course, often persisting for a long time before fading.

## Symptoms

Typical features of lichen planus pigmentosa include:

- Greyish-brown or slate-coloured patches, usually flat rather than raised
- Patches commonly appearing on the forehead, temples, cheeks, and neck
- Occasional involvement of skin folds such as the armpits or groin
- Patches that may merge together over time to form larger areas
- Usually mild or no itching, unlike typical lichen planus
- A slow, chronic course with patches sometimes fading and new ones appearing elsewhere

## Causes & Risk Factors

The precise cause is not fully understood. It is thought to represent a form of skin inflammation affecting the pigment-producing cells, leading to deposition of pigment in the deeper skin layers. Suggested contributing factors include:

- A possible relationship to lichen planus and related inflammatory skin processes
- Exposure to sunlight (ultraviolet light)
- Use of certain cosmetic products, oils, or hair dyes in some individuals
- Possible genetic or ethnic predisposition, as it is reported more often in people with darker skin tones
- Mustard oil use has been suggested as a possible association in some populations, though evidence is limited

Conventional management of lichen planus pigmentosa is often focused on identifying and avoiding possible triggers, consistent sun protection, and sometimes topical treatments such as prescribed creams, with dermatologist-guided options in more persistent cases. Because the pigmentation can fade very slowly on its own, treatment decisions are usually individualised and reviewed over time.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen daily, as sun exposure may worsen pigmentation
- Avoid harsh facial cosmetics, scrubs, or products that irritate the skin
- Be cautious with hair dyes or oils if you suspect they may be linked to flares
- Be patient, as visible improvement in pigmentation can take many months to years
- Avoid picking or rubbing affected skin
- Maintain a gentle, consistent skincare routine with mild, fragrance-free products

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Lichen planus pigmentosa itself is not cancerous and is not contagious, and it is generally a benign, slow-changing condition. However, any new, rapidly spreading, unusually textured, bleeding, or changing skin lesion should always be checked by a doctor to confirm the diagnosis, since several skin conditions can look similar. A dermatologist's assessment helps rule out other causes of pigmentation change and ensures the right diagnosis is made before starting any treatment.
