## What is GERD (Acid Reflux)?

Gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD, also spelled GORD) occurs when the muscle at the lower end of the food pipe (oesophagus) does not close properly or relaxes at the wrong times, allowing stomach acid to flow back up into the oesophagus. This backward flow, called acid reflux, causes the burning sensation known as heartburn. Occasional reflux is common and usually harmless, but when it happens frequently, it is classed as GERD and may need medical management to prevent irritation of the food pipe lining.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of GERD include:

- A burning sensation in the chest (heartburn), often after eating and sometimes worse at night or when lying down
- A sour or bitter taste in the mouth, or food and liquid coming back up into the throat
- Upper abdominal or chest discomfort
- Difficulty swallowing or a sensation of a lump in the throat
- Bloating, burping, or nausea
- A persistent cough, hoarse voice, or bad breath in some people, particularly with nighttime reflux

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes and contributing factors include:

- Weakening or inappropriate relaxation of the muscle between the stomach and oesophagus
- Being overweight or obese
- A hiatus hernia, where part of the stomach moves up into the chest
- Pregnancy
- Smoking
- Certain foods and drinks, such as coffee, alcohol, chocolate, tomatoes, and fatty or spicy foods
- Certain medications, including some painkillers like ibuprofen
- Stress and hormonal changes
- Delayed stomach emptying

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for GERD typically starts with lifestyle changes, such as eating smaller meals, losing weight if needed, avoiding trigger foods, and raising the head of the bed. A pharmacist may recommend antacids or alginates for short-term relief, while a doctor may prescribe proton pump inhibitors, such as omeprazole or lansoprazole, usually for four to eight weeks, with other options considered if these are not effective. Severe or treatment-resistant cases may need specialist referral for further testing or, occasionally, surgery.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this care, often seeking general digestive comfort support alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should not be used as a replacement for prescribed acid-suppressing medication or medical evaluation. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Eat smaller, more frequent meals rather than large ones
- Avoid known trigger foods and drinks, such as coffee, alcohol, chocolate, and fatty or spicy foods
- Avoid eating within a few hours of going to bed
- Raise the head of your bed by around 10 to 20 centimetres if nighttime reflux is a problem
- Maintain a healthy weight, as excess weight can worsen reflux
- Avoid smoking and tight clothing around the waist
- Manage stress through relaxation, adequate rest, and regular sleep

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor if heartburn occurs most days, lifestyle changes and pharmacy medicines are not helping, or symptoms keep coming back after treatment. Seek urgent or emergency medical attention if you experience:

- Vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds
- Black, tarry stools
- Severe chest pain, especially if it could be confused with a heart problem
- Food getting stuck when swallowing, or increasing difficulty swallowing
- Unexplained weight loss
- Persistent vomiting

These symptoms require prompt medical evaluation to rule out serious complications.
