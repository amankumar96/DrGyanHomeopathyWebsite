## What is Tonsillitis?

Tonsillitis is an infection and inflammation of the tonsils, the two soft tissue masses at the back of the throat on either side. It is most common in children but can affect people of any age. Most cases are caused by viruses, similar to those causing a common cold, though bacterial infections such as strep throat can also be responsible.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of tonsillitis include:

- A sore throat, which can range from mild to severe
- Red, swollen tonsils, sometimes with white or yellow patches or coating
- Difficulty or pain when swallowing
- Fever
- Swollen, tender glands in the neck
- Bad breath
- Headache, earache, nausea, and general tiredness
- Cough, in some cases

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes and contributing factors include:

- Viral infections, similar to those causing colds, which are the most frequent cause
- Bacterial infections, such as Group A Streptococcus (strep throat)
- Close contact with others who have a throat infection, especially in schools or crowded settings
- Age, with tonsillitis more common in children over two years old, and bacterial tonsillitis particularly common between ages 5 and 15

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for tonsillitis depends on the cause. Most viral cases are managed with rest, fluids, and over-the-counter pain relief, and resolve within three to four days. Bacterial tonsillitis, confirmed through examination or a throat swab, is usually treated with a prescribed course of antibiotics. Recurrent or severe cases unresponsive to treatment may be referred for consideration of tonsil removal (tonsillectomy).

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medical care, often seeking general throat comfort support alongside their prescribed treatment. ## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Rest as much as possible, especially during the first few days of illness
- Drink plenty of fluids, including cool or warm soothing drinks as preferred
- Eat soft, easy-to-swallow foods if swallowing is painful
- Gargle with warm salt water to help soothe throat discomfort
- Use throat lozenges or over-the-counter pain relievers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen as needed, following label guidance
- Avoid smoking and smoky environments, which can irritate the throat further
- Avoid close contact with others where possible to reduce the risk of spreading infection

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor if a sore throat is severe, lasts longer than a few days, or is accompanied by a high fever. Seek emergency medical attention (call emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department) if you or your child experience:

- Difficulty breathing or noisy breathing
- Severe difficulty swallowing, or drooling because swallowing is too painful
- Significant swelling in the mouth or throat, or a muffled "hot potato" voice
- Inability to open the mouth fully, or severe one-sided throat pain and swelling

These can indicate a serious complication, such as a throat abscess, that needs urgent assessment.
