## What is Molluscum Contagiosum?

Molluscum contagiosum is a common skin infection caused by a virus related to the poxvirus family. It produces small, firm, usually painless, dome-shaped bumps on the skin, often with a characteristic central dimple. It is most frequently seen in young children but can affect people of any age. The condition is generally mild and tends to resolve on its own without treatment, though this can take anywhere from several months to a couple of years, and in some cases longer, particularly in people with weakened immune systems.

## Symptoms

Typical features of molluscum contagiosum include:

- Small, firm, pearly or flesh-coloured bumps on the skin
- A characteristic small central dimple or pit in each bump
- Bumps that may appear singly or in clusters, commonly on the trunk, arms, legs, or face in children
- Mild itching around the bumps in some cases
- Spread of bumps to nearby skin through scratching
- Redness or irritation around lesions if they become inflamed or infected

The bumps are usually painless, though they can become itchy, irritated, or inflamed over time.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Molluscum contagiosum is caused by the molluscum contagiosum virus and spreads through:

- Direct skin-to-skin contact with an infected person
- Contact with contaminated objects such as towels, clothing, or bath sponges
- Self-spreading (auto-inoculation) by scratching or touching the bumps and then touching other areas of skin
- Close contact settings such as schools, swimming pools, or households

Certain factors can increase likelihood of infection or persistence, including:

- Young age, as it is most common in children
- Weakened immune systems, which may lead to more widespread or persistent lesions
- Participation in contact sports or shared swimming facilities
- Eczema or other conditions that disrupt the skin barrier
- Sexual contact, in adults, when lesions affect the genital area

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Avoid scratching or squeezing the bumps to reduce spread and risk of infection
- Keep the affected skin clean and cover visible lesions with clothing or a plaster where practical, especially for swimming or contact sports
- Avoid sharing towels, clothing, or bath items with others
- Wash hands regularly, particularly after touching the affected area
- Keep fingernails short in children to reduce scratching and self-spread
- Moisturise surrounding skin gently if it becomes dry or irritated

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

While molluscum contagiosum is usually mild, it is worth arranging a doctor's review, without needing to be an emergency, if you notice:

- Signs of secondary bacterial infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pain, or pus around the bumps
- Widespread or rapidly increasing lesions, particularly in someone known to have a weakened immune system
- Lesions affecting the eyelids or close to the eyes, which may need specific assessment
- Lesions appearing in the genital area, especially in adults, to rule out a sexually transmitted infection
- Bumps that persist for a very long time without any improvement

These situations benefit from a doctor's assessment to guide appropriate next steps.
