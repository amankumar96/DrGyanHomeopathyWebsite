## What is Asthma?

Asthma is a long-term condition affecting the airways, the small tubes that carry air in and out of the lungs. In asthma, these airways become inflamed and sensitive, and can narrow in response to certain triggers, making breathing difficult. While asthma often begins in childhood, it can develop at any age, including in adults who have never had breathing problems before, and it can also persist from childhood into adult life. Symptoms tend to vary over time, with periods of good control and occasional flare-ups.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of asthma include:

- Wheezing, a whistling sound when breathing, especially on breathing out
- Shortness of breath
- A tight feeling or pain in the chest
- Coughing, which may be worse at night or early morning, or triggered by exercise or cold air
- Symptoms that come and go, and may worsen with specific triggers
- Difficulty sleeping due to coughing, wheezing, or breathlessness

Symptoms can range from mild and occasional to severe and frequent, and may change over a person's lifetime.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of asthma is not fully understood, but it is thought to result from a combination of genetic and environmental factors that lead to airway inflammation and sensitivity.

Known risk factors and triggers include:

- A family history of asthma, eczema, or hay fever
- A personal history of allergic conditions
- Respiratory infections, particularly in early life
- Exposure to tobacco smoke, air pollution, or occupational irritants
- Allergens such as pollen, dust mites, mould, or pet dander
- Cold air, exercise, and strong smells or fumes
- Emotional stress, which can act as a trigger in some people
- Obesity, which is associated with increased risk and severity in adults

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for asthma usually involves inhalers, including preventer inhalers used regularly to reduce airway inflammation and reliever inhalers used during symptoms, along with a personalised action plan from your doctor. Avoiding known triggers is also an important part of management.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Identify and try to avoid your personal asthma triggers where possible
- Keep your reliever inhaler with you at all times
- Follow your personalised asthma action plan, if you have one, and review it regularly with your doctor
- Avoid smoking and exposure to secondhand smoke
- Get recommended vaccinations, such as the annual flu vaccine, as advised by your doctor
- Maintain a healthy weight, as excess weight can worsen asthma symptoms
- Monitor your symptoms and seek review if you are needing your reliever inhaler more often than usual

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical attention immediately if you or someone else experiences:

- A reliever inhaler not helping, or needing to be used repeatedly within a short time
- Severe breathlessness, such that it is difficult to speak, eat, or sleep
- Lips, face, or fingertips turning blue or grey
- Rapid worsening of wheeze, cough, or chest tightness
- Exhaustion, confusion, or a feeling that breathing is becoming more difficult despite treatment

These are signs of a serious asthma attack and require emergency care without delay.
