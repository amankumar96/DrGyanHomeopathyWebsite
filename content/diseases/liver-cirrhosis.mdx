## What is Liver Cirrhosis?

Liver cirrhosis is a long-term condition in which ongoing damage to the liver, from any cause, leads to the gradual replacement of healthy liver tissue with scar tissue. Over time, this scarring can interfere with the liver's ability to carry out its many functions, including processing nutrients, filtering toxins, and producing proteins the body needs. Cirrhosis develops gradually, often over many years, and can result from a range of underlying causes. It is a serious condition that needs ongoing specialist medical care, but with early diagnosis and management of the underlying cause, progression can often be slowed considerably.

## Symptoms

In its early stages, cirrhosis may cause few or no symptoms. As liver function declines, symptoms can include:

- Persistent tiredness and weakness
- Loss of appetite and unintentional weight loss
- Nausea
- Yellowing of the skin and eyes (jaundice)
- Itchy skin
- Swelling in the legs, ankles, or abdomen due to fluid build-up
- Easy bruising or bleeding
- Spider-like blood vessels visible on the skin
- Confusion, forgetfulness, or drowsiness in more advanced stages

Because early cirrhosis can be silent, it is sometimes first picked up through blood tests or scans done for other reasons.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cirrhosis results from long-term, repeated damage to the liver. Recognised causes and risk factors include:

- Long-term heavy alcohol use
- Chronic viral hepatitis, particularly hepatitis B and hepatitis C
- Long-standing fatty liver disease, including that associated with obesity and type 2 diabetes
- Certain autoimmune conditions affecting the liver
- Inherited conditions affecting how the body processes iron or copper
- Long-term blockage of the bile ducts
- Some medicines, when used over a long period

It is common for more than one factor to contribute, and in some cases, the specific cause is not clearly identified. There is no benefit in assigning blame for how cirrhosis developed — the priority is identifying the cause so it can be managed going forward.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of cirrhosis focuses on treating the underlying cause where possible, such as antiviral treatment for hepatitis, stopping alcohol use with appropriate support, or managing associated conditions like diabetes. Treatment also addresses complications, which may include medicines to manage fluid build-up, measures to reduce the risk of bleeding, and regular monitoring through blood tests, scans, and sometimes endoscopy. In more advanced cases, specialists may discuss liver transplant assessment.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled specialist and monitoring appointments, even when feeling well
- Follow your doctor's guidance regarding alcohol, which for most people with cirrhosis means avoiding it completely
- Eat a balanced diet, and discuss any specific dietary adjustments with your doctor or a dietitian, as nutrition needs can change with liver disease
- Take care with medicines, including over-the-counter drugs and supplements, as some can affect the liver, so check with your doctor or pharmacist before starting anything new
- Stay up to date with recommended vaccinations, such as for hepatitis A and B, as advised by your doctor
- Maintain a healthy weight and stay physically active as tolerated
- Seek support for alcohol use if relevant, without self-judgment, as help and treatment are available and effective

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical care, as these can be signs of serious complications, if you notice:

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes that is new or worsening (jaundice)
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty thinking clearly
- Vomiting blood, or vomit that looks like coffee grounds
- Black, tarry, or sticky stools, or visible blood in the stool
- Sudden or marked swelling of the abdomen or legs
- High fever or signs of infection
- Significant worsening of tiredness, breathlessness, or confusion

These symptoms can indicate serious complications of cirrhosis that need immediate medical attention, so do not wait to seek care.
