## What is Fatty Liver?

Fatty liver disease, more specifically non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD), is a condition in which excess fat accumulates in liver cells in people who drink little or no alcohol. It is closely linked to obesity, insulin resistance, and metabolic syndrome, and has become increasingly common worldwide. In most people, the fat build-up alone causes no significant harm, but in a smaller proportion it can progress to inflammation, scarring, and more serious liver disease over time if underlying risk factors are not addressed.

## Symptoms

Fatty liver disease often causes no noticeable symptoms, particularly in its early stages, and is frequently discovered incidentally during blood tests or imaging done for other reasons. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

- Fatigue or a general feeling of low energy
- Mild discomfort or a dull ache in the upper right side of the abdomen
- A feeling of fullness or bloating
- Unintentional weight changes in some cases

As the condition progresses to more advanced liver disease, additional symptoms such as jaundice, swelling, or confusion can develop, which require urgent medical review.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Fatty liver disease develops when the liver accumulates more fat than it can process, and is strongly associated with metabolic health. Known risk factors include:

- Being overweight or living with obesity, particularly with fat around the abdomen
- Type 2 diabetes or insulin resistance
- High blood cholesterol or triglyceride levels
- High blood pressure
- Metabolic syndrome
- Rapid weight loss or certain nutritional imbalances, in some cases
- Genetic and family factors influencing susceptibility

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of fatty liver disease focuses mainly on gradual, sustained weight loss, a balanced diet, regular physical activity, and careful management of related conditions such as diabetes, high cholesterol, and high blood pressure. Doctors typically monitor liver function through periodic blood tests and imaging to track progress and detect any signs of worsening.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medically guided plan. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes, particularly regarding weight loss methods, diabetes management, or any medications affecting the liver.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Aim for gradual, sustained weight loss if overweight, rather than rapid crash dieting
- Choose a balanced diet rich in vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein, while limiting refined sugars and processed foods
- Engage in regular physical activity, as advised by your doctor
- Limit or avoid alcohol, as it can add further strain on the liver
- Manage blood sugar, cholesterol, and blood pressure as guided by your doctor
- Avoid unnecessary or unsupervised use of supplements and medications that can stress the liver
- Attend regular follow-up appointments and blood tests as recommended

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice signs suggesting the liver may be struggling, including:

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice)
- Swelling of the abdomen or legs
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness, or difficulty concentrating
- Easy bruising or unexplained bleeding
- Severe or worsening abdominal pain
- Dark urine or pale, clay-coloured stools

These can be signs of progression to more serious liver damage and require urgent medical review rather than self-management.
