## What is Dandruff?

Dandruff is a common scalp condition that causes small flakes of dry skin to shed from the scalp, often visible in the hair or on the shoulders. It is not harmful, not contagious, and does not usually cause hair loss, though it can be itchy and sometimes embarrassing for those affected. Dandruff is generally considered a mild form of seborrhoeic dermatitis, a related skin condition that mainly affects the scalp. Symptoms can come and go and often worsen during cold, dry weather or periods of stress.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of dandruff include:

- White or yellowish flakes of skin on the scalp, hair, eyebrows, or shoulders
- An itchy scalp
- Scalp dryness or, in some cases, an oily scalp with flaking
- Scaly, crusty patches on a baby's scalp, known as cradle cap
- Symptoms that tend to worsen in cold, dry weather or during times of stress

## Causes & Risk Factors

Dandruff can have several contributing causes, including:

- Irritated or oily skin on the scalp
- Dry skin, which tends to flake in smaller, less oily pieces
- Malassezia, a yeast-like fungus that lives naturally on most adults' scalps and can overgrow in some people, feeding on scalp oils
- Sensitivity or irritation from certain hair care products (contact dermatitis)
- Other underlying skin conditions, such as psoriasis or eczema, which can affect the scalp

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

If this does not help, medicated dandruff shampoos containing ingredients such as ketoconazole, pyrithione zinc, selenium sulfide, salicylic acid, or coal tar are commonly recommended and are supported by clinical evidence.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wash hair regularly with a gentle shampoo to reduce oil and flake build-up
- Try a medicated dandruff shampoo if regular washing does not help, following the instructions carefully
- Avoid scratching the scalp, as this can cause irritation or minor skin breaks
- Manage stress where possible, as it can worsen symptoms for some people
- Limit heavy use of styling products that may irritate the scalp
- Maintain a generally balanced diet and good overall skin care routine

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Dandruff itself is rarely a medical emergency, but you should see a doctor if you notice:

- Redness, swelling, warmth, or pus on the scalp, which may indicate an infection
- Scalp symptoms that do not improve despite regular use of medicated shampoos
- Widespread scaling or redness spreading beyond the scalp to the face or other areas
- Hair loss occurring alongside scalp flaking and irritation
- Flaking in an infant that appears thick, widespread, or is not improving with gentle care

These situations are usually not emergencies, but they warrant a proper medical evaluation to confirm the diagnosis and appropriate treatment.
