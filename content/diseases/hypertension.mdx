## What is Hypertension?

Hypertension, or high blood pressure, is a very common condition in which the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries stays persistently higher than normal. Blood pressure is measured as two numbers: systolic (the pressure when the heart beats) and diastolic (the pressure when the heart rests between beats). Over time, consistently high blood pressure puts extra strain on the heart and blood vessels, increasing the risk of serious problems such as heart disease, stroke, and kidney damage. Hypertension often has no symptoms, which is why it is sometimes called a "silent" condition, and is usually found through routine blood pressure checks.

## Symptoms

Most people with hypertension have no symptoms at all, which is why regular checks are so important. In cases of very high blood pressure, or when complications develop, some people may notice:

- Headaches, particularly at the back of the head
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Blurred or disturbed vision
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleeds, in some cases
- Chest discomfort

These symptoms are not reliable indicators of blood pressure level on their own, and many people with significantly raised blood pressure feel completely normal.

## Causes & Risk Factors

In most people, hypertension develops gradually over time without a single identifiable cause, often referred to as primary or essential hypertension. In some cases, it is caused by an underlying condition such as kidney disease, hormonal disorders, or certain medicines, known as secondary hypertension.

Known risk factors include:

- Increasing age
- Family history of high blood pressure
- Being overweight or obese
- A diet high in salt and low in fruits and vegetables
- Lack of regular physical activity
- Excessive alcohol intake
- Smoking
- Chronic stress
- Certain long-term conditions, such as diabetes or kidney disease

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for hypertension usually starts with lifestyle changes such as reducing salt intake, regular exercise, weight management, and limiting alcohol. If blood pressure remains high, doctors may prescribe one or more medicines to help control it, chosen based on individual health factors, and regular monitoring is an important part of ongoing care.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Reduce salt intake in cooking and processed foods
- Eat a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
- Engage in regular physical activity, as advised by your doctor
- Maintain a healthy weight
- Limit alcohol intake
- Avoid smoking
- Practise stress-reduction techniques such as relaxation exercises or mindfulness
- Monitor your blood pressure regularly as advised by your doctor, and keep a record to share at appointments

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical care immediately if you experience a very high blood pressure reading together with any of the following, which can indicate a hypertensive crisis:

- Severe headache
- Chest pain or pressure
- Shortness of breath
- Sudden vision changes or blurred vision
- Difficulty speaking, weakness, or numbness on one side of the body
- Confusion or severe anxiety
- Severe nosebleed that will not stop

These combinations of symptoms can signal a medical emergency and require immediate attention at a hospital or emergency department, rather than self-care or complementary approaches.
