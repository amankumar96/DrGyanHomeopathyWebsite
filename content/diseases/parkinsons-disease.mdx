## What is Parkinson's Disease?

Parkinson's disease is a progressive condition of the nervous system in which nerve cells in a part of the brain that controls movement gradually reduce in number, leading to a drop in a chemical messenger called dopamine. This results in symptoms that typically develop slowly over time, mainly affecting movement, though non-movement symptoms are also common. Parkinson's disease is a serious, lifelong condition that currently has no cure, but a range of treatments can help manage symptoms and maintain quality of life, usually under the ongoing care of a neurologist.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of Parkinson's disease include:

- Tremor, often starting in one hand, especially at rest
- Slowness of movement (bradykinesia)
- Muscle stiffness or rigidity
- Changes in gait, such as shuffling steps or reduced arm swing
- Stooped posture and balance difficulties
- Smaller, cramped handwriting
- Reduced facial expression
- Soft or slurred speech
- Non-movement symptoms such as sleep disturbances, constipation, loss of sense of smell, mood changes, and, in some people, memory or thinking difficulties over time

Symptoms usually develop gradually and may initially be subtle.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of Parkinson's disease is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve a combination of factors, including:

- Loss of nerve cells that produce dopamine in a specific brain region
- Genetic factors, which play a more significant role in a minority of cases, particularly early-onset Parkinson's
- Environmental factors, which researchers continue to study
- Increasing age, as Parkinson's disease is more common in older adults
- Being male, as men are somewhat more likely to be affected than women

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for Parkinson's disease is managed by a neurologist and commonly includes medications such as levodopa and other dopamine-related drugs to help manage symptoms, alongside physiotherapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy as needed. Treatment plans are adjusted over time as the condition progresses.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary, general well-being-focused approach alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should never be used as a reason to stop, skip, or reduce levodopa or any other prescribed Parkinson's medication, as sudden changes to these medications can be dangerous. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take prescribed medication at the times advised by your neurologist, as timing can significantly affect symptom control
- Engage in regular exercise and physiotherapy as recommended, which may help with mobility and balance
- Adapt the home environment to reduce fall risk, such as removing loose rugs and improving lighting
- Eat a balanced diet and stay well hydrated, and discuss protein timing with your doctor if it affects medication absorption
- Seek support for mood changes, as depression and anxiety are common and treatable
- Join a support group or connect with others managing Parkinson's disease for practical and emotional support
- Keep a symptom and medication diary to share with your neurology team

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical attention if you or someone you care for experiences:

- Sudden, severe worsening of stiffness or inability to move, especially combined with high fever and confusion, which can indicate a rare but serious reaction related to missed or abruptly stopped medication
- Difficulty swallowing that leads to choking, or sudden inability to swallow safely
- A fall resulting in injury, or frequent falls
- New or worsening confusion, hallucinations, or significant mood changes
- Signs of infection, such as fever, alongside worsening movement symptoms

Never stop or alter levodopa or other prescribed Parkinson's medication abruptly, as this can cause dangerous worsening of symptoms. Always contact your neurologist or doctor before making any changes, and seek emergency care for any sudden, severe deterioration.
