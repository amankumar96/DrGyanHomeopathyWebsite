## What is Cheilitis?

Cheilitis refers to inflammation of the lips, which can cause them to become dry, cracked, scaly, swollen, or sore. It can affect the whole lip or just the corners of the mouth, and may be a short-lived irritation from weather or licking the lips, or a more persistent condition linked to an allergy, infection, or underlying skin disorder. Cheilitis is common and usually not serious, though it can be uncomfortable and, in some cases, a sign of an underlying issue that needs attention.

## Symptoms

Common features of cheilitis include:

- Dry, flaky, or scaly skin on the lips
- Cracking, splitting, or fissures, particularly at the corners of the mouth
- Redness, swelling, or soreness of the lips
- A burning or stinging sensation
- Crusting or bleeding in more severe cases
- Lips that feel tight or painful when opening the mouth wide

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cheilitis can have several causes, and sometimes more than one factor is involved. Common causes and risk factors include:

- Dry or cold weather, wind, or excessive sun exposure
- Habitual lip licking or biting, which worsens dryness
- Allergic or irritant reactions to lip products, toothpaste, or certain foods
- Fungal infections (such as Candida) or bacterial infections, especially at the corners of the mouth
- Nutritional deficiencies, including iron and B vitamins
- Underlying skin conditions such as eczema or psoriasis affecting the lips
- Ill-fitting dentures or excessive saliva pooling at the mouth corners

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Apply a plain, fragrance-free lip balm or petroleum-based ointment regularly
- Avoid licking or biting the lips, as saliva can worsen dryness
- Protect lips from sun, wind, and cold with a suitable balm or covering
- Stay well hydrated
- Avoid known irritants such as certain toothpastes, cosmetics, or flavoured lip products
- Eat a balanced diet to help avoid nutritional deficiencies linked to lip cracking
- Keep dentures well-fitted and clean if they may be contributing

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- Lip sores, cracks, or swelling that do not improve after a couple of weeks of self-care
- Signs of infection such as increasing redness, pus, warmth, or spreading swelling
- Severe pain, bleeding, or ulceration of the lips
- Associated fever or feeling generally unwell
- A persistent lip lump, patch, or sore that does not heal, which should always be checked
- Difficulty eating, drinking, or opening the mouth due to pain or swelling

These situations may need medical assessment and treatment beyond simple self-care.
