## What is Optic Neuritis?

Optic neuritis is inflammation of the optic nerve, the nerve that carries visual signals from the eye to the brain. It typically affects one eye, causing pain and a reduction in vision that can range from mild blurring to significant vision loss. Optic neuritis can occur on its own or be associated with underlying conditions affecting the nervous system, such as multiple sclerosis. Prompt specialist assessment is important, as the underlying cause and appropriate treatment can vary considerably.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of optic neuritis include:

- Pain in or around the eye, often worsened by eye movement
- Blurred, dimmed, or reduced vision in one eye, less commonly both
- Reduced ability to distinguish colours, particularly red and green
- A blind spot or patch of missing vision
- Flashing or flickering lights in the affected eye's field of vision
- Symptoms typically developing over hours to a few days

## Causes & Risk Factors

Optic neuritis results from inflammation damaging the protective covering (myelin) of the optic nerve, disrupting the transmission of visual signals. Possible causes and associations include:

- Multiple sclerosis, a condition affecting the brain and spinal cord, with which optic neuritis is closely linked in some people
- Neuromyelitis optica and other autoimmune conditions affecting the nervous system
- Viral or other infections
- Certain autoimmune or inflammatory conditions affecting the body more broadly
- Some medications, in rare cases
- No identifiable cause, in a proportion of cases

Optic neuritis is more common in younger adults, particularly women, though it can affect anyone.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all specialist follow-up appointments and recommended scans or tests
- Protect your eyes from bright light if sensitivity is present
- Keep a record of any new or changing symptoms to share with your specialist
- Rest the eyes when fatigued, especially during reading or screen use
- Manage overall health conditions, such as blood pressure, as advised by your doctor
- Seek support if vision changes are affecting daily activities such as driving

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Optic neuritis itself typically warrants urgent specialist assessment rather than routine care. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:

- Sudden or rapidly worsening vision loss in one or both eyes
- Eye pain combined with vision changes
- Vision loss alongside other neurological symptoms, such as weakness, numbness, slurred speech, or loss of balance
- Any sudden, significant change in vision, which should always be assessed urgently rather than waiting to see if it improves

Prompt evaluation helps identify the cause and begin appropriate treatment as early as possible.
