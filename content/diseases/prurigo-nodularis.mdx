## What is Prurigo Nodularis?

Prurigo nodularis is a chronic skin condition in which firm, raised, intensely itchy nodules develop, most commonly on the arms and legs, though other areas can be affected. It arises from a persistent itch-scratch cycle: itching leads to scratching or rubbing, which thickens the skin into nodules, and the thickened skin itself can become more itchy, perpetuating the cycle. The condition can last for months or years and can significantly affect sleep and quality of life because of the intensity of the itching.

## Symptoms

Common features of prurigo nodularis include:

- Multiple firm, raised, dome-shaped nodules, often several millimetres to a few centimetres across
- Intense itching, which can be severe and difficult to control
- Nodules most commonly on the arms, legs, and sometimes the trunk, typically in areas that are easy to scratch
- Darker, lighter, or scarred skin where nodules have been present for a long time
- Skin that may be cracked, crusted, or thickened from repeated scratching
- Disturbed sleep due to night-time itching

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of prurigo nodularis is not fully understood. It is believed to involve abnormal nerve signalling in the skin that amplifies the sensation of itch, combined with the physical effects of repeated scratching. It is often associated with other conditions rather than occurring in isolation.

Factors linked to prurigo nodularis include:

- Pre-existing skin conditions such as eczema or dry skin
- Chronic kidney disease or liver disease
- Nerve-related conditions affecting sensation
- Psychological factors such as stress, anxiety, or depression
- Insect bites or skin injuries that trigger an initial itch-scratch cycle
- In some cases, no clear underlying cause is identified

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep nails short and consider wearing gloves at night to reduce scratching during sleep
- Apply fragrance-free moisturisers regularly to reduce dryness that can worsen itching
- Use cool compresses or gentle pressure instead of scratching when itch flares
- Wear soft, breathable fabrics and avoid overheating, which can worsen itch
- Try to identify and manage stress, as it may intensify itching for some people
- Avoid known irritants such as harsh soaps or very hot showers

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention if you notice:

- Signs of skin infection such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever around the nodules
- Rapidly spreading or worsening nodules despite treatment
- Unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or feeling generally unwell alongside the itching, which may need investigation for an underlying cause
- Itching so severe it is preventing sleep or daily functioning over a prolonged period

These situations warrant prompt review by a doctor or dermatologist rather than self-management.
