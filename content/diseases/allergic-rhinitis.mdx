## What is Allergic Rhinitis?

Allergic rhinitis is inflammation and irritation of the inside of the nose caused by an allergic reaction, typically to airborne substances such as pollen, dust mites, mould, or animal dander. When someone with a sensitised immune system breathes in one of these allergens, the body releases chemicals, including histamine, that produce the familiar symptoms of sneezing, congestion, and a runny or itchy nose. Allergic rhinitis may occur seasonally (often called hay fever, linked to pollen) or year-round (perennial, often linked to indoor allergens like dust mites or pets).

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of allergic rhinitis include:

- Sneezing, often in bursts
- Runny nose with clear, watery discharge
- Nasal congestion or a blocked feeling in the nose
- Itching in the nose, throat, or mouth
- Watery, itchy, or red eyes (allergic conjunctivitis)
- Postnasal drip leading to a sore throat or cough
- Fatigue, headache, and general tiredness, especially with ongoing symptoms
- Dark circles under the eyes in some cases

## Causes & Risk Factors

Allergic rhinitis occurs when the immune system overreacts to substances that are normally harmless. Common triggers include:

- Tree, grass, and weed pollen (often worse on hot, dry, windy days)
- House dust mites
- Mould spores
- Animal dander from pets such as cats and dogs
- Cockroach droppings in some environments

Risk factors include having a family history of allergies, asthma, or eczema, since allergic conditions often run together and tend to be hereditary. Exposure to tobacco smoke and living or working in environments with high allergen levels can also increase the likelihood of symptoms.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of allergic rhinitis typically includes allergen avoidance, antihistamines, nasal corticosteroid sprays (often considered the most effective medical treatment), decongestants for short-term use, and in some cases, allergy immunotherapy (allergy shots or sublingual tablets) prescribed by a doctor or allergy specialist.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside these treatments, often seeking general support alongside their prescribed care. Homeopathy should not be seen as a replacement for medically recommended allergy treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Try to identify and reduce exposure to your specific triggers, such as pollen, dust, or pet dander
- Keep windows closed during high pollen periods and consider showering after being outdoors
- Use dust-mite-proof covers on pillows and mattresses if dust mites are a trigger
- Vacuum and clean indoor spaces regularly to reduce allergen build-up
- Wash bedding frequently in hot water
- Consider a saline nasal rinse to help clear allergens and mucus from the nose
- Keep pets out of bedrooms if animal dander is a known trigger

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most allergic rhinitis symptoms are manageable, but seek prompt or urgent medical attention if you experience:

- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or chest tightness
- Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat
- Symptoms that suddenly worsen despite previously effective treatment
- High fever, facial pain, or thick discoloured nasal discharge, which may suggest a sinus infection rather than simple allergy
- Severe headache or facial pain that does not improve

If you experience signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis), such as sudden difficulty breathing, swelling, or dizziness, seek emergency medical care immediately.
