## What is Acromegaly?

Acromegaly is a rare condition in which the body produces too much growth hormone, most often due to a benign (non-cancerous) tumour on the pituitary gland at the base of the brain. In adults, because bone growth has already finished, the excess hormone causes bones to thicken and soft tissues to gradually enlarge, rather than causing an increase in height as it would in a growing child. Acromegaly typically develops slowly, often becoming noticeable between the ages of 30 and 50, and is generally treatable once correctly diagnosed by a specialist.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of acromegaly include:

- Gradual enlargement of the hands and feet
- Enlarged or coarsened facial features, including the nose, lips, and jaw
- Deepening of the voice
- Fatigue and excessive sweating
- Numbness or tingling in the hands
- Joint pain
- Headaches
- Thickened, oily skin
- Sleep apnoea
- Menstrual irregularities in women, and erectile dysfunction in men

Because changes develop gradually over years, acromegaly is sometimes only recognised when old photographs are compared with recent ones.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Acromegaly is most commonly caused by a non-cancerous tumour (adenoma) of the pituitary gland that leads to excessive growth hormone production. It is generally not considered an inherited condition and most often occurs without a clear family pattern. Because it develops slowly, there is often a delay of several years between the start of symptoms and diagnosis.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Acromegaly requires specialist conventional treatment, which may include surgical removal of the pituitary tumour, radiotherapy to reduce hormone-producing tissue, and medicines to control growth hormone levels. These treatments are evidence-based and are directed by an endocrinologist, often working with a neurosurgeon, and typically involve long-term specialist follow-up to monitor hormone levels and related health risks such as diabetes and heart disease.

Homeopathy has no established role in treating the underlying pituitary tumour or hormone excess and should only ever be considered, if at all, as an additional general-support measure alongside this required specialist care — never as a substitute for it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled specialist follow-up appointments and hormone monitoring tests
- Maintain a balanced diet and healthy weight to help manage associated risks such as diabetes
- Monitor for symptoms of sleep apnoea, such as loud snoring or daytime sleepiness, and report these to your doctor
- Engage in regular, moderate physical activity as advised by your care team, being mindful of joint discomfort
- Keep a record of physical changes or new symptoms to discuss at follow-up visits
- Seek support for emotional wellbeing, as changes in appearance and ongoing treatment can affect mental health

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Acromegaly itself is usually diagnosed and managed on a non-emergency, specialist basis, but you should seek prompt or urgent medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden, severe headache, especially with vision changes or double vision
- Sudden loss of vision or significant visual field changes
- Signs of a possible complication such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, or symptoms of uncontrolled diabetes (such as extreme thirst, frequent urination, or confusion)
- Any new or worsening neurological symptoms, such as weakness or confusion

These can indicate a serious complication involving the pituitary tumour or associated health risks, and should be assessed urgently by a doctor or at an emergency department.
