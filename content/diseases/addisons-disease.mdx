## What is Addison's Disease?

Addison's disease, also known as primary adrenal insufficiency, is a rare condition in which the adrenal glands — small glands located above each kidney — fail to produce enough of certain essential hormones, particularly cortisol and aldosterone. These hormones play a vital role in regulating the body's response to stress, blood pressure, and salt and water balance. Addison's disease is a long-term condition that requires lifelong management, almost always under the care of an endocrinologist, and cannot currently be cured, though it can usually be effectively managed with proper treatment.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of Addison's disease, which often develop gradually, include:

- Severe fatigue and muscle weakness
- Darkening of the skin, particularly in skin folds, scars, and gums
- Loss of appetite and unintentional weight loss
- Dizziness or fainting, especially on standing
- Low mood, anxiety, or irritability
- Nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain
- Salt cravings
- Joint or muscle pain

Because these symptoms can be vague and develop slowly, Addison's disease is sometimes diagnosed only after symptoms have been present for some time.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The most common cause of Addison's disease is an autoimmune process, where the body's immune system mistakenly attacks and damages the adrenal glands. Other, less common causes include certain infections such as tuberculosis, HIV-related infections, or meningitis affecting the adrenal glands, some genetic conditions present from birth, and surgical removal of the adrenal glands. A family history of autoimmune conditions may increase the likelihood of developing Addison's disease.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for Addison's disease involves daily hormone replacement medicines, such as hydrocortisone, prednisolone, or fludrocortisone, prescribed and closely monitored by an endocrinologist, usually for life. Patients are generally advised to carry a steroid emergency card and may be given guidance, and sometimes an emergency injection kit, for use if they become seriously unwell, to prevent a life-threatening adrenal crisis.

Homeopathy has no established role in correcting adrenal hormone deficiency and should only ever be considered, if at all, as an additional, general-support measure alongside this required hormone replacement therapy — never as a substitute. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes. Never reduce or stop prescribed steroid replacement medication without your specialist's explicit guidance.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take hormone replacement medication exactly as prescribed, at the same times each day
- Carry a steroid emergency card or medical alert identification at all times
- Discuss with your doctor in advance how to adjust medication during illness, injury, surgery, or significant stress ("sick day rules")
- Stay well hydrated, and be mindful of salt intake as advised by your specialist
- Keep a small emergency kit or extra medication supply when travelling
- Inform family members, close contacts, and employers about the condition and what to do in an emergency
- Attend all scheduled specialist follow-up appointments

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Addison's disease carries a risk of adrenal crisis, a life-threatening emergency. Seek emergency medical care immediately if you or someone with Addison's disease experiences:

- Severe weakness or sudden collapse
- Sudden, severe pain in the lower back, abdomen, or legs
- Severe vomiting or diarrhoea, with inability to keep medication down
- Signs of severe dehydration
- Confusion, extreme drowsiness, or loss of consciousness
- Very low blood pressure or fainting

An adrenal crisis can be fatal without prompt treatment, which usually involves an emergency injection of steroid medication. If you suspect an adrenal crisis, seek emergency care immediately — do not wait to see if symptoms improve, and do not attempt to manage this with homeopathy or any home remedy.
