## What is Tinea Corporis?

Tinea corporis, commonly known as ringworm of the body, is a fungal skin infection that causes a distinctive, often ring-shaped, itchy rash. Despite its name, it has nothing to do with worms — it is caused by a group of fungi called dermatophytes, which thrive on the outer layer of skin. It is a common and generally mild condition that usually responds well to antifungal treatment, though it can be persistent if not treated adequately or if reinfection occurs.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of tinea corporis include:

- A red, pink, or scaly patch that often grows outward from a central point, forming a ring shape with a clearer centre
- Itching in the affected area
- Slightly raised, scaly edges around the patch
- Patches that may appear on the trunk, arms, legs, or other areas of the body
- Sometimes several patches merging together

The rash can vary in appearance depending on skin tone and may not always form a perfect ring.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Tinea corporis is caused by dermatophyte fungi, which can spread through:

- Direct skin-to-skin contact with an infected person
- Contact with infected animals, such as cats, dogs, or farm animals
- Touching contaminated objects, such as towels, clothing, bedding, or gym equipment
- Warm, humid environments that favour fungal growth

Risk factors include living in close contact with others, such as in shared households or sports teams, excessive sweating, having a weakened immune system, and close contact with animals.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep the affected area clean and dry, as fungi thrive in warm, moist conditions
- Avoid sharing towels, clothing, or bedding with others while infected
- Wash clothes, towels, and bedding regularly in hot water during treatment
- Avoid scratching the rash, as this can spread the infection to other areas of the body
- Wear loose, breathable clothing
- Have pets checked by a vet if they have patches of hair loss or scaly skin
- Complete the full course of antifungal treatment, even if the rash looks better, to prevent recurrence

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Tinea corporis is usually mild, but you should see a doctor promptly if you notice:

- The rash spreading rapidly or covering large areas of the body
- Signs of secondary bacterial infection, such as increasing redness, swelling, warmth, or pus
- No improvement after using an over-the-counter antifungal treatment as directed
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside the rash
- A weakened immune system and a new or worsening fungal rash

These situations may need prescription-strength treatment or further assessment, so a timely doctor's visit is advisable.
