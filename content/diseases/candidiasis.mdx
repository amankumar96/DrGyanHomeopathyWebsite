## What is Candidiasis?

Candidiasis is an infection caused by an overgrowth of Candida, a type of yeast that normally lives harmlessly on the skin and inside the mouth, gut, and vagina. When conditions allow it to multiply beyond its usual levels, it can cause infection in different parts of the body. Two common forms are oral thrush, which affects the inside of the mouth, and cutaneous (skin) candidiasis, which typically develops in warm, moist, creased areas of skin such as the armpits, groin, and skin folds. Vaginal candidiasis, commonly known as vaginal thrush, is another well-known form, though it is not the main focus of this page. Candidiasis is usually straightforward to treat, though it can recur, particularly if the underlying triggers are not addressed.

## Symptoms

**Oral thrush (mouth)**
- White patches inside the mouth, on the tongue, or inner cheeks, which can sometimes be wiped off to reveal a red, sometimes bleeding, area underneath
- Redness and soreness inside the mouth
- Cracked skin at the corners of the mouth
- A changed or unpleasant taste, or loss of taste
- Soreness that can make eating or drinking uncomfortable
- In babies, a white coating on the tongue resembling cottage cheese, which may be accompanied by difficulty feeding

**Skin-fold (cutaneous) candidiasis**
- A red, sometimes itchy or painful rash in warm, moist skin folds such as the armpits, groin, or under the breasts
- Scaling, soreness, or a burning sensation in the affected area
- Moist, macerated skin with a well-defined, sometimes slightly raised edge
- On darker skin, the rash may look less obviously red and more subtle

## Causes & Risk Factors

Candidiasis develops when Candida yeast, usually kept in check by the immune system and the body's normal balance of microbes, multiplies beyond its usual levels. Known risk factors include:

- Taking antibiotics, which can disturb the natural balance of microbes
- Using inhaled or oral corticosteroids
- Poorly controlled diabetes, which raises skin and mucosal sugar levels that yeast can thrive on
- A weakened immune system, including from certain illnesses, chemotherapy, or some medicines
- Dentures that fit poorly, dry mouth, or poor oral hygiene
- Smoking
- Warm, moist, or poorly ventilated skin folds, often made worse by tight clothing, obesity, or excessive sweating
- Pregnancy and hormonal changes

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for candidiasis usually involves antifungal medication, matched to the site of infection. Oral thrush is commonly treated with an antifungal gel or liquid applied inside the mouth, while skin-fold candidiasis is usually treated with an antifungal cream or powder. More widespread or stubborn infections may need an oral antifungal tablet, prescribed and monitored by a doctor. Keeping the affected area clean and dry, and managing underlying risk factors such as blood sugar control, is an important part of treatment and prevention.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep skin folds clean and thoroughly dry, especially after bathing or sweating
- Wear loose-fitting, breathable clothing, and change out of damp or sweaty clothes promptly
- Practise good oral hygiene, including regular brushing and, where relevant, proper cleaning of dentures
- If you have diabetes, keep blood sugar levels well controlled, as this reduces the risk of recurrent infections
- Avoid unnecessary or prolonged use of antibiotics and steroid creams where possible, and follow your doctor's guidance on their use
- Maintain a generally healthy, balanced diet to support overall immunity

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most cases of candidiasis are mild and respond well to treatment, but see a doctor if:

- Oral thrush or a skin infection does not improve after using an over-the-counter or prescribed antifungal treatment as directed
- Candidiasis keeps recurring, which may point to an underlying condition such as undiagnosed or poorly controlled diabetes, or a weakened immune system that needs investigation
- You have difficulty swallowing, or pain when swallowing, alongside mouth symptoms, which can suggest the infection has spread further down the throat
- You have a weakened immune system (for example, due to chemotherapy, HIV, or other conditions) and develop symptoms of candidiasis, as these infections can become more serious and need prompt medical assessment
- You notice fever, spreading redness, or feeling generally unwell alongside a skin or mouth infection

A doctor or pharmacist can confirm the diagnosis and recommend appropriate antifungal treatment, and investigate further if infections are severe or recurrent.
