## What is Diabetes Type II?

Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body either does not produce enough insulin or does not use it effectively, a state often called insulin resistance. This leads to higher-than-normal levels of glucose in the blood. It is the more common form of diabetes and typically develops in adults, though it is increasingly seen in younger people too. Type 2 diabetes usually develops gradually and, while it is a lifelong condition for most people, it can often be managed well with lifestyle changes and medication.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of type 2 diabetes include:

- Increased thirst
- Frequent urination, particularly at night
- Persistent tiredness
- Unexplained weight loss, in some cases
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of cuts or wounds
- Frequent infections, such as thrush
- Tingling or numbness in the hands or feet

Symptoms often develop slowly over months or years, and some people have no noticeable symptoms at diagnosis, which is why routine screening is important for those at higher risk.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Type 2 diabetes develops from a combination of genetic and lifestyle factors that affect how the body produces and responds to insulin. Known risk factors include:

- Being overweight or obese, particularly with abdominal weight
- A family history of type 2 diabetes
- Age, with risk increasing after 45
- Certain ethnic backgrounds, including South Asian, Black African, and Caribbean heritage
- Physical inactivity
- High blood pressure or abnormal cholesterol levels
- A history of gestational diabetes
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Follow a balanced diet, with attention to carbohydrate intake and portion sizes
- Engage in regular physical activity, as advised by your doctor
- Aim to maintain a healthy weight, as modest weight loss can meaningfully improve blood glucose control
- Monitor blood glucose levels as recommended by your care team
- Attend regular check-ups, including eye, foot, and kidney screenings
- Limit sugary drinks and highly processed foods
- Avoid smoking and limit alcohol intake

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Signs of very high blood glucose: extreme thirst, frequent urination, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, confusion, or drowsiness, which can indicate a serious complication requiring emergency care
- Signs of low blood glucose (hypoglycaemia) if taking certain medications or insulin: shakiness, sweating, confusion, slurred speech, or loss of consciousness
- Fruity-smelling breath or rapid breathing, which can indicate diabetic ketoacidosis in some cases
- Sores or wounds, particularly on the feet, that are not healing or appear infected
- Sudden changes in vision
- Chest pain, breathlessness, or symptoms suggestive of a heart attack or stroke

These situations can be medical emergencies, so contact your doctor or emergency services without delay rather than waiting or self-treating.
