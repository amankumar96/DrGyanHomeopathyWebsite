## What is Hyperthyroidism?

Hyperthyroidism is a condition in which the thyroid gland, located at the front of the neck, produces more thyroid hormone than the body needs. Thyroid hormone helps regulate metabolism, so when levels are too high, many of the body's processes speed up. This can affect heart rate, energy levels, weight, and mood, among other things. The most common cause is Graves' disease, an autoimmune condition, though other causes include thyroid nodules and thyroid inflammation. Hyperthyroidism is generally manageable with appropriate medical treatment, though the approach depends on the underlying cause and individual factors.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of hyperthyroidism include:

- Unintended weight loss, despite normal or increased appetite
- A rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Feeling anxious, irritable, or restless
- Trembling hands
- Increased sweating and sensitivity to heat
- Difficulty sleeping
- Tiredness and muscle weakness
- Frequent bowel movements
- Swelling at the front of the neck (goitre)
- Changes in the eyes, such as bulging or irritation, particularly in Graves' disease
- Irregular menstrual periods in women

Symptoms can develop gradually and may be mistaken for stress or anxiety before hyperthyroidism is diagnosed.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The most common cause of hyperthyroidism is Graves' disease, an autoimmune condition in which the immune system mistakenly stimulates the thyroid gland to overproduce hormone. Other causes include:

- Toxic nodular goitre or thyroid nodules that produce excess hormone independently
- Thyroiditis, inflammation of the thyroid gland that can temporarily release excess stored hormone
- Excessive iodine intake, in some cases
- Taking too much thyroid hormone replacement medicine

Risk factors include being female, having a family history of thyroid or autoimmune conditions, and having other autoimmune disorders.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for hyperthyroidism depends on the underlying cause and may include antithyroid medicines to reduce hormone production, beta-blockers to control symptoms like rapid heartbeat and tremor, radioactive iodine treatment, or surgery to remove part or all of the thyroid gland in some cases. Regular blood tests are used to monitor hormone levels and adjust treatment.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this prescribed treatment, often in the hope of supporting general well-being. Homeopathy should never be used as a replacement for antithyroid medicines, and prescribed thyroid treatment should never be stopped or changed without your doctor's guidance. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes to your care plan.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled blood tests to monitor thyroid hormone levels
- Avoid excessive iodine intake unless advised otherwise by your doctor
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, as stress can worsen symptoms for some people
- Get adequate rest, as hyperthyroidism can be tiring on the body
- Protect your eyes with sunglasses and lubricating drops if you have eye involvement from Graves' disease, as advised by your doctor
- Avoid smoking, as it can worsen thyroid eye disease
- Eat a balanced diet to help manage weight changes

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- A very fast or irregular heartbeat, chest pain, or palpitations
- High fever along with a rapid heart rate and confusion, which could suggest a rare but serious emergency called thyroid storm
- Severe agitation, confusion, or difficulty staying calm
- Significant eye pain, bulging, or vision changes
- Severe diarrhoea, vomiting, or signs of dehydration
- Sudden worsening of symptoms, especially after an infection, injury, or surgery

Thyroid storm is a medical emergency requiring immediate hospital care. Never stop or change your prescribed thyroid medicine without speaking to your doctor first, even if you are considering complementary approaches.
