## What is Fissure-in-Ano?

Fissure-in-ano, commonly known as an anal fissure, is a small tear or cut in the lining of the anal canal, the short passage through which stool leaves the body. It is a common cause of anal pain and bleeding, particularly during or shortly after passing stool. Fissures can be acute, healing within a few weeks with simple measures, or chronic, persisting for longer periods and sometimes requiring more active medical treatment.

## Symptoms

Typical symptoms of an anal fissure include:

- Sharp, often severe pain during or immediately after passing stool
- A burning or stinging sensation that may last from minutes to hours after a bowel movement
- Bright red blood, usually seen on the stool surface or on toilet paper
- A visible small tear or crack in the skin around the anus
- A small skin tag or lump near the fissure in chronic cases
- Anxiety or reluctance to pass stool due to anticipated pain, which can worsen constipation

## Causes & Risk Factors

Anal fissures most commonly result from trauma to the anal canal lining. Common causes and risk factors include:

- Passing hard or large stools due to constipation
- Chronic diarrhoea
- Straining excessively during bowel movements
- Childbirth, which can cause fissures in some women
- Reduced blood flow to the anal area, which can affect healing
- Increased tightness (spasm) of the anal sphincter muscle, which may both cause and result from a fissure
- Underlying conditions such as inflammatory bowel disease, in some persistent or unusual cases

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Increase dietary fibre through fruits, vegetables, and whole grains to keep stools soft
- Drink plenty of water throughout the day
- Avoid straining during bowel movements
- Respond promptly to the urge to pass stool rather than delaying
- Keep the anal area clean and gently dry after bowel movements
- Consider warm sitz baths, which can help ease discomfort and relax the anal muscle
- Stay physically active, as this supports regular bowel movements

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Heavy or persistent rectal bleeding, rather than small amounts of bright red blood
- Signs of infection, such as fever, increasing pain, swelling, or discharge of pus around the anus
- A fissure that has not healed after several weeks of self-care
- Severe pain that significantly limits daily activity
- Unexplained weight loss, changes in bowel habit, or persistent abdominal pain alongside the fissure

Significant rectal bleeding or signs of infection need prompt medical attention, so do not delay seeking care in these situations.
