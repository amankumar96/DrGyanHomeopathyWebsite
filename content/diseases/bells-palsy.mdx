## What is Bell's Palsy?

Bell's palsy is a condition causing sudden, usually temporary, weakness or paralysis of the muscles on one side of the face. It happens when the nerve controlling facial muscles, the facial nerve, becomes inflamed, swollen, or compressed. It can affect people of any age but is more common in adults. Most people begin to notice some recovery within a few weeks, and many recover fully over the following months, though a small number of people are left with some residual weakness.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of Bell's palsy include:

- Sudden weakness or drooping on one side of the face, often noticed within hours to a day or two
- Difficulty closing the eye on the affected side
- Drooling or difficulty controlling saliva on the affected side
- Loss or distortion of taste on part of the tongue
- Increased sensitivity to sound in one ear
- Pain around the jaw or behind the ear on the affected side, sometimes before the weakness appears
- Dryness of the eye or mouth on the affected side

Because the weakness in Bell's palsy affects only the face and comes on relatively quickly, and because stroke can look similar, urgent medical assessment is essential to distinguish between them.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of Bell's palsy is not always clear, but it is thought to usually result from inflammation and swelling of the facial nerve, often following a viral infection, which compresses the nerve as it travels through a narrow bony canal in the skull.

Known associations and risk factors include:

- Recent viral infection, including cold sores (herpes simplex) and other viruses
- Pregnancy, particularly in the third trimester or shortly after delivery
- Diabetes
- Upper respiratory infections
- A family history of Bell's palsy, in some cases

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Protect the affected eye with lubricating drops and, if advised, taping it closed at night
- Gently massage the facial muscles as advised by a physiotherapist, to help maintain tone
- Eat smaller, manageable bites of food and chew carefully if the mouth is affected
- Keep follow-up appointments to monitor recovery
- Avoid exposing the affected side of the face to excessive cold or wind
- Be patient with recovery, as it can take weeks to months, and progress varies between individuals

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek emergency medical attention immediately for any sudden facial weakness or drooping, as this requires urgent evaluation to rule out stroke, which is a medical emergency. Call for emergency help right away if facial weakness is accompanied by:

- Weakness or numbness in an arm or leg
- Sudden difficulty speaking, slurred speech, or confusion
- Sudden vision changes
- Severe headache with no known cause
- Loss of balance or coordination

Even without these additional symptoms, any new facial weakness should be assessed by a doctor as soon as possible, since prompt treatment for Bell's palsy, if that is the diagnosis, can support recovery, and other serious causes must first be excluded.
