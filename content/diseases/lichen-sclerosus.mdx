## What is Lichen Sclerosus?

Lichen sclerosus is a long-term skin condition that causes patches of skin to become thin, white, and slightly wrinkled or shiny in texture. It most often affects the genital and anal skin, though it can occasionally appear elsewhere on the body. The exact cause is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve the immune system, and it tends to follow a pattern of flares and calmer periods. While it cannot currently be cured, it is generally a manageable condition, and most people are able to keep symptoms comfortable with appropriate treatment and regular follow-up with a doctor.

## Symptoms

Common features of lichen sclerosus include:

- Patches of thin, pale, or white skin, which may look smooth, shiny, or slightly crinkled
- Itching, which can range from mild to persistent and troublesome
- Soreness or discomfort in the affected area
- Skin that bruises, tears, or blisters easily
- Changes in skin texture over time, which can occasionally lead to narrowing of genital openings if untreated
- Pain or discomfort during urination or intimacy in some cases

Symptoms vary in severity, and some people notice very mild changes, while others experience more persistent discomfort.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of lichen sclerosus is not fully known, but it is believed to involve the immune system reacting against the skin in a particular area, possibly influenced by hormonal factors and genetic predisposition. Known associations include:

- A personal or family history of autoimmune conditions, such as thyroid disease
- Hormonal changes, as the condition is more common after menopause in women
- Skin that has experienced previous injury or irritation in the affected area
- Occurring in girls before puberty, in whom it sometimes improves after puberty

Lichen sclerosus is not caused by poor hygiene and is not related to any sexually transmitted infection.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use plain, fragrance-free emollients regularly on the affected area to help protect and soothe the skin
- Avoid soaps, bubble baths, and scented products in the genital area
- Wear loose, breathable cotton underwear and avoid tight clothing that may irritate the skin
- Pat the area dry gently after washing rather than rubbing
- Apply prescribed treatment exactly as advised by your doctor, even once symptoms improve, to help maintain control
- Keep up with scheduled follow-up appointments for ongoing monitoring
- Avoid scratching, as this can lead to further skin damage and discomfort

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor promptly, rather than waiting for a routine appointment, if you notice:

- A new lump, ulcer, or sore that does not heal
- Any unusual change in the texture, colour, or shape of the affected skin that is different from your usual pattern
- Increasing pain, bleeding, or discharge from the area
- Difficulty or pain passing urine, or narrowing of the urinary or genital opening
- Symptoms that are not improving despite following your prescribed treatment

Lichen sclerosus is generally a manageable, long-term condition, but because there is a small increased likelihood of skin changes developing in the affected area over time, any new or unusual change should be checked by a doctor promptly as part of routine, ongoing care rather than cause for alarm.
