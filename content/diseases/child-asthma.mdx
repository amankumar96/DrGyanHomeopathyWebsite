## What is Child Asthma?

Asthma is a common long-term condition in which the airways in the lungs become inflamed, narrowed, and sensitive to certain triggers, making it harder for air to move in and out of the lungs. It is one of the most common chronic conditions in childhood and a frequent cause of missed school days. Asthma symptoms can vary in frequency and severity from child to child, and episodes of worsening symptoms, sometimes called asthma attacks or flares, can range from mild to severe. Childhood asthma requires ongoing assessment and management by a paediatrician or specialist, and this page is intended as general education only, not a substitute for that care.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of asthma in children include:

- Wheezing, a whistling sound when breathing, particularly on breathing out
- Coughing, which may be worse at night or early morning
- Shortness of breath or rapid breathing
- Chest tightness or a feeling of chest discomfort
- Difficulty finishing sentences or feeding due to breathlessness in severe cases
- Visible pulling in of the skin around the chest or neck during breathing difficulty
- Fatigue or reduced activity due to breathing problems

Symptoms often fluctuate and may be triggered or worsened by specific factors such as exercise, cold air, or allergens.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of asthma is not fully understood, but it is thought to result from a combination of genetic and environmental factors. Risk factors and common triggers include:

- A family history of asthma, allergies, or eczema
- Having other allergic conditions, such as eczema or allergic rhinitis
- Exposure to tobacco smoke, including before birth
- Premature birth or low birth weight
- Exposure to air pollution
- Being overweight
- Common triggers include dust mites, pollen, mould, animal dander, cold air, exercise, strong emotions, and viral respiratory infections

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of childhood asthma is typically guided by a written asthma action plan from a doctor, and usually involves reliever inhalers for quick relief of symptoms and preventer inhalers taken regularly to reduce airway inflammation and prevent flares. Spacer devices are often used to help children use inhalers effectively, and in some cases nebulisers or additional medication may be needed, particularly for more severe asthma.

Some parents choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this prescribed care, often seeking general support alongside their child's treatment plan. Homeopathy should not be seen as a replacement for a child's prescribed asthma medication or action plan, and ongoing specialist or paediatric follow-up remains essential. Continue your child's current treatment and consult their doctor before making any changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Help your child identify and avoid their specific asthma triggers where possible
- Keep the home free of tobacco smoke and reduce exposure to dust, mould, and strong fragrances
- Ensure your child follows their prescribed asthma action plan, including correct inhaler and spacer technique
- Encourage regular physical activity suited to your child's asthma control, as advised by their doctor
- Keep bedding clean and consider dust-mite-proof covers if dust mites are a known trigger
- Keep track of symptom patterns and triggers to share with your child's doctor
- Ensure vaccinations, including for respiratory infections, are up to date as recommended by your paediatrician

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Asthma in children can sometimes become a medical emergency. Seek immediate emergency medical help if your child shows:

- Severe difficulty breathing, rapid breathing, or visible straining to breathe
- Bluish discoloration of the lips, face, or fingertips
- Inability to speak, feed, or complete sentences due to breathlessness
- Severe anxiety, distress, or decreased alertness and responsiveness
- No improvement, or worsening symptoms, after using their reliever inhaler as directed in their asthma action plan

If your child does not have their reliever inhaler available during a severe episode, or symptoms are rapidly worsening, seek emergency care immediately rather than waiting. Always follow up with your child's paediatrician after any emergency asthma episode, and maintain regular review appointments even when symptoms seem well controlled.
