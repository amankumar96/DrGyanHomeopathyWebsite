## What is Backache?

Backache, or back pain, refers to discomfort felt anywhere along the spine, from the neck down to the lower back, and is one of the most common reasons people seek medical advice. It can range from a mild, dull ache to sudden, severe pain, and may be classified as acute if it comes on suddenly and lasts days to weeks, or chronic if it persists beyond three months. In most cases, backache is linked to muscle or ligament strain rather than a serious underlying condition, and it tends to improve over time with appropriate care.

## Symptoms

Backache can present in different ways, including:

- A dull, persistent ache or a sharp, sudden pain in the back
- Pain that spreads to the buttocks, hip, or down one or both legs (which may suggest sciatica)
- Stiffness or reduced flexibility in the back
- Muscle spasms or tightness
- Pain that worsens with certain movements, prolonged sitting, or standing
- Discomfort that may ease with rest or change in position, though prolonged rest is not generally advised

## Causes & Risk Factors

Most backache is caused by straining the muscles or ligaments supporting the spine, often from awkward movement, heavy lifting, or poor posture. Other possible causes and contributing factors include:

- A slipped (herniated) disc pressing on nearby nerves
- Sciatica, where irritation of the sciatic nerve causes pain radiating down the leg
- Conditions such as ankylosing spondylitis affecting the spine
- Poor posture, prolonged sitting, or incorrect lifting technique
- Being overweight, which places additional strain on the back
- Lack of regular physical activity and weak supporting muscles
- Pregnancy, due to changes in posture and added weight
- Rarely, more serious causes such as a spinal fracture, infection, or cancer

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay as physically active as you comfortably can rather than resting for long periods
- Practice good posture when sitting, standing, and lifting
- Use correct lifting technique, bending at the knees rather than the waist
- Maintain a healthy body weight to reduce strain on the spine
- Apply heat or cold packs to ease muscle tension and pain
- Build core and back muscle strength through gentle, guided exercise
- Take regular breaks from prolonged sitting, especially at a desk

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most backache improves with simple self-care, but seek urgent medical attention if you experience:

- Sudden, severe back pain that is rapidly worsening
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in one or both legs
- Loss of feeling around the genitals or anus
- New problems controlling your bladder or bowels
- Fever or feeling generally unwell along with back pain
- Back pain following a significant fall, accident, or injury
- Unexplained weight loss alongside persistent back pain

These features can indicate a more serious underlying condition and need prompt medical assessment.
