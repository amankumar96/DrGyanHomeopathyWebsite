## What is Piebaldism?

Piebaldism is a rare genetic condition present from birth that causes patches of skin and hair to lack normal pigment, appearing white or very pale compared to the surrounding skin and hair. It happens because the cells responsible for producing pigment (melanocytes) are absent in certain areas of the body from early development. Unlike some other pigment conditions, piebaldism patches are usually present at birth and tend to remain stable throughout life rather than spreading.

## Symptoms

Typical features of piebaldism include:

- A patch of white hair, often at the front of the scalp (a white forelock), frequently the most noticeable feature
- Irregular, well-defined patches of depigmented (white) skin, commonly on the forehead, front of the trunk, and middle parts of the arms and legs
- Normally pigmented skin often remaining within or at the edges of the affected patches, creating a speckled appearance
- Patches generally present from birth and usually stable in size over time
- Normal skin sensation and texture within the affected patches

## Causes & Risk Factors

Piebaldism is caused by a genetic change affecting a gene involved in the development and function of pigment-producing cells. It is usually inherited in an autosomal dominant pattern, meaning a child has a notable chance of inheriting it if one parent has the condition, though it can occasionally arise from a new genetic change with no family history. It is not caused by anything during pregnancy, diet, or environmental exposure, and cannot be prevented.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use sunscreen on depigmented patches, as they lack natural pigment protection and can burn more easily
- Consider cosmetic camouflage products or hairstyling if the appearance is a personal concern
- Support emotional well-being, particularly in children, as visible differences can affect confidence and social experiences
- Keep track of the patches over time, noting any genuine changes to mention at doctor visits
- Connect with support groups or genetic counselling services if planning a family

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Piebaldism itself is not cancerous and is not contagious, and the affected patches are typically stable once present from birth. However, any new, changing, unusually textured, or unexplained skin lesion — including changes within or around existing patches — should always be checked by a doctor to confirm the diagnosis and rule out other causes of pigment change.
