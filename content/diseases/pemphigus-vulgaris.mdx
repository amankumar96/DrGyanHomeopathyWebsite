## What is Pemphigus Vulgaris?

Pemphigus vulgaris is a rare, serious autoimmune condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks proteins that hold skin cells together, causing painful blisters to form on the skin and, very commonly, inside the mouth and other mucous membranes. It is considered a serious medical condition because widespread blistering can lead to infection, fluid loss, and difficulty eating or drinking, and it typically requires prompt diagnosis and ongoing specialist dermatological treatment, often with immunosuppressant medication, to control the disease and prevent complications.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of pemphigus vulgaris include:

- Soft, fluid-filled blisters on the skin that break easily, leaving raw, painful areas
- Painful sores or blisters inside the mouth, which are often the first sign and can make eating, drinking, or swallowing difficult
- Blisters or sores affecting the throat, nose, eyes, or genital area in some cases
- Skin that feels tender or painful, even before blisters appear
- Peeling or shedding of the top layer of skin with gentle pressure
- Weeping or crusted areas where blisters have burst
- General fatigue or feeling unwell, particularly with widespread involvement

## Causes & Risk Factors

Pemphigus vulgaris occurs when the immune system produces antibodies against proteins that normally hold skin cells firmly together, causing the cells to separate and blisters to form. The exact trigger is not fully understood, but recognised factors include:

- Autoimmune tendency, sometimes alongside other autoimmune conditions
- Genetic factors that may increase susceptibility in some populations
- Certain medications, which have been associated with triggering pemphigus in rare cases
- Middle to older age, as it is more commonly diagnosed in this group, though it can occur at other ages

Pemphigus vulgaris is rare and is not caused by poor hygiene, diet, or lifestyle choices.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for pemphigus vulgaris is led by a dermatologist or specialist team and typically involves corticosteroids and other immunosuppressant or immune-modulating medications to control the abnormal immune response, along with careful wound care and monitoring for infection. Treatment often needs to continue for an extended period and is adjusted based on response.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy only as a general well-being-focused, complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should never be used as a reason to reduce or stop immunosuppressant medication, as doing so can lead to serious flare-ups. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep blistered or raw skin clean and protected as advised by your specialist team to reduce infection risk
- Use a soft toothbrush and gentle, alcohol-free mouth rinses if mouth sores are present
- Choose soft, non-irritating foods if mouth ulcers make eating difficult, and stay well hydrated
- Avoid sun exposure and skin friction or trauma, which can worsen blistering
- Attend all follow-up appointments to monitor disease activity and medication side effects
- Seek emotional support, as living with a chronic blistering condition can be distressing

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Pemphigus vulgaris is a serious condition, and some situations require urgent specialist or emergency care, including:

- Widespread or rapidly spreading blistering over the body
- Mouth sores severe enough to prevent eating, drinking, or taking medication
- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever
- Signs of dehydration, such as reduced urination, dizziness, or extreme thirst
- Difficulty breathing or swallowing
- Feeling generally very unwell alongside worsening skin or mouth symptoms

These situations need prompt dermatological or emergency medical attention, and should never be managed with self-care or complementary approaches alone.
