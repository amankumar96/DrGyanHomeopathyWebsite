## What is Kidney Stones?

Kidney stones are hard deposits made of minerals and salts that form inside the kidneys when urine becomes concentrated, allowing these substances to crystallise and stick together. Stones can vary greatly in size, from as small as a grain of sand to as large as a golf ball in rare cases. Many small stones pass out of the body in urine without causing major problems, while larger stones can become lodged in the urinary tract, causing significant pain and sometimes blocking the flow of urine. Kidney stones are a common condition, and some people experience repeated episodes over their lifetime.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of kidney stones include:

- Sudden, severe pain in the side or back, below the ribs, which can radiate to the lower abdomen and groin (renal colic)
- Pain that comes in waves and fluctuates in intensity
- Pain or burning sensation while urinating
- Pink, red, or brown blood in the urine
- Cloudy or foul-smelling urine
- Nausea and vomiting
- A frequent or urgent need to urinate
- Fever and chills if an infection is present

Small stones may cause no symptoms at all and can sometimes be found incidentally during imaging for another reason.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Kidney stones form when urine contains more crystal-forming substances, such as calcium, oxalate, and uric acid, than the fluid in the urine can dilute. Several factors increase the risk, including:

- Not drinking enough fluids, leading to concentrated urine
- A diet high in salt, sugar, or animal protein
- Being overweight
- A family or personal history of kidney stones
- Certain medical conditions, such as hyperparathyroidism, gout, or inflammatory bowel disease
- Some medicines and dietary supplements
- Digestive surgeries that affect how the body absorbs calcium and water

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of kidney stones depends on their size and location. Small stones are often managed with pain relief, increased fluid intake, and sometimes medication to help the stone pass. Larger stones or those causing blockage or infection may need procedures such as shock wave lithotripsy to break up the stone, or surgical removal. Doctors also investigate the type of stone to guide long-term dietary and medical prevention strategies.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Drink plenty of water throughout the day to keep urine dilute, unless your doctor has advised otherwise
- Limit salt intake, as high sodium can increase calcium in urine
- Moderate intake of animal protein and foods high in oxalate, such as spinach and nuts, if advised by your doctor based on your stone type
- Maintain a healthy body weight through balanced diet and regular activity
- Avoid excessive vitamin C or calcium supplements unless recommended by a doctor
- Follow up with any recommended urine or blood tests to understand your specific stone-forming tendency
- Keep any passed stones for laboratory analysis if advised, as this guides future prevention

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical care if you experience:

- Severe pain that is not controlled with usual pain relief
- Pain accompanied by fever, chills, or feeling very unwell, which can suggest an infection
- Inability to pass urine at all
- Visible blood in the urine
- Persistent vomiting alongside severe pain
- Signs of a blocked kidney, such as worsening pain with reduced urine output

These symptoms can indicate an infection or a blockage in the urinary tract, both of which need prompt medical assessment and treatment.
