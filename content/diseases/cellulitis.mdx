## What is Cellulitis?

Cellulitis is a bacterial infection of the deeper layers of the skin and underlying tissue. It most commonly occurs when bacteria, often Streptococcus or Staphylococcus species, enter the skin through a cut, insect bite, surgical wound, or other break in the skin barrier. Cellulitis typically affects the lower legs but can occur anywhere on the body. Unlike more superficial skin infections, cellulitis can spread relatively quickly through the tissue and, if untreated, carries a risk of serious complications, which is why prompt medical assessment is important.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of cellulitis include:

- Red, swollen skin that feels warm and tender to touch
- Skin that may appear tight, glossy, or stretched
- Pain or tenderness in the affected area
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell
- Swelling of nearby lymph nodes
- In some cases, blistering or areas of darker, bruised-looking skin
- Red streaking extending from the affected area, which can indicate the infection is tracking along lymphatic channels

## Causes & Risk Factors

Cellulitis develops when bacteria enter the skin, usually through a break in its surface. Risk factors include:

- Cuts, scrapes, insect bites, or surgical wounds that allow bacteria to enter
- Existing skin conditions such as eczema, athlete's foot, or psoriasis that damage the skin barrier
- Diabetes, which can impair wound healing and increase infection risk
- Poor circulation or lymphoedema, particularly in the legs
- Obesity
- A weakened immune system, from illness or certain medicines
- Intravenous drug use

Elevating the affected limb, keeping the area clean, and managing any underlying conditions such as diabetes or poor circulation also support recovery. ## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take the full course of prescribed antibiotics exactly as directed, even if symptoms improve early
- Keep the affected area clean and elevated to help reduce swelling
- Mark the edge of the redness with a pen so you and your doctor can monitor whether it is spreading
- Avoid scratching or further injuring the affected skin
- Manage underlying conditions such as diabetes, lymphoedema, or poor circulation with your doctor's guidance
- Treat minor cuts, insect bites, and skin breaks promptly with cleaning and appropriate dressings
- Moisturise dry or cracked skin to reduce entry points for bacteria

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor the same day, or seek emergency care, if you notice:

- Fever, chills, or feeling very unwell alongside red, swollen skin
- Redness that is spreading rapidly or covers a large area
- Red streaking extending from the affected area (a sign of lymphangitis)
- Increasing pain, blistering, or areas of blackened or dying skin
- Confusion, rapid heartbeat, low blood pressure, or difficulty staying awake, which can indicate the infection is becoming systemic
- Cellulitis affecting the face or around the eyes
- No improvement, or worsening, within 48 hours of starting antibiotics

These signs can indicate that the infection is spreading or progressing towards sepsis, a medical emergency, and need urgent medical and antibiotic treatment without delay.
