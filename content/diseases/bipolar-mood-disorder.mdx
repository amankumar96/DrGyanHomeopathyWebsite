## What is Bipolar Mood Disorder?

Bipolar mood disorder, commonly called bipolar disorder, is a long-term mental health condition characterised by significant shifts in mood, energy, and activity levels. People with bipolar disorder experience episodes of mania or hypomania, periods of unusually elevated mood, energy, or irritability, as well as episodes of depression, periods of low mood and reduced energy. Between episodes, many people experience periods of relatively stable mood. The pattern, frequency, and severity of episodes vary considerably between individuals, and the condition typically requires long-term psychiatric management.

## Symptoms

During a manic or hypomanic episode, symptoms can include:

- Unusually elevated, euphoric, or irritable mood
- Increased energy, activity, or restlessness
- Reduced need for sleep without feeling tired
- Rapid speech, racing thoughts, or jumping between ideas
- Increased impulsivity, such as reckless spending, risky activities, or poor judgement
- Inflated self-confidence or grandiosity

During a depressive episode, symptoms can include:

- Persistent low mood or sadness
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities
- Fatigue or low energy
- Difficulty concentrating
- Changes in sleep or appetite
- Feelings of worthlessness or hopelessness
- Thoughts of death or suicide

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of bipolar disorder is not fully understood, but it is thought to result from a combination of genetic, biological, and environmental factors affecting brain chemistry and function.

Known risk factors include:

- A family history of bipolar disorder or other mood disorders
- Significant stress or traumatic life events
- Major life changes, such as loss of a loved one or job
- Substance use, which can trigger or worsen episodes
- Sleep disruption
- Certain medical conditions or medications, in some cases

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for bipolar disorder usually involves mood-stabilising medication prescribed and monitored by a psychiatrist, often alongside talking therapies and lifestyle strategies to help maintain stability, such as regular sleep routines. Long-term, ongoing psychiatric follow-up is typically an important part of care.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, often in the hope of supporting overall well-being. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and never stop or alter prescribed mood-stabilising medication without medical supervision.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain a regular sleep schedule, as sleep disruption can trigger episodes
- Keep a mood diary to help identify early warning signs and patterns
- Avoid alcohol and recreational drug use, which can worsen mood instability
- Build a consistent daily routine for meals, activity, and rest
- Stay connected with a support network of trusted family, friends, or support groups
- Attend regular follow-up appointments with your psychiatrist, even when feeling well
- Learn your personal early warning signs of mania or depression and have a plan for them

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek immediate help from emergency services, a crisis line, or an emergency department if you or someone you know experiences:

- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or a suicide attempt
- Severe mania with dangerous, reckless, or risky behaviour, or loss of touch with reality, such as hallucinations or delusions
- Severe depression with an inability to function, eat, or care for oneself
- Any immediate risk of harm to self or others

These are mental health emergencies. Do not wait for a routine appointment — seek immediate help without delay.
