## What is Acne Rosacea?

Rosacea, sometimes called acne rosacea, is a long-term skin condition that mainly affects the face. It typically causes redness and flushing across the cheeks, nose, forehead, and chin, which may come and go at first before becoming more persistent over time. In some people, small red bumps or pus-filled spots develop, which can resemble acne, along with visible small blood vessels and, in some cases, thickened skin, most often around the nose. Rosacea most commonly begins in adulthood, often between the ages of 30 and 60, and tends to be more noticeable on lighter skin, though it affects all skin tones.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of rosacea include:

- Facial flushing or redness that comes and goes, especially across the cheeks and nose
- Persistent redness that does not fade over time, as the condition progresses
- Small, red, inflamed bumps or pus-filled spots, resembling acne
- Visible small blood vessels (telangiectasia) on the face
- A burning or stinging sensation, particularly when using water or skincare products
- Dry, rough, or sensitive-feeling facial skin
- Thickened skin, particularly around the nose, in longstanding or more severe cases
- Eye irritation, dryness, redness, or a gritty feeling (ocular rosacea)

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of rosacea is not fully understood, but it is thought to result from a combination of genetic, immune, and environmental factors, including abnormal blood vessel and immune responses in facial skin. Known risk factors include:

- A family history of rosacea
- Fair skin, particularly in people of Celtic or Northern European descent, though rosacea occurs in all skin types
- Being middle-aged or older, with onset often between 30 and 60 years
- A personal history of frequent blushing

Common triggers that can worsen symptoms include alcohol, spicy foods, hot drinks, sunlight, extreme heat or cold, stress, and vigorous exercise.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for rosacea may include topical creams or gels, oral antibiotics taken over several weeks for their anti-inflammatory effect, laser or light therapy for visible blood vessels, and daily use of broad-spectrum sunscreen. A dermatologist may be involved if initial treatments are not effective or if eye symptoms develop. These treatments are supported by clinical evidence and form the standard approach to care.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medical care, often seeking general skin support alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should not be used as a replacement for prescribed rosacea treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 30 or higher) daily, as sun exposure is a common trigger
- Identify and avoid your personal triggers, such as alcohol, spicy food, hot drinks, or extreme temperatures
- Use gentle, fragrance-free skincare products and avoid harsh scrubs or exfoliants
- Protect your face in cold or windy weather with a scarf
- Manage stress through relaxation techniques, adequate rest, and activities you enjoy
- Avoid very hot showers or baths, which can worsen facial redness
- Keep a symptom diary to help identify patterns and triggers specific to you

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor if you suspect you have rosacea or if your symptoms are not improving with self-care. Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Eye pain, significant redness, blurred vision, or sensitivity to light, which may indicate ocular rosacea or keratitis requiring timely treatment
- Rapidly worsening facial swelling, thickening, or disfigurement
- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing warmth, swelling, or pus
- Sudden, severe flare-ups accompanied by fever or feeling generally unwell

A doctor or dermatologist can confirm the diagnosis and recommend an appropriate treatment plan.
