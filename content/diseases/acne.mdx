## What is Acne?

Acne is a very common skin condition that causes spots, ranging from small whiteheads and blackheads to larger, inflamed, and sometimes painful bumps beneath the skin. It most often affects the face, chest, shoulders, and back, areas where oil-producing glands are more active. Acne can range from mild, with occasional spots, to more severe forms involving deep, painful cysts and nodules that carry a higher risk of scarring. It is most common during the teenage years due to hormonal changes, but it can also affect adults.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of acne include:

- Whiteheads and blackheads (blocked pores with or without surface darkening)
- Papules — small, raised, tender red bumps
- Pustules — pus-filled, inflamed spots
- Nodules and cysts — larger, deeper, often painful lumps beneath the skin
- Oily or greasy-looking skin
- Skin discolouration, redness, or scarring, particularly after more severe spots heal

Acne can also affect self-esteem, confidence, and mental well-being, particularly when it is persistent or visible.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Acne develops when hair follicles become blocked with excess oil (sebum) and dead skin cells, allowing bacteria to multiply and cause inflammation. Known contributing factors include:

- Hormonal changes, such as during puberty, the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, or menopause
- A family history of acne
- Certain medical conditions, including polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and rosacea
- Certain medicines, such as some steroids or hormonal treatments
- Greasy or heavy cosmetic and hair products that block pores
- Heavy sweating, humidity, or frequent touching and rubbing of the skin

Current evidence does not support poor hygiene or sexual activity as causes of acne, though some studies suggest a high-glycaemic diet may worsen symptoms in some individuals.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for acne depends on severity and may include topical treatments such as benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, or retinoids; topical or oral antibiotics; hormonal treatments such as certain contraceptive pills; and, for severe or scarring acne, isotretinoin prescribed and monitored by a dermatologist. These treatments are supported by clinical evidence and form the standard approach to care.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medical care, often seeking general skin and well-being support alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should not be used as a replacement for prescribed acne treatment, particularly where there is a risk of scarring. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Wash affected skin gently twice daily with a mild cleanser; avoid harsh scrubbing
- Choose non-comedogenic (non-pore-blocking) skincare and makeup products
- Avoid picking, squeezing, or popping spots to reduce the risk of scarring and spreading infection
- Wash oily hair regularly and keep hair products away from the face where possible
- Remove makeup before bed and avoid touching your face unnecessarily
- Be patient — most acne treatments take several weeks to show visible improvement

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See a doctor if your acne is not improving after several months of self-care and over-the-counter treatment, is painful, deep, or cystic, is causing scarring, or is affecting your mental health and confidence. While acne itself is rarely a medical emergency, seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Sudden, severe, widespread breakouts with fever or feeling generally unwell
- Signs of a spreading skin infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or spreading pus
- Significant emotional distress, anxiety, or low mood related to your skin, which deserves timely support

A doctor or dermatologist can assess your skin and recommend an appropriate treatment plan.
