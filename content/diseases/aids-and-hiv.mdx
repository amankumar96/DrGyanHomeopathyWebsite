## What is HIV/AIDS?

HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) is a virus that attacks and gradually weakens the immune system by targeting specific white blood cells the body needs to fight infection. Left untreated, HIV infection can progress over many years to AIDS (Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome), the most advanced stage of the disease, when the immune system is severely damaged and the body becomes vulnerable to serious infections and certain cancers.

HIV cannot be cured, but it can be effectively controlled. With early diagnosis and lifelong antiretroviral therapy (ART), a person living with HIV can keep the virus suppressed to very low or undetectable levels, maintain a strong immune system, and live a long, healthy life. AIDS itself is largely preventable today when HIV is diagnosed and treated early and consistently. This page is intended purely as general patient education, and it does not replace care from a qualified infectious disease specialist or HIV treatment centre.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of HIV vary by stage, and many people have no symptoms at all for years after infection, especially once on treatment.

**Early (acute) infection**, typically two to four weeks after exposure, may cause flu-like symptoms, including:

- Fever and chills
- Sore throat
- Swollen lymph nodes
- Body aches and joint pain
- Rash
- Fatigue
- Mouth ulcers

**Chronic infection** can be symptom-free for many years even though the virus remains active in the body, which is why testing matters even when a person feels completely well.

**Advanced HIV disease (AIDS)**, which develops only when HIV is left untreated for a long time, may involve:

- Rapid, unexplained weight loss
- Recurrent fever or night sweats
- Prolonged swelling of lymph nodes
- Chronic diarrhoea
- Persistent fatigue
- Opportunistic infections (such as tuberculosis, certain fungal infections, or pneumonia) that take advantage of a weakened immune system
- Certain cancers

These symptoms are not unique to HIV and can occur with many other conditions, so testing is the only reliable way to know a person's HIV status.

## Causes & Risk Factors

HIV spreads through contact with certain body fluids from a person who has the virus, mainly blood, semen, vaginal fluids, and breast milk. Recognised routes of transmission and risk factors include:

- Unprotected vaginal or anal sex with a person living with HIV
- Sharing needles, syringes, or other injecting drug equipment
- Mother-to-child transmission during pregnancy, childbirth, or breastfeeding
- Exposure to unsterilised medical, dental, or tattooing equipment
- Occupational exposure to blood, such as a needlestick injury in a healthcare setting, without appropriate precautions
- Having another sexually transmitted infection, which can increase the risk of acquiring or transmitting HIV
- Receiving blood or blood products that have not been properly screened (now extremely rare where screening protocols are followed)

HIV is **not** spread through casual, everyday contact. It does not spread through hugging, shaking hands, sharing food, drinks, or utensils, using the same toilet, coughing, sneezing, saliva, sweat, tears, or insect bites. The virus does not survive long outside the human body.

## How Homeopathy Approaches HIV/AIDS

To be clear from the outset: homeopathy has no role in treating, managing, suppressing, or curing HIV infection, and it has no effect on the virus, viral load, or CD4 count. HIV is a medical condition that must be diagnosed and managed by qualified doctors using antiretroviral therapy (ART), which is lifelong, essential, and non-negotiable.

Some patients living with HIV choose to explore homeopathy purely as a general wellbeing and emotional-support option, alongside their prescribed ART and specialist medical care, not instead of it. A homeopathic consultation, if sought, would typically focus on the person's overall constitution, general symptoms unrelated to HIV itself, sleep, stress, and day-to-day quality of life. It is important to understand that scientific evidence for homeopathy's effectiveness in any health condition is limited, and bodies such as the NHS state there is no robust evidence that homeopathy is effective for treating any disease. Homeopathy must never be used in place of ART, and it cannot address HIV, AIDS, or any related complication in any way.

## What Homeopathic Medicines Are Based On

Homeopathic materia medica and repertories describe remedies that practitioners may consider for general constitutional wellbeing support, such as supporting a sense of overall balance, sleep, or coping with the emotional burden of a long-term health condition. These are referenced here only as general, non-specific examples of how classical homeopathic practice is organised, and are not tied in any way to treating HIV, AIDS, or any of their symptoms.

Remedy selection in classical homeopathic practice is individualised and determined only by a qualified practitioner after detailed consultation. Any such consultation is strictly a complement to, and never a replacement for, ART and ongoing specialist medical care. This section is educational only and is not an instruction to self-medicate, and it does not recommend homeopathy for HIV or AIDS.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of HIV is centred on antiretroviral therapy (ART), a combination of medicines that suppress the virus, usually to undetectable levels in the blood, allowing the immune system to recover and stay strong. ART is lifelong: it must be started as soon as possible after diagnosis and taken every day, without interruption, for life. It does not cure HIV, but when taken consistently it controls the virus, prevents progression to AIDS, protects long-term health, and greatly reduces the risk of transmitting HIV to others. Regular monitoring of viral load and CD4 count, along with screening and prevention for opportunistic infections, is an essential part of ongoing care, alongside vaccinations and management of any other health conditions.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy purely as a complementary, general wellbeing approach for quality of life and emotional support, strictly alongside this medical care, never as a replacement for it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes. Stopping or skipping ART, for any reason, including to try an alternative treatment, is dangerous and can allow the virus to multiply, damage the immune system further, and lead to drug resistance.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Take antiretroviral therapy (ART) exactly as prescribed, every day, without stopping or skipping doses
- Attend all scheduled appointments, viral load checks, and CD4 count monitoring, even when feeling completely well
- Eat a balanced diet, stay physically active, and get adequate rest to support overall health
- Practise safer sex and discuss prevention options, such as condoms and treatment-as-prevention, with your doctor and partners
- Avoid sharing needles, razors, or other items that may carry traces of blood
- Seek prompt medical attention for any new infection or illness, however minor it may seem
- Build a support network, whether through family, friends, or counselling, to manage the emotional and social aspects of living with HIV
- Inform sexual partners so they can be tested, and discuss safer family planning and pregnancy options with your doctor if relevant

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Living with HIV requires continuous, specialist medical care for life, not just a one-time consultation. Regular follow-up with an infectious disease specialist or HIV treatment centre is essential for everyone living with HIV, even when feeling well, to monitor the virus, adjust treatment if needed, and catch complications early.

Seek prompt or emergency medical attention if you or someone you know experiences:

- High fever that persists or will not come down
- Severe or worsening shortness of breath
- Persistent diarrhoea, vomiting, or inability to keep fluids down
- Significant, unexplained weight loss
- Signs of a serious infection, such as a severe cough, confusion, or a stiff neck
- New or worsening vision problems
- Severe headache with neck stiffness or sensitivity to light
- Any sign that antiretroviral medication is not being tolerated, or missed doses due to illness or access issues

If you have never been tested and believe you may have been exposed to HIV, or if you are newly diagnosed, contact a doctor or HIV testing and treatment centre as soon as possible. Early diagnosis and prompt, lifelong treatment give the best possible long-term outcome, and ongoing specialist care, not occasional check-ins, is what keeps a person living with HIV healthy over time.
