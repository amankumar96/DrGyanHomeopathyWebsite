## What is Pernicious Anemia?

Pernicious anemia is a type of vitamin B12 deficiency anemia that occurs when the body cannot absorb enough vitamin B12 from food, usually because the immune system attacks cells in the stomach that make a protein called intrinsic factor, which is needed for B12 absorption. Without enough B12, the body cannot make healthy red blood cells, leading to anemia, and over time, B12 deficiency can also affect the nervous system. Pernicious anemia is a long-term condition that is manageable with regular treatment, but it generally requires ongoing medical care rather than a one-time fix.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of pernicious anemia and B12 deficiency include:

- Persistent tiredness and lack of energy
- Pale or slightly yellowish skin
- Shortness of breath, especially on exertion
- Heart palpitations
- A sore, red tongue or mouth ulcers
- Pins and needles sensations (paraesthesia) in the hands and feet
- Difficulty with balance or coordination
- Memory problems, confusion, or mood changes
- Vision disturbances in some cases

Symptoms often develop gradually and can be mistaken for general tiredness, so diagnosis sometimes takes time.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The most common cause of pernicious anemia is an autoimmune reaction that damages the stomach cells producing intrinsic factor, which is needed to absorb vitamin B12 from food. Other causes and risk factors for B12 deficiency include:

- A family history of pernicious anemia or other autoimmune conditions
- Other autoimmune disorders, such as thyroid disease, vitiligo, or type 1 diabetes
- Surgery involving the stomach or small intestine
- Conditions affecting the small intestine, such as Crohn's disease or coeliac disease
- A diet very low in animal products, such as a strict vegan diet, without supplementation
- Increasing age, as absorption can decline over time
- Certain long-term medications that affect stomach acid or absorption

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for pernicious anemia usually involves regular vitamin B12 injections, sometimes followed by maintenance injections every few months, or in some cases high-dose oral B12 supplements. Treatment is typically lifelong, with periodic blood tests to monitor levels and response.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this prescribed treatment, often for general well-being rather than to address the B12 deficiency itself. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and never stop or reduce prescribed B12 treatment without medical advice, since doing so can allow deficiency and its complications to progress.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled B12 injections or follow supplement advice exactly as prescribed
- Eat a varied diet including sources of B12 if your doctor advises dietary measures are appropriate for you
- Keep up with scheduled blood tests to monitor B12, folate, and iron levels
- Pace activities and rest adequately while energy levels recover
- Report new or worsening numbness, tingling, or balance problems to your doctor promptly
- Manage other autoimmune conditions you may have, as they can occur alongside pernicious anemia
- Avoid self-prescribing high-dose supplements without medical guidance

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical review if you notice:

- New or worsening numbness, tingling, or weakness in the hands or feet
- Difficulty walking, unsteadiness, or balance problems
- Increasing confusion, memory problems, or mood changes
- Severe shortness of breath, chest pain, or a very fast or irregular heartbeat
- Vision changes

These neurological symptoms can become serious or, if treatment is delayed for too long, irreversible, so prompt medical assessment is important. Never stop or delay your prescribed B12 treatment while seeking other advice.
