## What is Flatulent Dyspepsia?

Flatulent dyspepsia refers to indigestion (dyspepsia) in which bloating, excess gas, and frequent burping are especially prominent, alongside the general discomfort or burning feeling in the upper abdomen typical of dyspepsia. It is a common functional digestive complaint, meaning symptoms often occur without a clearly identifiable structural disease, and is usually related to eating habits, diet, or lifestyle factors.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of flatulent dyspepsia include:

- Bloating and a feeling of fullness in the abdomen
- Excess gas and frequent burping
- Discomfort or a burning feeling in the upper abdomen, often described as heartburn
- Nausea, and occasionally vomiting
- Regurgitation of food or bitter-tasting fluid
- Symptoms that often worsen after eating, particularly after large or fatty meals

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes and contributing factors include:

- Eating too much, eating too quickly, or eating during stressful situations
- High-fat, spicy, or acidic foods
- Excess caffeine or alcohol intake
- Smoking
- Certain medications, including some painkillers like ibuprofen
- Being overweight
- Pregnancy
- Chronic stress and fatigue
- Underlying conditions such as a hiatus hernia, GERD, or occasionally stomach ulcers

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Eat smaller, more frequent meals and avoid eating too quickly
- Identify and limit personal trigger foods, such as fatty, fried, or very spicy foods
- Reduce caffeine, alcohol, and carbonated drinks if they worsen symptoms
- Avoid eating large meals close to bedtime
- Manage stress through relaxation, regular rest, and adequate sleep
- Stay physically active, as gentle movement can help ease bloating for some people
- Maintain a healthy weight

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor if indigestion and bloating persist for more than two weeks, are severe, or keep recurring despite dietary changes. Seek prompt or urgent medical attention if you notice:

- Unexplained weight loss
- Difficulty swallowing
- Persistent vomiting
- Vomiting blood or material resembling coffee grounds
- Black, tarry stools, or blood in the stool
- A lump or swelling in the abdomen, or signs of anaemia such as unusual tiredness and paleness

These symptoms need timely medical evaluation to identify or rule out a more serious underlying cause.
