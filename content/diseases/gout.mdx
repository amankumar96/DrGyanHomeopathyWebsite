## What is Gout?

Gout is a type of inflammatory arthritis that occurs when excess uric acid builds up in the blood and forms sharp crystals within a joint, triggering sudden and often intense pain, swelling, and redness. Uric acid is a natural waste product formed when the body breaks down purines, substances found in body tissues and in many foods. Gout typically appears as flares, or attacks, that can last several days to a couple of weeks before settling, and it most commonly affects the big toe, though it can involve other joints such as the ankles, knees, and fingers.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of a gout flare include:

- Sudden, severe pain in a joint, often starting at night
- Hot, swollen, and red skin over the affected joint
- Intense tenderness, sometimes severe enough that even light touch is painful
- Limited movement in the affected joint during a flare
- In long-standing or poorly controlled gout, firm lumps called tophi may develop under the skin near joints

## Causes & Risk Factors

Gout occurs when the level of uric acid in the blood becomes too high, allowing crystals to form in and around joints. Several factors can raise this risk, including:

- Being male, particularly in midlife, and being female after menopause
- A diet high in purine-rich foods, such as red meat, organ meat, and certain seafood
- Regular or heavy alcohol consumption
- Being overweight
- Kidney disease, which can reduce the body's ability to clear uric acid
- Certain medications, such as diuretics and low-dose aspirin
- A family history of gout
- Dehydration, illness, injury, or large meals, which can trigger a flare in someone already prone to gout

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of gout typically involves anti-inflammatory medication or colchicine to relieve an acute flare, and in many cases, long-term uric-acid-lowering medication taken regularly to prevent future attacks and complications. Lifestyle changes, such as weight management, limiting alcohol, and dietary adjustments, are often recommended alongside medical treatment.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside these treatments, often seeking general support alongside their prescribed care. Homeopathy should not be seen as a replacement for medically recommended gout treatment, particularly uric-acid-lowering medication, which plays an important role in preventing long-term joint damage. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Stay well hydrated throughout the day
- Limit alcohol intake, particularly beer and spirits
- Reduce consumption of purine-rich foods such as red meat, organ meat, and certain seafood
- Work towards and maintain a healthy body weight
- Rest the affected joint and elevate it during a flare
- Apply ice packs to ease pain and swelling during an acute attack
- Keep pressure off the affected joint, for example by wearing loose, comfortable footwear

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most gout flares are intensely painful but not dangerous, however seek prompt medical attention if you experience:

- Your first-ever episode of sudden, severe joint pain and swelling, to confirm the diagnosis
- A gout flare that does not respond to usual treatment
- Severe pain accompanied by high fever, feeling very unwell, or an inability to eat or drink, which may indicate a joint infection rather than a simple gout flare
- Signs of spreading redness, warmth, or swelling beyond the joint

Untreated or repeatedly undertreated gout can lead to lasting joint damage, so ongoing follow-up with your doctor is important even between flares.
