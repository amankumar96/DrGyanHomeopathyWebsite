## What is Depression?

Depression, sometimes called clinical depression or major depressive disorder, is a common mental health condition that causes a persistent low mood and a loss of interest or pleasure in activities that were once enjoyed. It is more than a temporary dip in spirits; it typically lasts for weeks or longer and can affect sleep, appetite, energy, concentration, and self-worth. Depression can range from mild to severe and, with appropriate professional support, most people experience meaningful improvement over time.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of depression include:

- Persistent low mood, sadness, or feeling empty most of the day
- Loss of interest or pleasure in activities previously enjoyed
- Fatigue or loss of energy
- Changes in appetite or weight
- Difficulty sleeping, or sleeping more than usual
- Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- Feelings of worthlessness, excessive guilt, or hopelessness
- Slowed thinking or movement, or restlessness
- Thoughts of death, self-harm, or suicide

Symptoms generally need to be present for most of the day, for at least two weeks, and to affect daily functioning, for a diagnosis of depression to be considered.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Depression usually arises from a combination of factors rather than a single cause. These may include:

- A family history of depression or other mental health conditions
- Stressful or traumatic life events, such as bereavement, relationship difficulties, or financial problems
- Chronic physical illness or pain
- Significant life changes, such as childbirth or retirement
- Social isolation or lack of support
- Certain personality traits or past experiences
- Alcohol or substance use
- Imbalances in brain chemistry and hormonal changes

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Try to maintain regular sleep patterns where possible
- Stay physically active, even gentle movement such as walking can help
- Keep in contact with friends, family, or support groups rather than withdrawing
- Eat regular, balanced meals
- Limit alcohol and avoid recreational drug use
- Break tasks into small, manageable steps if everyday activities feel overwhelming
- Consider keeping a simple routine, as structure can help during low periods

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek immediate help if you or someone you know experiences any of the following:

- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm, or making a plan to harm oneself
- Feeling that life is not worth living, or feeling hopeless about the future
- Sudden worsening of mood, withdrawal, or behaviour that worries others
- Hearing voices or experiencing other symptoms of psychosis alongside low mood
