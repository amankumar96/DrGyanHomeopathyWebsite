## What is Keloid?

A keloid is a type of raised scar that forms when the skin's healing process produces excess scar tissue, growing beyond the edges of the original wound. Keloids can develop after any injury to the skin, including surgical cuts, burns, acne, insect bites, piercings, or even minor scratches, and sometimes appear months after the original injury healed. They most commonly form on the chest, shoulders, earlobes, and upper back, and tend to be more common in people with darker skin tones and in those with a family history of keloids. Keloids are benign, meaning not cancerous, but they can be persistent and difficult to fully resolve.

## Symptoms

Common features of a keloid include:

- A raised, firm, smooth lump of scar tissue that extends beyond the original wound's edges
- Skin colour that may be pink, red, purple, brown, or darker than the surrounding skin
- Itching or tenderness, particularly in earlier stages
- Gradual growth over weeks to months, sometimes continuing to enlarge slowly over years
- Restricted movement if the keloid forms over a joint
- A smooth, shiny, or rubbery texture to the touch

Keloids vary widely in size, from small raised areas to large growths covering significant areas of skin.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Keloids form when the skin produces excess collagen during the healing process after an injury. The exact reason some people's skin reacts this way is not fully understood, but several risk factors are recognised:

- A personal or family history of keloids
- Darker skin tone, which is associated with a higher reported risk
- Being under 30 years of age
- Pregnancy, which may increase the likelihood for some individuals
- The location and type of injury, with the chest, shoulders, and earlobes being particularly prone
- Any skin trauma, including surgery, piercings, burns, vaccination sites, and severe acne

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment options for keloids include corticosteroid injections to help flatten the scar, pressure therapy, silicone sheets or gels, cryotherapy (freezing), laser treatment, and occasionally surgical removal, which is often combined with other treatments since surgery alone can sometimes trigger an even larger keloid. The choice of treatment depends on the keloid's size, location, and the individual's history.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep new wounds, cuts, or piercings clean and protected to support normal healing
- Avoid unnecessary skin trauma, such as further piercings or elective procedures, if you are prone to keloids
- Use silicone sheets or gels as advised by your dermatologist on healing wounds if you have a history of keloids
- Protect keloid-prone areas from sun exposure, as this can darken the scar
- Avoid picking at or irritating acne, insect bites, or healing skin
- Inform any doctor or piercer of your keloid tendency before a planned procedure
- Avoid self-treating a keloid with home remedies without medical guidance, as this can sometimes worsen irritation

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Contact your doctor if you notice:

- Rapid growth, increasing pain, or bleeding from a keloid
- Signs of infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or discharge from the area
- A new lump, ulcer, or unusual change in a long-standing scar that looks different from before
- A keloid restricting movement of a nearby joint
- Significant distress or impact on daily life due to the keloid's appearance or symptoms

While keloids themselves are not cancerous, any unusual or changing skin growth should be properly assessed by a doctor to confirm the diagnosis and discuss suitable treatment options.
