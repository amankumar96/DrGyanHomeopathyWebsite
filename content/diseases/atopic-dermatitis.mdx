## What is Atopic Dermatitis?

Atopic dermatitis is a long-term skin condition that causes patches of skin to become dry, itchy, cracked, and sore. It is the most common form of eczema, and the two terms are often used to mean the same thing; you may also see atopic dermatitis described simply as "eczema" on this site and elsewhere. The word "atopic" refers to a tendency toward allergic conditions, since atopic dermatitis often runs in families alongside asthma and hay fever. It affects people of all ages but typically begins in childhood, often between two and six months of age, and many children find it improves as they grow older, though some adults continue to experience flares throughout life. It tends to follow a pattern of flare-ups, when symptoms worsen, and periods when the skin is calmer. It is a chronic condition that currently cannot be cured, but it can usually be managed well with ongoing care.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of atopic dermatitis include:

- Dry, sensitive skin that can feel rough or scaly
- Intense itching, often worse at night
- Red, inflamed patches on lighter skin, or darker brown, purple, or grey patches on darker skin
- Cracked or weeping skin during more severe flares
- Thickened, leathery skin from repeated scratching over time
- Patches commonly appearing on the hands, inner elbows, backs of the knees, and on babies' faces and scalps

Symptoms vary considerably between individuals and can range from mild, occasional dryness to widespread, intensely itchy inflammation.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of atopic dermatitis is not fully understood, but it is thought to involve a skin reaction linked to a weakened skin barrier, which allows moisture to escape and irritants or allergens to penetrate more easily, triggering ongoing itching, swelling, and redness. Genetic factors play a role, and the condition often runs in families alongside other "atopic" conditions such as asthma and hay fever. People with atopic dermatitis frequently also have allergies, although allergies themselves do not directly cause the condition.

Several factors are known to trigger or worsen flares, including:

- Irritants such as soaps, detergents, and some fabrics, particularly wool
- Environmental allergens such as pollen, dust mites, and pet dander
- Cold, dry weather or changes in temperature and humidity
- Skin infections
- Stress and hormonal changes
- Certain foods, in some individuals, particularly children
- Fragrances or dyes in lotions and skincare products

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for atopic dermatitis usually starts with regular use of emollients (moisturisers) to support the skin barrier, along with topical steroid creams to reduce inflammation during flares. Antihistamines may help with itching, and antibiotics may be needed if the skin becomes infected. More persistent or severe cases may be managed with other prescription treatments, phototherapy, or referral to a dermatologist.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Moisturise regularly with a fragrance-free emollient, even when skin looks clear
- Use mild, soap-free cleansers and lukewarm rather than hot water when bathing
- Pat skin dry gently rather than rubbing after washing
- Identify and avoid personal triggers such as certain fabrics, soaps, or products where possible
- Keep fingernails short to reduce skin damage from scratching
- Try to manage stress, as it can worsen flares for some people
- Dress in soft, breathable fabrics such as cotton, and avoid overheating

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical attention promptly if you notice:

- Skin that becomes blistered, crusted, weeping fluid, or develops pus-filled spots, which may indicate infection
- Sudden, widespread worsening of the rash, especially with fever or feeling generally unwell, which can suggest a serious viral infection called eczema herpeticum
- Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pain around a patch of skin
- Atopic dermatitis affecting a large area of the body along with feeling unwell
- Signs of a significant allergic reaction, such as facial swelling or difficulty breathing

These situations can require urgent medical care, so contact your doctor without delay.
