## What is ADHD?

Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition in which the brain works differently from most people, affecting attention, activity levels and impulse control. It is usually first noticed in childhood, often before the age of 12, though many people continue to experience symptoms into adulthood. ADHD is generally described as having patterns that are mostly inattentive, mostly hyperactive-impulsive, or a combination of both. It is a recognised medical condition, not a result of poor parenting or lack of discipline.

## Symptoms

Symptoms of ADHD generally fall into two groups:

**Inattentive signs**
- Being easily distracted
- Finding it hard to listen to instructions or follow conversations
- Forgetting everyday tasks
- Difficulty organising tasks or completing schoolwork

**Hyperactive-impulsive signs**
- High energy levels and restlessness
- Fidgeting or tapping hands and feet
- Difficulty sitting still when expected to
- Finding it hard to wait their turn, or interrupting others

For a diagnosis, these behaviours need to be frequent, persistent across more than one setting (such as home and school), and significant enough to interfere with daily life, learning, or relationships.

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of ADHD is not fully known, but it is believed to involve genetic differences, and ADHD often runs in families. Other factors associated with a higher likelihood of ADHD include being born prematurely or at low birth weight, and having certain co-occurring conditions such as epilepsy or autism. ADHD is not caused by too much screen time, sugar, or poor parenting, although these factors may affect how symptoms present day to day.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of ADHD, assessed and prescribed by paediatricians, psychiatrists, or other qualified specialists, may include behavioural strategies, school and home support, talking therapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, and medicines such as stimulants or non-stimulant options when appropriate. These approaches are evidence-based and form the recommended first line of care.

Some families use homeopathy as an additional, complementary approach alongside this specialist-guided care, often hoping to support general wellbeing. Homeopathy should not be used as a replacement for a formal ADHD assessment or prescribed treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Maintain consistent daily routines for sleep, meals, and activities
- Encourage regular physical activity, which can help with focus and restlessness
- Break tasks into smaller, manageable steps with clear instructions
- Limit excessive screen time, especially close to bedtime
- Work with teachers or school counsellors to put consistent support structures in place
- Ensure adequate, regular sleep, as poor sleep can worsen attention and behaviour
- Offer consistent, calm positive reinforcement rather than harsh discipline

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

ADHD itself is not usually a medical emergency, but you should seek prompt medical or specialist advice if:

- A child's or adult's symptoms are significantly affecting school, work, or relationships
- There are signs of self-harm, severe mood changes, or thoughts of suicide
- Behavioural or emotional difficulties suddenly worsen significantly
- There are concerns about side effects from prescribed ADHD medication, such as severe mood changes, chest pain, or fainting

If you notice any of the above, especially thoughts of self-harm or sudden severe symptoms, contact your doctor promptly or seek emergency care without delay.
