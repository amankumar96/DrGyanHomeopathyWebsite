## What is an Allergy?

An allergy occurs when the immune system reacts to a substance, known as an allergen, that is normally harmless to most people. Common allergens include pollen, dust mites, pet dander, certain foods, insect stings, and some medicines. Reactions can range from mild, such as sneezing or itching, to severe and potentially life-threatening, such as anaphylaxis. Allergic conditions are very common and can affect the skin, nose, eyes, lungs, or digestive system, sometimes all at once, and tend to run in families.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of allergies include:

- Sneezing, a runny or blocked nose, and itchy, watery eyes
- Skin reactions, such as hives, redness, or itching
- Coughing, wheezing, or shortness of breath
- Swelling of the lips, face, eyes, or tongue
- Digestive symptoms, such as nausea, vomiting, or stomach pain, particularly with food allergies
- Feeling generally unwell or fatigued during a reaction

Severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) can cause sudden swelling of the throat, severe difficulty breathing, a rapid drop in blood pressure, and collapse, and require emergency treatment.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Allergies occur when the immune system mistakenly identifies a normally harmless substance as a threat and releases chemicals, such as histamine, that cause allergic symptoms. Common triggers include:

- Pollen from trees, grass, or weeds (hay fever)
- House dust mites
- Animal dander from pets such as cats and dogs
- Certain foods, including peanuts, tree nuts, milk, eggs, and shellfish
- Insect stings, such as from bees or wasps
- Certain medicines, including some antibiotics
- Latex and certain chemicals or cosmetic ingredients

Risk factors for developing allergies include a personal or family history of allergic conditions such as asthma, eczema, or hay fever, a tendency sometimes referred to as being "atopic."

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for allergies depends on the type and severity and may include avoiding known triggers, antihistamines, steroid nasal sprays or creams, and, for severe or life-threatening allergies, an adrenaline auto-injector prescribed and carried at all times. Specialist allergy clinics may offer tests and, in some cases, desensitisation (immunotherapy) treatment. These approaches are supported by clinical evidence and form the standard approach to care.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this medical care, often seeking general support for well-being alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should never replace an adrenaline auto-injector or other emergency allergy treatment. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Identify and avoid your known allergy triggers where possible
- Keep living spaces clean and well-ventilated to reduce dust mites and pet dander
- Check food labels carefully if you have a food allergy, and inform restaurants and caterers of your allergy
- Keep prescribed medicines, such as antihistamines or an adrenaline auto-injector, accessible at all times if advised by your doctor
- Consider wearing a medical alert bracelet if you have a severe allergy
- Keep a symptom diary to help identify patterns and possible triggers
- Inform family, friends, and workplaces about your allergy and what to do in an emergency

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most mild allergic symptoms can be managed with avoidance and over-the-counter treatment, but seek emergency medical help immediately (call emergency services) if you or someone else experiences:

- Sudden swelling of the lips, face, tongue, or throat
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or a tight feeling in the chest or throat
- A rapid heartbeat, dizziness, fainting, or collapse
- Widespread hives or flushing combined with any of the above symptoms
- In children, sudden limpness, unresponsiveness, or pale, clammy skin

These are signs of a severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis), which is a medical emergency. If you have been prescribed an adrenaline auto-injector, use it right away and seek emergency care even if symptoms seem to improve afterward.
