## What is Hepatitis B?

Hepatitis B is a viral infection that affects the liver, caused by the hepatitis B virus. It can be spread through contact with infected blood and certain body fluids, including from mother to child during childbirth. In many adults, the infection is acute and clears within a few months, but in some people, particularly those infected as infants or young children, it becomes a chronic, long-term infection that can increase the risk of liver damage over many years if not monitored and managed. Hepatitis B is a common but manageable condition with vaccination, medical monitoring, and, when needed, antiviral treatment.

## Symptoms

Many people with hepatitis B, especially in the early stages, have no symptoms at all. When symptoms do occur, they may include:

- Fatigue and a general feeling of being unwell
- Loss of appetite
- Nausea and vomiting
- Abdominal discomfort, particularly in the upper right area
- Joint pain
- Dark urine and pale stools
- Jaundice, a yellowing of the skin and eyes

Chronic hepatitis B often causes no symptoms for years, even while it may be affecting the liver, which is why regular monitoring matters even when a person feels well.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Hepatitis B is caused by the hepatitis B virus, and it spreads through contact with infected blood and certain body fluids. Recognised routes of spread and risk factors include:

- Unprotected sexual contact with an infected person
- Sharing needles or other injecting equipment
- Mother-to-child transmission during childbirth
- Sharing items that may carry traces of blood, such as razors or toothbrushes
- Exposure to unsterilised medical, dental, or tattooing equipment
- Occupational exposure to blood, for example in healthcare settings, without appropriate precautions

It is not spread through casual contact, sharing food or drinks, hugging, or coughing and sneezing.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of acute hepatitis B usually involves supportive care while the body clears the infection. Chronic hepatitis B is managed through regular blood tests and liver scans to monitor the virus and liver health, with antiviral medication prescribed when needed to keep the virus suppressed and reduce the risk of long-term liver damage. Vaccination is an important preventive measure for those not yet infected, including close contacts of someone with hepatitis B.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary, general well-being approach alongside this medical care. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Attend all scheduled monitoring appointments and blood tests, even when feeling well
- Avoid alcohol, or limit it strictly as advised by your doctor, to protect liver health
- Maintain a balanced diet and healthy weight to support overall liver function
- Take antiviral medication exactly as prescribed, without stopping or skipping doses
- Avoid sharing razors, toothbrushes, or needles to prevent transmission to others
- Inform close contacts and sexual partners so they can be tested and vaccinated if needed
- Discuss family planning and pregnancy with your doctor, as hepatitis B can be managed safely with appropriate precautions

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Hepatitis B is generally managed with ongoing monitoring, but prompt medical review is needed if you notice:

- Yellowing of the skin or eyes (jaundice) that is new or worsening
- Confusion, drowsiness, or difficulty staying alert, which can indicate serious liver dysfunction
- Severe abdominal pain or swelling
- Easy bruising or unusual bleeding
- Significant, persistent nausea or vomiting
- Dark urine with pale stools alongside feeling increasingly unwell

Confusion or marked drowsiness in particular may signal liver failure and requires emergency medical attention without delay.
