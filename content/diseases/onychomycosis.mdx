## What is Onychomycosis?

Onychomycosis is a fungal infection of the nail, most commonly affecting the toenails, though fingernails can also be affected. It develops when fungi, similar to those causing athlete's foot, invade the nail and surrounding tissue, leading to gradual changes in the nail's colour, thickness, and texture. The condition is common, tends to develop slowly, and often requires sustained treatment because nails grow slowly and the infection can be persistent.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of onychomycosis include:

- Thickening of the nail
- Yellow, white, or brownish discolouration
- Brittle, crumbly, or ragged nail edges
- Distorted nail shape
- A slightly foul odour from the affected nail
- Separation of the nail from the nail bed in more advanced cases
- Mild discomfort or pain, particularly when wearing shoes, in more severe cases

## Causes & Risk Factors

Onychomycosis is caused by fungal organisms, most often dermatophytes, though yeasts and moulds can also be responsible. Risk factors include:

- Increasing age, as nail growth slows and circulation can reduce
- Having athlete's foot or other fungal skin infections
- Diabetes or conditions affecting circulation
- A weakened immune system
- Excessive sweating or prolonged dampness, such as from tight, non-breathable footwear
- Minor nail or skin injury, which can allow fungus to enter
- Walking barefoot in shared damp areas such as public showers or swimming pools
- Family history of fungal nail infections

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep feet clean and thoroughly dry, especially between the toes
- Wear breathable footwear and moisture-wicking socks, changing them regularly
- Avoid walking barefoot in communal showers, gyms, or swimming pools
- Do not share nail clippers, files, or footwear with others
- Trim nails straight across and keep them an appropriate length
- Treat any accompanying athlete's foot to reduce reinfection
- Allow shoes to fully dry out between uses, alternating pairs if possible

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most fungal nail infections are not an emergency, but seek medical attention if you notice:

- Spreading redness, warmth, swelling, or pain around the nail or foot, which may indicate a bacterial infection
- Signs of infection in someone with diabetes or poor circulation, as foot infections can progress more seriously in these individuals
- Pus or discharge from around the nail
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside the nail changes
- Nail infection accompanied by non-healing sores or ulcers on the foot

These situations may need prompt medical assessment rather than routine care.
