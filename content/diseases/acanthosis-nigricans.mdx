## What is Acanthosis Nigricans?

Acanthosis nigricans is a skin condition in which patches of skin become darker, thicker, and develop a velvety texture. It most commonly appears in body folds and creases, such as the back and sides of the neck, the armpits, groin, and sometimes the knuckles, elbows, or knees. The changes usually develop gradually and are not itchy or painful. Acanthosis nigricans itself is not a disease but a visible sign on the skin, and it is most important as a marker that often points toward an underlying health condition, such as insulin resistance, which is why it should always be medically evaluated rather than treated only as a cosmetic skin concern.

## Symptoms

Common features of acanthosis nigricans include:

- Darkened skin, ranging from light brown to dark brown or grey-black, depending on skin tone
- Thickened, velvety, or slightly raised texture to the affected skin
- Patches most often on the neck, armpits, groin, and sometimes elbows, knees, or knuckles
- Gradual development over months, usually without pain or itching
- Occasionally, a mild odour or skin tags in the same areas
- Rapidly spreading or widespread patches, or involvement of the lips, palms, or soles, which is less common and warrants prompt medical evaluation

## Causes & Risk Factors

Acanthosis nigricans most often develops because of insulin resistance, a state in which the body's cells do not respond normally to insulin, leading to higher insulin levels in the blood. High insulin levels are thought to stimulate skin cell growth, producing the characteristic dark, thickened patches. Common underlying associations include:

- Insulin resistance, obesity, and type 2 diabetes, which are the most frequent causes
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS) and other hormonal conditions linked to insulin resistance
- Certain genetic syndromes
- Certain medicines, including some hormonal treatments, corticosteroids, and niacin
- Rarely, acanthosis nigricans that develops suddenly and spreads rapidly, especially affecting the lips, palms, or soles, can be associated with an underlying cancer, most often of the digestive system

Because the causes range from very common and manageable (such as insulin resistance) to rare and serious, acanthosis nigricans should always prompt a proper medical work-up to identify and address the underlying cause, rather than being managed as a skin condition alone.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Aim for a balanced diet and regular physical activity to support healthy insulin function and body weight
- Discuss gradual, sustainable weight management with your doctor if you are overweight, as this can improve both the underlying insulin resistance and the skin changes
- Keep affected skin clean and dry to reduce friction and minor irritation in body folds
- Avoid harsh scrubbing or bleaching creams, which can irritate the skin without addressing the underlying cause
- Monitor and manage related conditions such as blood sugar levels, blood pressure, and cholesterol with your doctor's guidance
- Keep track of any new or spreading patches and mention these to your doctor

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Acanthosis nigricans should always be evaluated by a doctor rather than self-managed, because it frequently signals an underlying condition. Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Rapidly spreading or worsening dark, thickened skin patches over weeks rather than months
- Involvement of unusual areas such as the lips, palms, or soles of the feet
- Unexplained weight loss, fatigue, or other new symptoms alongside the skin changes
- Known risk factors such as obesity, a family history of diabetes, or irregular periods, which warrant testing for insulin resistance or diabetes

A doctor can arrange appropriate blood tests and examinations to identify the cause and guide treatment.
