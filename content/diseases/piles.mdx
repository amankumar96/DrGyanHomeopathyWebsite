## What is Piles?

Piles, also called haemorrhoids, are swollen blood vessels that develop in and around the anus and lower rectum. They can form inside the anal canal (internal piles) or under the skin around the anus (external piles). Piles are very common and, in many cases, can be managed effectively with simple self-care measures, though some people need further medical treatment.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of piles include:

- Bright red blood after a bowel movement, seen on toilet paper, in the stool, or in the toilet bowl
- Itching around the anus
- A feeling of not having fully emptied the bowels
- Mucus discharge from the anus
- A visible lump or swelling around the anus
- Pain or discomfort, particularly with external piles or when a pile becomes prolapsed (pushed outside the anus)

## Causes & Risk Factors

Common causes and contributing factors include:

- Chronic constipation and straining during bowel movements
- Spending long periods sitting on the toilet
- A low-fibre diet and insufficient fluid intake
- Pregnancy, due to increased pelvic pressure and hormonal changes
- Increasing age, as supporting tissues can weaken over time
- Frequent heavy lifting
- Being overweight

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for piles usually begins with dietary changes to increase fibre and fluid intake, avoiding straining, and warm baths or over-the-counter pain relief and creams recommended by a pharmacist. If these measures are not enough, doctors may offer non-surgical procedures such as rubber band ligation, sclerotherapy, electrotherapy, or infrared coagulation. Surgery, such as a haemorrhoidectomy, is generally reserved for more persistent or severe cases.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this care, often seeking general comfort support alongside their prescribed treatment. Homeopathy should not be used as a replacement for recommended procedures or medical evaluation of rectal bleeding. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Increase dietary fibre through fruits, vegetables, and whole grains
- Drink plenty of fluids throughout the day
- Avoid straining during bowel movements and avoid spending long periods on the toilet
- Keep the anal area clean and gently dry after bathing
- Try warm baths to help ease discomfort
- Use ice packs or over-the-counter pain relief such as paracetamol if needed, following label guidance, and avoid codeine-based painkillers which can worsen constipation
- Stay physically active and avoid prolonged sitting where possible

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

See your doctor if symptoms of piles do not improve with self-care within about a week, or if you notice any rectal bleeding for the first time, as it should always be checked. Seek urgent or emergency medical attention if you experience:

- Heavy or continuous bleeding from the anus
- Passing large blood clots
- Severe, unrelenting pain around the anus
- Fever, chills, or feeling generally unwell alongside pile symptoms
- Pus or foul-smelling discharge from the anal area

These can indicate a more serious complication or an unrelated condition that needs prompt assessment.
