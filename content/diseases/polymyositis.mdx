## What is Polymyositis?

Polymyositis is a rare, long-term autoimmune condition in which the immune system mistakenly attacks the body's own muscle tissue, leading to inflammation and progressive muscle weakness. It most commonly affects the muscles closest to the trunk, such as those in the hips, thighs, shoulders, and upper arms, making everyday activities like climbing stairs or lifting objects difficult. Polymyositis is managed by specialists, usually rheumatologists or neurologists, and typically requires long-term treatment and monitoring rather than a one-time cure.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of polymyositis include:

- Gradual, progressive weakness in muscles closest to the trunk, such as hips, thighs, shoulders, and upper arms
- Difficulty climbing stairs, rising from a chair, or lifting objects overhead
- Muscle tenderness or aching in some cases, though pain is not always prominent
- Fatigue
- Difficulty swallowing in more advanced cases
- Shortness of breath if the muscles involved in breathing or the lungs are affected
- Occasionally, mild joint pain or low-grade fever

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of polymyositis is not fully understood, but it is classified as an autoimmune condition, where the immune system mistakenly targets muscle tissue, causing inflammation and damage. Recognised associations and risk factors include:

- Being more common in adults, typically appearing after the age of 20, and more frequently in women
- Association with other autoimmune or connective tissue conditions, such as lupus or scleroderma
- Rarely, an association with certain cancers, particularly in a related condition called dermatomyositis
- Possible triggers in genetically susceptible individuals, such as certain infections, though this is not fully proven

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional treatment for polymyositis typically involves corticosteroids and other immunosuppressant medications to reduce the autoimmune attack on muscle tissue, along with physiotherapy to help maintain muscle strength and function. Treatment is usually long-term, with regular specialist follow-up and blood tests to monitor muscle enzymes and disease activity.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach for general well-being alongside this specialist care. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes, and never reduce or stop immunosuppressant medication without your specialist's guidance, as doing so can allow the disease to flare.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Follow a physiotherapy programme as recommended to help maintain muscle strength and mobility
- Pace activities to avoid excessive fatigue while staying as active as safely possible
- Eat a balanced diet, which can support general health during long-term treatment
- Protect bone health, as long-term steroid use can affect bones; discuss this with your doctor
- Attend all scheduled specialist appointments and blood tests
- Seek support for emotional well-being, as living with a chronic autoimmune condition can be challenging

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical review if you notice:

- New or worsening difficulty swallowing, including choking or coughing while eating or drinking
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath at rest
- Sudden, significant worsening of muscle weakness
- Signs of infection, such as fever, especially while on immunosuppressant medication, as these medicines can reduce the body's ability to fight infection
- Chest pain or irregular heartbeat

Difficulty swallowing or breathing in polymyositis needs urgent specialist review, as these can indicate involvement of muscles needed for these vital functions. Never reduce or stop immunosuppressant treatment on your own in response to these or any other symptoms; contact your specialist team promptly instead.
