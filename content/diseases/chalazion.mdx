## What is Chalazion?

A chalazion is a small, usually firm lump that forms within the eyelid when one of the small oil-producing glands (meibomian glands) along the eyelid margin becomes blocked. Unlike a stye, a chalazion is typically painless, though it can cause a feeling of heaviness or mild irritation, and may cause temporary blurring if it presses on the eyeball. Chalazia are common and generally not serious, often improving within a few weeks with simple home care.

## Symptoms

Common features of a chalazion include:

- A round, usually painless lump within the eyelid, more often the upper lid
- Mild swelling or heaviness of the eyelid
- Slight redness or tenderness, especially in the early stages
- Gradual increase in size over several days before it stabilises
- Occasional blurred vision if a larger chalazion presses on the eye
- Rarely, a feeling of grittiness or watering of the eye

## Causes & Risk Factors

A chalazion develops when a meibomian gland in the eyelid becomes blocked, causing the oily secretion to build up and form a lump. The exact reason a gland blocks is not always clear, but several factors can increase the likelihood, including:

- A previous stye or eyelid inflammation
- Chronic blepharitis (inflammation of the eyelid margins)
- Certain skin conditions, such as rosacea or seborrhoeic dermatitis
- Poor eyelid hygiene or infrequent removal of eye makeup
- A history of previous chalazia

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional care for a chalazion usually begins with warm compresses applied several times a day along with gentle massage of the eyelid, which helps many chalazia resolve naturally over a few weeks. If a chalazion persists, grows large, or affects vision, a doctor may recommend a steroid injection into the lump or a minor surgical procedure to drain it.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside this standard care, rather than as a replacement for it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Apply a clean, warm compress to the affected eyelid for 10-15 minutes, several times a day
- Gently massage the lid toward the eyelashes after warming, if advised by your doctor
- Keep eyelids clean, especially if you have blepharitis or use eye makeup
- Avoid squeezing, popping, or picking at the lump
- Remove eye makeup thoroughly before sleeping
- Wash hands before touching or treating the eye area

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek prompt medical attention if you notice:

- Spreading redness, swelling, or warmth around the eye or eyelid
- Fever or feeling generally unwell alongside the eyelid swelling
- Sudden or worsening changes in vision
- Severe pain, rather than mild discomfort
- A lump that grows rapidly, bleeds, or looks unusual in a way that concerns you
- A lump that has not improved after several weeks of home care

These signs could indicate a more serious eye infection or other condition needing urgent review.
