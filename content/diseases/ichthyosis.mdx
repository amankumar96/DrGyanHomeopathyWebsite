## What is Ichthyosis?

Ichthyosis refers to a group of skin conditions, most of them inherited, in which the skin does not shed dead cells normally, leading to a build-up of dry, thickened, scaly patches over large areas of the body. The name comes from the Greek word for fish, describing the fish-scale-like appearance of the skin in many cases. The most common form, ichthyosis vulgaris, is usually mild, while some rarer inherited forms can be more extensive and present from birth. Ichthyosis is a long-term condition, and while it cannot currently be cured, consistent skin care usually helps keep the skin more comfortable.

## Symptoms

Common features of ichthyosis include:

- Dry, rough, scaly skin that may look like small fish scales or tiles
- Skin that feels tight, especially after bathing
- Thickened skin, particularly over the shins, scalp, and the backs of the hands and feet
- Fine, white or grey scaling in milder forms, or thicker, darker scaling in more severe types
- Itching, which can vary from mild to persistent
- Worsening of dryness and scaling in cold, dry weather and improvement in warm, humid conditions

The severity and exact appearance vary a great deal depending on the specific type of ichthyosis.

## Causes & Risk Factors

Most forms of ichthyosis are caused by inherited gene changes that affect how the skin forms its outer protective layer and sheds dead cells. Because of this, a family history of similar skin problems is a key risk factor, and the condition is often noticed in infancy or early childhood.

Less commonly, ichthyosis-like scaling can develop later in life as a result of other health conditions, certain medicines, or, rarely, in association with other illnesses, rather than being inherited. Risk factors and triggers for flares in known cases include:

- A family history of ichthyosis
- Cold, dry weather, which tends to worsen dryness and scaling
- Frequent bathing with harsh soaps that strip natural skin oils
- General dehydration

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Moisturise generously and frequently, ideally straight after bathing while skin is still slightly damp
- Use lukewarm rather than hot water, and limit bathing time to avoid further drying the skin
- Choose mild, fragrance-free soaps and cleansers
- Gently exfoliate thickened scale as advised by your dermatologist, avoiding harsh scrubbing
- Use a humidifier indoors during dry or cold weather
- Stay well hydrated and avoid known irritants to your skin
- Dress in breathable fabrics and avoid overheating, which can worsen itching

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Contact your doctor promptly if you notice:

- Signs of skin infection, such as increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever
- Deep cracks in the skin that are bleeding or very painful
- Sudden, widespread worsening of scaling or new scaling appearing for the first time in adulthood
- Red, inflamed skin covering a very large area of the body, or associated chills and feeling unwell, which can occasionally indicate a more serious skin condition needing urgent assessment
- Any difficulty with eye closure, hearing, or movement related to tight or thickened skin in a newborn, which needs immediate medical attention

These situations should not be managed with self-care alone, so seek medical advice without delay.
