## What is Crohn's Disease?

Crohn's disease is a long-term condition that causes inflammation of the digestive tract. It is one of the main types of inflammatory bowel disease and can affect any part of the gut, from the mouth to the anus, though it most commonly involves the small intestine and the beginning of the large intestine. The inflammation can occur in patches, with areas of healthy tissue between affected sections. Crohn's disease typically follows a pattern of flare-ups, when symptoms are active, and periods of remission, when symptoms settle, and it requires ongoing specialist management to monitor disease activity and prevent complications.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of Crohn's disease include:

- Persistent diarrhoea
- Abdominal pain and cramping, often in the lower right side
- Fatigue
- Unintended weight loss
- Blood or mucus in the stool
- Loss of appetite
- Mouth ulcers
- A feeling of needing to pass stool urgently, or incomplete emptying
- In some cases, symptoms outside the gut, such as joint pain, skin problems, or eye inflammation

## Causes & Risk Factors

The exact cause of Crohn's disease is not fully understood, but it is thought to result from a combination of factors, including:

- An abnormal immune system response that causes the body to attack its own digestive tract tissue
- Genetic factors, as the condition can run in families
- Environmental factors, including diet and exposure to certain bacteria or viruses, though no single cause has been identified
- Smoking, which is a known risk factor that can also worsen the course of the disease
- An imbalance in the gut's normal bacteria (the microbiome)

Crohn's disease can develop at any age but is most often diagnosed in the teenage years and twenties.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of Crohn's disease typically involves medications to reduce inflammation and suppress an overactive immune response, nutritional support, and monitoring for complications. Some people require surgery at some point to manage strictures, fistulas, or sections of severely damaged bowel. Regular follow-up with a gastroenterologist is important to monitor disease activity and adjust treatment as needed.

Some patients choose to explore homeopathy only as a supportive, complementary approach alongside their prescribed treatment, in the hope of general well-being support, and never as a substitute for it. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making any changes, including starting any complementary therapy, however gentle it may seem.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep a food and symptom diary to help identify personal triggers, with guidance from your care team
- Eat small, frequent meals during flares if large meals are poorly tolerated
- Stay well hydrated, particularly during periods of diarrhoea
- Avoid smoking, as it is linked to more frequent flares and complications in Crohn's disease
- Manage stress through techniques such as relaxation exercises, gentle activity, or counselling, as stress can sometimes affect symptom flares
- Attend regular check-ups and nutritional reviews, as Crohn's disease can affect absorption of key nutrients
- Discuss any travel, vaccinations, or new medications with your gastroenterologist, as immune-suppressing treatments can affect these

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek urgent medical attention if you notice:

- Severe, persistent abdominal pain, especially if the abdomen becomes swollen, hard, or very tender
- Significant rectal bleeding or passing large amounts of blood
- Persistent vomiting, or an inability to keep fluids down
- Signs of a bowel obstruction, such as severe cramping pain, bloating, and an inability to pass stool or gas
- High fever with chills, which may suggest a serious infection or abscess
- Sudden, severe worsening of symptoms, or signs of shock such as rapid heartbeat, dizziness, or fainting, which could suggest a bowel perforation

These can be signs of serious complications of Crohn's disease, such as bowel obstruction, significant bleeding, or perforation, and require immediate emergency medical care.
