## What is Sleeplessness (Insomnia)?

Insomnia is a common sleep disorder in which a person has ongoing difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or both, despite having adequate opportunity to sleep. It can be short-term (acute), often linked to a specific stress or life event and lasting days to a few weeks, or long-term (chronic), lasting a month or longer. Insomnia can affect how rested a person feels during the day and may impact mood, concentration, and overall wellbeing.

## Symptoms

Common symptoms of insomnia include:

- Difficulty falling asleep at the start of the night
- Waking up frequently during the night
- Waking up too early and being unable to return to sleep
- Feeling unrefreshed or tired despite spending enough time in bed
- Daytime fatigue, low energy, or sleepiness
- Irritability, low mood, or difficulty concentrating
- Worry or frustration specifically about sleep itself

## Causes & Risk Factors

Insomnia can arise from a wide range of causes, which can act alone or together. Common contributors include:

- Stress, anxiety, or depression
- Environmental factors such as noise, light, an uncomfortable bed, or an unsuitable room temperature
- Irregular sleep schedules, shift work, or travel across time zones (jet lag)
- Consumption of caffeine, nicotine, or alcohol, particularly close to bedtime
- Certain medications and their side effects
- Underlying medical conditions, such as thyroid disorders, chronic pain, or sleep apnoea
- Significant life changes or traumatic events

Risk factors for insomnia include high stress levels, older age, being female, an inconsistent daily routine, and a generally inactive lifestyle.

## Homeopathy and Conventional Treatment

Conventional management of insomnia typically begins with improving sleep habits (sleep hygiene) and addressing any underlying cause, such as stress, a medical condition, or medication side effects. Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia is often recommended as an effective longer-term approach, and short-term sleeping medication may occasionally be prescribed for brief periods under medical supervision.

Some patients choose to use homeopathy as a complementary approach alongside these treatments, often seeking general support alongside their prescribed care. Homeopathy should not be seen as a replacement for medically recommended insomnia treatment, particularly where an underlying condition has been identified. Continue your current treatment and consult your doctor before making changes.

## Lifestyle & Self-care Tips

- Keep a consistent sleep and wake schedule, including on weekends
- Create a relaxing bedtime routine to help wind down before sleep
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet, and reserve the bed mainly for sleep
- Limit caffeine, nicotine, and alcohol, particularly in the hours before bedtime
- Reduce screen time and bright light exposure in the evening
- Avoid large or heavy meals close to bedtime
- Get regular physical activity during the day, though not too close to bedtime
- If you cannot sleep after a while, consider getting up and doing something calming before trying again

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Most insomnia can be addressed with good sleep habits and time, but it is important to consult a doctor if you experience:

- Sleep difficulty persisting for several weeks despite trying self-care measures
- Insomnia significantly affecting your daily functioning, safety, mood, or relationships
- Signs of depression or anxiety alongside poor sleep, such as persistent low mood, hopelessness, or excessive worry
- Loud snoring, gasping, or breathing pauses during sleep, which may suggest sleep apnoea
- Thoughts of self-harm or feeling unable to cope

If you ever experience thoughts of harming yourself, seek help immediately from a doctor, local emergency services, or a mental health helpline.
